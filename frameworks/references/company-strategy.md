# Company and portfolio strategy

Read only the selected card. Mechanisms summarize the cited public sources. Application procedures, examples, and fit judgments are this library's own synthesis. All examples are synthetic.

## Before selecting a card

1. Price the current course alongside the proposed move to expose its opportunity cost. [Green Book shortlist](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-green-book--2/the-green-book-2026)
2. Identify the party that must pay or participate to check whether that course is viable. [Strategyzer model canvas](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas)
3. Test which rights or distribution advantage the firm can actually carry into the new market.

<a id="zook-adjacencies"></a>

## zook-adjacencies: Zook adjacency growth

Supports: strategy-choices, long-range-plan, portfolio-priorities.

Context: work.domain=strategy, org.scope, company.product_maturity, role.authority.

Decision trigger: Growth options claim to reuse the core, but the actual transferred advantage and new assumptions are unclear.

Inputs and evidence: Core customer economics, repeatable activities and advantages, new buyer need, overlapping capabilities, capacity and cannibalization.

Procedure: Define the actual core. Generate geographic, channel, product/service, customer, value-chain and capability-based new-business alternatives. Identify what each reuses and changes. Test customer economics and the weakest consequential assumption. Compare reinforcement of the core, incremental economics, timing and shared-resource burden. Stage a repeatable move instead of assuming proximity proves success.

Output: An adjacency map, selected sequence, transferred advantage, rejected alternative and expansion/stop conditions. See [worked growth choices](../../skills/strategy/references/growth-and-portfolio-choices.md).

Illustrative decision: A synthetic reporting business compares an adjacent customer segment with a new service for current buyers. Both fit the cash limit but need sixteen weeks against twelve available. Choose one under explicit economics and preserve the threshold where the other wins.

Failure conditions and alternatives: A weak core may need renewal rather than incremental expansion. Counting changed dimensions is not a success probability. Ansoff classifies product/market newness. Zook examines overlap and repeatability. Playing to Win specifies the chosen customer and advantage system.

Source: [Alistair Corbett and Chris Zook, Hard-core growth](https://www.bain.com/insights/hard-core-growth-bain-research-shows-how-smart-companies-profit-from-their-core-business-beyond).

Trace the buyer as well as the product. [Buffer's product retrospective](https://buffer.com/resources/vp-product/) describes revenue growing in an adjacent product while its strongest buyers needed unfamiliar enterprise capabilities. Price those capabilities before approving expansion.

<a id="build-buy-partner"></a>

## build-buy-partner: Capability sourcing

Supports: strategy-choices, business-model, financial-resource.

Context: work.domain=strategy, org.scope, role.authority, task.consequence.

Decision trigger: A selected strategy requires a capability the business does not possess at the required quality, scale or time.

Inputs and evidence: Exact gap, internal resources, minimum control rights, latest useful date, scarce capability availability and transferability, route economics and implementation capacity.

Procedure: Compare internal build, commercial purchase/license, collaboration and acquisition as distinct routes. Reject options that miss mandatory timing, rights, quality or capacity. Compare feasible options on common-horizon economics and reversible learning. Test whether ownership preserves the desired capability and whether a contract supplies sufficient control. Consider staged combinations with explicit transition rights and triggers.

Output: Feasible set, chosen route, hardest objection, decision thresholds and acceptance-ready implementation record. See [capability sourcing](../../skills/strategy/references/capability-sourcing.md).

Illustrative decision: A synthetic offer needs a rare engine by month four. Build takes until month nine and acquisition until month six. A partner route can pass time and capacity limits only if required data and release rights are validated. Scarcity alone does not justify acquisition.

Failure conditions and alternatives: Signing or closing is not usable value. Ownership does not ensure retention of tacit know-how. Weighted scores cannot waive a required right. If no route passes, change the promise or stop. Resource pathways explain sourcing, not where to compete.

Source: [Laurence Capron and Will Mitchell, Resource Pathways Framework, publisher synopsis](https://hbsp.harvard.edu/product/9497BC-PDF-ENG), [INSEAD interview with Laurence Capron](https://knowledge.insead.edu/strategy/corporate-growing-pains-build-borrow-or-buy).

[Microsoft's LinkedIn agreement](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2016/06/13/microsoft-to-acquire-linkedin/) preserved the acquired brand and reporting arrangement. Specify which right requires ownership. If a contract delivers the critical right, compare its full operating cost with the acquisition.

<a id="kernel"></a>

## kernel: Rumelt strategy kernel

Supports: strategy-choices, strategy-review.

Context: work.domain=strategy, org.scope, role.authority.

Decision trigger: A plan lists ambitions and projects but nobody agrees what obstacle they solve.

Inputs and evidence: Performance by segment, customer losses, competing diagnoses, constraints, and actual resource commitments.

Procedure: Name the main obstacle and explain the causal diagnosis. Choose a guiding policy that concentrates effort on that obstacle. Specify mutually reinforcing actions, resources, and exclusions. Check each action against the diagnosis and identify evidence that would overturn it.

Output: A short diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent action set, excluded moves, and review trigger.

Illustrative decision: A synthetic scheduling tool loses clinics at onboarding. Diagnose integration delays rather than low awareness, focus on a narrow set of systems, and move one acquisition project into onboarding delivery.

Failure conditions and alternatives: A diagnosis can be wrong even when actions are coherent. Use customer research or process evidence before settling it. Playing to Win adds explicit market and advantage choices.

Source: [Richard Rumelt, The perils of bad strategy](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/the-perils-of-bad-strategy).

[Microsoft's phone restructuring](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2015/07/08/microsoft-announces-restructuring-of-phone-hardware-business) replaced standalone growth with an ecosystem objective. If the diagnosis changes, identify one action that stops and the receiving business that can verify the new benefit.

<a id="playing-to-win"></a>

## playing-to-win: Playing to Win choice cascade

Supports: strategy-choices, business-model.

Context: work.domain=strategy, org.scope, company.product_maturity, role.authority.

Decision trigger: Leaders need a coherent choice of customers, advantage, capabilities, and management support.

Inputs and evidence: Customer alternatives, segment economics, capabilities, rival responses, and resource constraints.

Procedure: Answer five linked questions: winning aspiration, where to play, how to win there, capabilities required, and management systems required. Develop more than one credible cascade. Work backward to the conditions each requires, test the weakest conditions, and resolve inconsistencies between choices.

Output: One chosen cascade, a rejected alternative, and a short set of assumptions to test.

Illustrative decision: A synthetic reporting service chooses regional distributors and rapid deployment rather than all enterprises. That choice requires repeatable connectors and delivery-time reviews, so bespoke multinational work is declined.

Failure conditions and alternatives: A cascade is a hypothesis, not evidence of advantage. Five Forces tests industry structure. The kernel helps when the fundamental problem is still unclear.

Source: [A.G. Lafley and Roger Martin, Strategy](https://rogerlmartin.com/thought-pillars/strategy).

Use [Buffer's product retrospective](https://buffer.com/resources/vp-product/) to test whether winning with today's buyer requires the same service and sales capabilities as winning with the new one. Reprice the bet when the buyer changes.

<a id="five-forces"></a>

## five-forces: Porter Five Forces

Supports: market-competitive, strategy-choices.

Context: work.domain=product_and_market, org.scope, company.go_to_market_motion.

Decision trigger: An attractive revenue market may leave little profit for a new entrant or incumbent.

Inputs and evidence: Defined industry and substitutes, buyer and supplier concentration, switching costs, entry barriers, and rivalry economics.

Procedure: Define the industry boundary and customer need. Examine rivalry, supplier power, buyer power, substitutes, and entry threats. For each, explain how value is captured or competed away using evidence. Identify a position or change that weakens a pressure and test likely responses.

Output: An industry profit-structure argument and positioning implications, with unresolved evidence.

Illustrative decision: A synthetic business sells standardized alerts through one distributor. Concentrated distribution and easy substitution undermine the apparent market size, so the team tests direct access and a differentiated workflow.

Failure conditions and alternatives: An industry average does not decide a firm's prospects. Add value-chain or power analysis. Do not call every new technology a new entrant or confuse a substitute with a direct rival.

Source: [Michael Porter, The Five Competitive Forces That Shape Strategy](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=34522).

Follow money and alternatives through one transaction. In [Netflix's advertising partnership](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/netflix-partners-with-microsoft), the cheaper tier added advertisers and an ad technology partner. Ask who can divert spend, who owns access, and which subscriber would otherwise pay full price. Industry pressure alone cannot estimate profit after the switch.

<a id="value-chain"></a>

## value-chain: Porter value chain

Supports: strategy-choices, business-model, organization-design.

Context: work.domain=strategy, org.scope, company.product_maturity.

Decision trigger: The team claims differentiation but cannot show which activities produce it.

Inputs and evidence: Activity costs, customer willingness to pay, supplier and channel interfaces, handoffs, and rival activity choices.

Procedure: Map the activities that deliver the offer, including supporting activities. Identify where an activity raises willingness to pay or lowers cost. Examine links between activities and trade-offs with alternatives. Change the activity system as a whole rather than optimizing each cost line independently.

Output: An activity map with advantage mechanisms, trade-offs, and changes required.

Illustrative decision: A synthetic service promises a two-day setup. Standardized intake, narrow integrations, and specialist support reinforce that promise. Adding every custom integration would undermine the system.

Failure conditions and alternatives: Operational improvement alone does not establish a distinct strategy. Use value-stream mapping for delay and waste, and Five Forces for industry-level pressures.

Source: [Michael Porter, Harvard Institute for Strategy and Competitiveness, The Value Chain](https://www.isc.hbs.edu/strategy/business-strategy/Pages/the-value-chain.aspx).

[Spotify's infrastructure partner](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/gcp/spotify-chooses-google-cloud-platform-to-power-data-infrastructure) took over infrastructure work. Mark the activities that move, the customer promise that stays with the buyer and who fixes an outage across the boundary.

<a id="seven-powers"></a>

## seven-powers: Helmer 7 Powers

Supports: strategy-choices, market-competitive, business-model.

Context: work.domain=strategy, company.product_maturity, company.go_to_market_motion.

Decision trigger: A claimed moat needs a mechanism that can sustain superior economics against imitation.

Inputs and evidence: Cost and price differences, relative scale, switching behavior, rival incentives, resource rights, and copying time.

Procedure: Test plausible sources: scale economies, network economies, counter-positioning, switching costs, branding, cornered resource, and process power. For each, identify the economic benefit and the barrier preventing rivals from copying or arbitraging it away. Separate present power from a possible route to it and test timing and rival response.

Output: A defended power thesis or an explicit finding of no demonstrated power, with evidence and milestones.

Illustrative decision: A synthetic workflow product has many integrations. Those count as a power candidate only if replacing it imposes meaningful customer cost and rivals cannot neutralize that cost. Integration count alone is insufficient.

Failure conditions and alternatives: Good execution and a large market are insufficient evidence. This lens does not allocate a multi-business corporate portfolio or prove customers want the product. Pair with customer research and economics.

Source: [Hamilton Helmer, original interview on business strategy, author synopsis](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/business-strategy-with-hamilton-helmer).

[Buffer's product retrospective](https://buffer.com/resources/vp-product/) describes partner signups that converted less often. Reach alone cannot establish a durable distribution advantage. Seek retained paid cohorts and the cost for a rival to secure comparable access.

<a id="strategy-diamond"></a>

## strategy-diamond: Hambrick and Fredrickson strategy diamond

Supports: strategy-choices, long-range-plan.

Context: work.domain=strategy, org.scope, role.authority.

Decision trigger: A strategy presentation omits the route, sequence, or economic rationale.

Inputs and evidence: Market choices, candidate vehicles, sources of differentiation, sequencing dependencies, investment needs, and returns.

Procedure: State arenas, vehicles, differentiators, staging, and economic logic. Show how the five reinforce one another. Compare a coherent alternative and identify any contradiction, such as premium positioning paired with channels that undermine the premium. Test the economic logic before committing resources.

Output: A complete five-element strategy and a list of contradictions resolved or still open.

Illustrative decision: A synthetic training company chooses one industry, partner distribution, job-specific content, staged geographic expansion, and recurring contracts. The sequence changes if partners cannot deliver the needed customer access.

Failure conditions and alternatives: Completeness is not correctness. Use Five Forces and customer evidence to test the choices. Playing to Win is an alternative communication structure, not another required diagram.

Source: [Donald Hambrick and James Fredrickson, Are you sure you have a strategy?](https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/ame.2001.5897655).

[Adobe and Figma's termination statement](https://news.adobe.com/news/news-details/2023/adobe-and-figma-mutually-agree-to-terminate-merger-agreement) shows that a plausible destination and acquisition route can still fail at approval. Compare the last viable partner date with the earliest credible close before assigning the acquisition team.

<a id="ansoff"></a>

## ansoff: Ansoff product-market growth alternatives

Supports: commercial-growth, portfolio-priorities, strategy-choices.

Context: work.domain=strategy, company.product_maturity, org.scope.

Decision trigger: Growth options mix selling more of today's offer with new businesses.

Inputs and evidence: Current product and customer mission definitions, proposed offers, reachable markets, capabilities, and incremental economics.

Procedure: Separate existing and new products from existing and new markets or missions. Generate penetration, market-development, product-development, and diversification options. Specify what changes in each option, screen for fit and required capabilities, and compare economics and dependencies. Classification generates alternatives, not automatic rankings.

Output: A growth-options map and a shortlist for testing and investment analysis.

Illustrative decision: A synthetic maintenance supplier distinguishes cross-selling its current sensor, adapting it for a new customer mission, adding predictive software, and entering an unrelated service. Each requires a different evidence plan.

Failure conditions and alternatives: Newness depends on the boundary used. Geography alone may hide an unchanged mission. Do not infer a universal risk multiplier from the quadrants. Use NPV and staged tests to choose.

Source: [H. Igor Ansoff, Strategies for Diversification, original article scan](https://ia803104.us.archive.org/32/items/strategiesfordiversificationansoff1957hbr/Strategies%20for%20Diversification-Ansoff1957-HBR.pdf).

Classify the new buyer separately from the new feature. [Buffer's product retrospective](https://buffer.com/resources/vp-product/) crossed into customer service, where enterprise requirements changed delivery costs. Put those costs in the growth proposal.

<a id="growth-share"></a>

## growth-share: BCG growth-share matrix

Supports: portfolio-priorities, financial-resource.

Context: work.domain=resource_allocation, org.scope=initiative_or_portfolio, company.product_maturity=multi_product.

Decision trigger: A multi-business portfolio needs a first discussion of cash generation and reinvestment.

Inputs and evidence: Consistent market definitions, market growth, relative share, cash generation, required investment, and interdependencies.

Procedure: Plot businesses on market growth and relative share. Distinguish stars, cash cows, question marks, and low-growth low-share businesses. Form investment or exit hypotheses, then challenge the link between share and cash returns in this market. Check standalone economics and shared capabilities before reallocating.

Output: A portfolio map, cash-flow hypotheses, and specific investment questions.

Illustrative decision: A synthetic software portfolio has a mature dominant tool and a small fast-growing tool. The map raises a funding question. It does not justify funding the second tool until retention and economics support it.

Failure conditions and alternatives: Share is an imperfect proxy for advantage. Network effects, niches, and shared assets can change the answer. Do not mechanically divest a low-share business or import fixed growth cutoffs.

Source: [BCG, What Is the Growth Share Matrix?](https://www.bcg.com/about/overview/our-history/growth-share-matrix).

[Microsoft's phone restructuring](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2015/07/08/microsoft-announces-restructuring-of-phone-hardware-business) changed the purpose of its phone business. Before using market share to allocate cash, ask whether the unit now earns its keep elsewhere. Require the receiving unit to verify that benefit.

<a id="three-horizons"></a>

## three-horizons: Three horizons of growth

Supports: long-range-plan, portfolio-priorities, annual-plan.

Context: work.domain=resource_allocation, company.product_maturity, org.scope.

Decision trigger: Current delivery consumes all attention and future businesses have no protected learning path.

Inputs and evidence: Current profit engines, emerging businesses, speculative options, resource envelopes, and maturation evidence.

Procedure: Place current businesses in horizon one, emerging businesses in horizon two, and future options in horizon three. Manage all three now with different evidence and review criteria. Specify how an option can mature into an emerging business and later a core business. Reconcile total funding and management capacity.

Output: A concurrent portfolio of current performance, emerging scale, and future learning commitments.

Illustrative decision: A synthetic company supports its core product, tests an emerging channel, and funds a bounded discovery project. The discovery project has a learning gate rather than the core product's quarterly revenue quota.

Failure conditions and alternatives: Horizons are not permission to postpone thinking or a synonym for core, adjacent, and transformational. Do not impose a standard allocation percentage. Use Ansoff for product-market distance and NPV for investments.

Source: [McKinsey, Enduring Ideas: The three horizons of growth](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/enduring-ideas-the-three-horizons-of-growth).

Use the supplier migration described by [Spotify's infrastructure partner](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/gcp/spotify-chooses-google-cloud-platform-to-power-data-infrastructure) to test shared capacity across horizons. Name who must run today's service during migration and which future commitment waits if migration slips.

<a id="swot"></a>

## swot: SWOT evidence synthesis

Supports: research-analysis, strategy-choices, strategy-review.

Context: work.domain=strategy, org.scope, company.product_maturity, role.authority.

Decision trigger: The team has research on capabilities and external conditions but needs to connect it to strategic options.

Inputs and evidence: A defined objective and scope, relative capability evidence, customer and competitor findings, external changes, and sources for each claimed factor.

Procedure: Separate internal strengths and weaknesses from external opportunities and threats. Challenge each factor with evidence and a relevant comparison. Identify which capabilities could exploit an opportunity or protect against a threat, and which weaknesses make an attractive option infeasible. Turn those links into alternative action hypotheses, then test and compare them before choosing.

Output: An evidence-backed synthesis with linked strategic options, unresolved assumptions, and the next decision or investigation.

Illustrative decision: A synthetic service has reliable connectors but little implementation capacity. Demand for faster onboarding is an external opportunity. Limited delivery capacity constrains the company's ability to exploit it. Compare a standardized onboarding offer with a delivery partnership, then test the required capacity and economics.

Failure conditions and alternatives: A four-box list is not a strategy or proof that factors matter. Generic labels such as strong brand need a relative measure. Use PESTEL for macro-driver discovery, Five Forces for industry profit pressures, and the kernel or Playing to Win to form coherent choices. CIPD is cited for its published practice, not as SWOT's inventor.

Source: [CIPD, SWOT analysis factsheet](https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/factsheets/swot-analysis-factsheet).

[Adobe and Figma's termination statement](https://news.adobe.com/news/news-details/2023/adobe-and-figma-mutually-agree-to-terminate-merger-agreement) records a specific approval obstacle. Convert an external threat into a dated contingency with a fallback owner. Drop entries that cannot change any action.

<a id="pestel"></a>

## pestel: PESTEL macro-environment analysis

Supports: research-analysis, market-competitive, scenario-response, long-range-plan.

Context: work.domain=strategy, org.scope, governance.regime, task.consequence.

Decision trigger: A business decision may be affected by changes outside the immediate customer and competitor market.

Inputs and evidence: The decision, time horizon, affected geographies, dated sources on political, economic, social, technological, environmental, and legal drivers, and exposure through the business model.

Procedure: Scan the six categories, also commonly ordered as PESTLE. Keep drivers with a plausible causal route to the defined decision and separate observed changes from proposals and speculation. Assess impact, timing, uncertainty, and interaction between drivers. Translate consequential drivers into options, required investigations, and signals to monitor rather than an undifferentiated trend list.

Output: A prioritized macro-driver assessment with business implications, evidence gaps, response options, and monitoring triggers.

Illustrative decision: A synthetic software company considers a data-intensive service. A hypothetical proposed procurement standard could raise buyer requirements. The analysis distinguishes a proposal from an adopted requirement, identifies affected sales, and assigns verification before changing the product plan.

Failure conditions and alternatives: A category heading does not establish materiality or predict an outcome. Overlapping drivers can be counted twice and broad global trends may not reach the served business. Five Forces examines industry profit structure. Scenario planning tests interacting uncertainties. CIPD provides a published practitioner treatment, not an origin claim.

Source: [CIPD, PESTLE analysis factsheet](https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/factsheets/pestle-analysis-factsheet).

[Adobe and Figma's termination statement](https://news.adobe.com/news/news-details/2023/adobe-and-figma-mutually-agree-to-terminate-merger-agreement) ended a transaction despite both companies supporting it. Identify the approving body, decision date and evidence that would reopen alternatives. A general regulatory risk score cannot supply them.

<a id="blue-ocean-four-actions"></a>

## blue-ocean-four-actions: Blue Ocean four actions and value curve

Supports: strategy-choices, customer-problem, product-direction, business-model.

Context: work.domain=strategy, company.product_maturity, company.go_to_market_motion, org.scope.

Decision trigger: Competing offers converge on the same features and costs, and the team needs a different buyer-value configuration.

Inputs and evidence: Buyer and noncustomer research, alternatives, actual offering levels on competing factors, activity costs, willingness to pay, and adoption constraints.

Procedure: Build a baseline strategy canvas with competing factors horizontally and buyer-received offering levels vertically. Use the four actions to identify factors to eliminate, reduce, raise, and create. Draw the proposed value curve and trace the operational and cost implications of each change. Test whether the configuration attracts demand and supports viable economics before funding it.

Output: A proposed value curve, four-action choices, associated activity and cost changes, and a demand/economics validation plan.

Illustrative decision: A synthetic reporting service eliminates bespoke presentation meetings, reduces formatting options, raises setup reliability, and creates a self-service preview. The candidate offer proceeds only if target buyers value the changes and delivery costs support the proposed price.

Failure conditions and alternatives: A visually different curve does not prove an uncontested market, profitable demand, or durable advantage. Offering-level judgments need evidence, and elimination can remove something buyers require. Use Jobs to Be Done for switching evidence, contribution economics for viability, and 7 Powers for defensibility.

Source: [W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne, Four Actions Framework](https://www.blueoceanstrategy.com/tools/four-actions-framework) and [Strategy Canvas](https://www.blueoceanstrategy.com/tools/strategy-canvas/).
[Netflix's advertising partnership](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/netflix-partners-with-microsoft) added a partner as well as a cheaper tier. For each benefit added or removed, identify the paying party and displaced purchase. Test contribution after partner costs.
