# Customer, product, and growth

Read only the selected card. Mechanisms summarize the cited public sources. Application procedures, examples, and fit judgments are this library's own synthesis. All examples are synthetic.

## Before selecting a card

1. Observe the job customers already accomplish and the alternative they would give up. [Strategyzer value proposition](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/achieve-product-market-fit-with-our-brand-new-value-proposition-designer-canvas)
2. State a prediction and try the smallest change that could disprove it. [IHI testing](https://www.ihi.org/library/model-for-improvement/testing-changes)
3. Follow the same starting population through purchase and repeat use before calling it growth.

<a id="jobs-to-be-done"></a>

## jobs-to-be-done: Jobs to Be Done

Supports: customer-problem, product-direction, commercial-growth.

Context: work.domain=product_and_market, company.product_maturity, company.go_to_market_motion.

Decision trigger: Demographic segments and feature requests fail to explain actual purchase or switching.

Inputs and evidence: Recent buyer and nonbuyer stories, circumstances before switching, alternatives including doing nothing, and functional, social, and emotional progress.

Procedure: Interview around a specific past decision. Reconstruct the situation, trigger, prior workaround, desired progress, attraction to the new option, anxieties, and habits resisting change. Compare stories for common causal circumstances. Form a job hypothesis and test a changed offer against real behavior.

Output: A circumstance-based job statement, alternatives, switching forces, and a testable offer.

Illustrative decision: A synthetic scheduling buyer wants to stop weekend catch-up after a new location opens. An implementation service may address the job better than more calendar features.

Failure conditions and alternatives: Retrospective stories can rationalize choices and small samples do not establish prevalence. Validate with behavioral evidence. Value Proposition Canvas designs the response. Cohorts assess repeated use.

Source: [Christensen Institute, What is Jobs to Be Done Theory?](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/video/what-is-jobs-to-be-done-theory).

Use [Joel Gascoigne's Buffer account](https://buffer.com/resources/vp-product/) to distinguish the publishing buyer from the service buyer. Ask for the last actual switch, who approved it and what support the new job required.

<a id="business-model-canvas"></a>

## business-model-canvas: Business Model Canvas

Supports: business-model, strategy-choices, financial-resource.

Context: work.domain=business_model, company.product_maturity, company.go_to_market_motion.

Decision trigger: The offer sounds useful but the delivery and payment system is unclear.

Inputs and evidence: Customer and payer evidence, offer, channel costs, relationships, revenues, resources, activities, partners, and cost structure.

Procedure: Describe one business model across customer segments, value propositions, channels, relationships, revenue streams, resources, activities, partners, and costs. Connect the blocks and mark observed facts versus assumptions. Identify the weakest economic or customer assumption and select a test. Compare a distinct model when the links do not work.

Output: A linked business-model description and prioritized evidence gaps.

Illustrative decision: A synthetic advisory product serves users through an employer. Employer procurement, user engagement, and employer payment become separate assumptions. Consumer signup interest does not validate employer willingness to pay.

Failure conditions and alternatives: A completed canvas is not validation or competitive advantage. Use contribution economics for viability, customer interviews for demand, and power analysis for defensibility.

Source: [Strategyzer, Business Model Canvas](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas).

[Google's Stadia closure](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/stadia/message-on-stadia-streaming-strategy/) separated proven streaming technology from weak consumer traction. For each critical box name the other party, its commitment and its right to walk away. Test the weakest commitment first.

<a id="value-proposition-canvas"></a>

## value-proposition-canvas: Value Proposition Canvas

Supports: customer-problem, product-direction, pricing-monetization.

Context: work.domain=product_and_market, company.product_maturity, company.go_to_market_motion.

Decision trigger: The team needs to choose which customer pains and gains the offer will address.

Inputs and evidence: One customer segment, observed jobs, pains, gains, current alternatives, and evidence of willingness to change.

Procedure: Describe customer jobs, pains, and desired gains separately from the offer. Specify products or services, pain relievers, and gain creators. Match important customer evidence to particular offer elements. Remove unsupported or low-priority promises and test the most consequential fit assumption.

Output: A focused offer-to-need map and a short validation plan.

Illustrative decision: A synthetic onboarding tool targets repeated data entry and fear of migration errors. A tested import preview addresses those pains more directly than a dashboard of extra features.

Failure conditions and alternatives: Do not fill the customer side from internal guesses or promise to solve every pain. Jobs to Be Done investigates switching causes. The Business Model Canvas covers the broader business economics.

Source: [Alexander Osterwalder, Strategyzer, Value Proposition Canvas introduction](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/achieve-product-market-fit-with-our-brand-new-value-proposition-designer-canvas).

[Intercom's onboarding designers](https://www.intercom.com/blog/designing-modular-user-onboarding) split reusable onboarding patterns from product-specific work. Test whether a customer reached the promised result after setup. Completion of the common steps alone can hide a product-specific failure.

<a id="cohort-retention"></a>

## cohort-retention: Cohort retention analysis

Supports: performance-information, product-direction, commercial-growth.

Context: work.domain=performance_and_metrics, company.product_maturity, company.go_to_market_motion.

Decision trigger: Aggregate user growth may conceal worsening retention or a changing customer mix.

Inputs and evidence: User-level start and return events, stable identity rules, timestamps, acquisition or segment labels, and complete observation windows.

Procedure: Define the start event, return event, interval, and whether return means exactly at that interval or on or after it. Group comparable entrants into cohorts. Compare retention at the same age and exclude entrants not observed long enough. Separate mix effects from within-cohort changes and investigate differences before choosing an intervention.

Output: A retention table or curve, denominator definitions, and a targeted investigation or experiment.

Illustrative decision: Two synthetic cohorts each start with 100 accounts. At week four, 60 older-cohort accounts return versus 40 newer-cohort accounts. Check channel mix and activation before blaming a recent product release.

Failure conditions and alternatives: Association does not establish the cause of retention. Immature cohorts, account mergers, and shifting event definitions invalidate comparisons. Use controlled tests or qualitative research for the cause.

Source: [Amplitude documentation, How the Retention Analysis chart calculates retention](https://amplitude.com/docs/analytics/charts/retention-analysis/retention-analysis-calculation).

[Rahul Vohra's Superhuman account](https://blog.superhuman.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/) selected active users for a reliance survey. Preserve that denominator. Separately examine all eligible starters, including those who never activated, before inferring retention.

[DoorDash's registration filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000119312520311490/d752207ds1a.htm) grouped consumers by first-order year. Second-year spending rose 38%, 48% and 65% for the 2016, 2017 and 2018 groups. A few large repeat spenders can lift the whole cohort. Calculate the returning customer fraction separately.

<a id="growth-loops"></a>

## growth-loops: Growth loops

Supports: commercial-growth, product-direction, financial-resource.

Context: work.domain=product_and_market, company.go_to_market_motion, company.product_maturity.

Decision trigger: Acquisition spend produces one-off volume and the team needs to understand reinvestment or referral mechanics.

Inputs and evidence: Entry volumes, step conversions, cycle time, retained-user quality, reinvestable output, costs, and saturation evidence.

Procedure: Map the sequence from input to value-producing activity to output that becomes the next input. Quantify each step and cycle time. Locate leakage and capacity or market ceilings. Compare changing one step with increasing paid input and test whether the extra output feeds another cycle.

Output: A causal loop diagram, a simple cycle model, and a chosen test with quality controls.

Illustrative decision: In a synthetic referral loop, 100 retained accounts send 50 invitations, 20 accept, and 10 become retained accounts. That is 0.1 retained account per starting account per cycle, before repeat behavior. It is not self-sustaining growth on those assumptions.

Failure conditions and alternatives: A circle in a slide is not a loop. Separate referrals from retention and measure the returning input. Cohort analysis checks quality. Contribution economics checks whether reinvestment is affordable.

Source: [Reforge, Growth Loops are the New Funnels](https://www.reforge.com/blog/growth-loops).

[Joel Gascoigne's Buffer account](https://buffer.com/resources/vp-product/) describes partner-driven signups converting less often. Carry the originating channel into the next generation. Stop funding a loop if additional recruits produce less retained value than acquisition consumes.

<a id="build-measure-learn"></a>

## build-measure-learn: Build-Measure-Learn

Supports: customer-problem, product-direction, product-delivery.

Context: work.domain=product_and_market, company.product_maturity=pre_pmf or finding_fit, task.consequence.

Decision trigger: An uncertain product or business-model assumption could consume substantial build effort.

Inputs and evidence: A falsifiable hypothesis, target customers, observable behavior, baseline, minimum useful test, and decision criteria.

Procedure: Specify the learning question and the decision it changes before building. Design the smallest credible product or experiment that can answer it. Measure actionable customer behavior. Compare evidence with the stated criterion and decide whether to persevere, change the hypothesis, or stop. Record what the test cannot establish.

Output: A learning result and a next investment decision, not only a shipped prototype.

Illustrative decision: A synthetic team tests a manually delivered report with paying customers before building automation. Repeat purchase can support demand while leaving scalable delivery cost unproven.

Failure conditions and alternatives: A weak proxy or unrepresentative test creates false confidence. Minimum effort cannot remove safety or reliability requirements. Use staged investment for financing and PDSA for improving a known process.

Source: [Eric Ries, The Lean Startup methodology](https://theleanstartup.com/principles?locale=en_GB).
[DoorDash's engineering team](https://careersatdoordash.com/blog/switchback-tests-and-randomized-experimentation-under-network-effects-at-doordash/) alternated treatments by time and region when orders shared couriers. Before testing a marketplace change, draw the shared resource boundary. Randomizing individual orders can contaminate the comparison.
