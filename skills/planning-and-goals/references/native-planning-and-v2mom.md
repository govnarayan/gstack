# Work in the company's planning system

Use the planning language and decision process the company already operates. A second goal tracker can create conflicting priorities, duplicate reviews and apparent commitments that the actual resource owners never accepted. Repair a failed mechanism before replacing the planning system.

## Establish the native contract

Read the current approved plan, the served unit's contribution, resource decisions and latest review. Record which document is authoritative, its version and period, what remains a draft, and who may change it. Ask leaders where an actual priority conflict was resolved. An official framework description does not establish how this particular unit works.

Translate the existing terms into one operating record: direction, ranked choices, obstacles, expected results, initiatives, resources, dependencies, actual decision rights and review evidence. Retain the native names in the deliverable. If another format is requested, create a view over those same records. Do not create a second set of owners, dates or targets.

## Apply V2MOM when it is the native system

Salesforce's public V2MOM guidance connects Vision, Values, Methods, Obstacles and Measures. Its writing guidance ranks Values, Methods and Measures, and describes manager, peer and team feedback. Treat these as the published method, rather than evidence of any current unit's approved priorities or calendar. [Salesforce writing guidance](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/manage_the_sfdc_organizational_alignment_v2mom/msfw_oav2m_writing_a_v2mom), [alignment article](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/how-to-create-alignment-within-your-company/).

| Native element | Operating test | Agent must preserve |
|---|---|---|
| Vision | Does the intended change make the choice clear? | Leader-approved direction versus a proposed synthesis, beneficiary and horizon |
| Values | Which principle prevails when two desirable outcomes conflict? | Actual ranked values and their trade-off, with missing leadership decisions marked unknown |
| Methods | What work is most important, and what loses capacity? | Ranked choices, initiative IDs, accountable owners and accepted resource dependencies |
| Obstacles | What could prevent each method from working? | Evidence, consequence, action or decision required, owner and latest useful escalation |
| Measures | What observation shows progress or completion? | Counted unit, baseline, target basis, period, source, owner and outcome or milestone type |

The official material describes outcome measures and also gives completion-style examples. Label a completed task as a milestone. Keep the business outcome visible separately. Launching a partner program does not establish incremental growth. Use the company's actual review calendar and change rights. The published guidance alone does not establish a particular business unit's weekly or quarterly process.

## Keep translations faithful

An OKR view can express a desired outcome and measurable results. It cannot carry all the native values, method ranking, obstacles and resource commitments by itself. Link the objective to the native direction, each result to the same metric definition, and the initiatives to their native methods. Preserve the obstacle and decision records outside the short OKR view.

When two views disagree, reconcile the underlying record with the actual owner. A locally drafted target cannot amend a parent plan, budget or customer promise. Record the original commitment, proposed revision, reason, approver and effective date. Preserve the prior version.

## A completed synthetic translation

All inputs are invented for this example. An acquired software unit has a parent-sponsored distribution pilot. Its approved constraint is two implementation staff over the next eight weeks. The parent has not yet accepted a sales-capacity commitment.

| Record | Populated draft |
|---|---|
| Direction G-01 | Test whether parent distribution can create retained customer value without degrading the acquired product's service |
| Proposed value trade-off | Protect existing customer service before expanding pilot reach. Leadership must accept this proposed trade-off. It is not an inferred company value. |
| Ranked method I-01 | Deliver a narrow pilot to eligible accounts, conditional on a named parent commercial owner and accepted capacity |
| Later method I-02 | Broader rollout waits for retained contribution and service evidence. The same implementation capacity cannot also support an immediate full rollout. |
| Obstacle D-01 | Parent selling capacity and account eligibility remain unaccepted. The commercial decision owner must resolve them before customer commitments. |
| Milestone M-01 | Demonstrate the agreed customer workflow and tested service handoff by the pilot's accepted date |
| Outcome M-02 | Compare retained incremental contribution in the eligible pilot cohort with the actual alternative. Baseline and target remain unresolved until definitions and evidence are accepted. |
| OKR view | Objective G-01 uses the same outcome M-02. I-01 is an initiative and M-01 a delivery condition. Neither becomes a substitute revenue result. |
| Review decision | Authorize discovery and internal feasibility only. Keep the commercial pilot conditional while D-01 remains open. |

The useful result is a bounded commitment and an explicit unresolved decision. Producing complete-looking targets would make this case worse. Use [goal design](goal-design.md) to establish evidence-backed targets and [operating cadence](../../operating-cadence/SKILL.md) to carry the accepted records into reviews.
