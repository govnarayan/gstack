# Planning methods

This is a procedure for producing and revising an integrated plan. It proposes operating choices rather than asserting how a particular company currently works. Use the organization's existing artifacts where they already carry the decisions.

## 1. Find the failure before selecting a framework

Read the current plan and a recent real execution. Trace a priority from its stated reason to resource allocation, work done, measured result, and next decision. Look for separate failure points:

| Symptom | Test | First repair |
|---|---|---|
| Every function interprets the direction differently | Ask each to explain the customer, constraint, and trade-off in one sentence | Resolve the strategic choice before rewriting goals |
| Teams hit local targets while the company loses | Compare denominators, customer segments, incentives, and shared resources | Change the contribution measures and arbitrate the shared outcome |
| A plan has owners but never gets reviewed | Check whether the reviewer has a mandate, time, and escalation route | Grant the actual operating mandate and place review inside an existing forum |
| A previous goal program was abandoned | Compare excessive upkeep with missing authority, bad measures, and impossible commitments | Pilot the smallest mechanism that addresses the observed cause |
| Launches repeatedly consume support or infrastructure time | Join change demand and operating coverage by person, skill, and week | Choose a smaller portfolio or an explicit coverage alternative |

Avoid importing a process from a company label. Product maturity, interdependence, uncertainty, obligations, and decision rights are stronger inputs than funding series alone.

## 2. Connect the upstream layers

The layers answer different questions. They can share a document and should exchange evidence in both directions.

| Layer | Required choice or evidence | Review trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose and long-term direction | Whom the company serves and what enduring change it seeks | A changed view of the problem or company's purpose |
| Company strategy | Diagnosis, chosen customer/arena, mechanism for winning, explicit exclusions | Evidence undermines a material strategic belief |
| Functional or product strategy | The contribution this function must make, required capabilities, management and incentive changes | A company choice or functional assumption changes |
| Long-range view | Multi-period scenarios, capabilities and irreversible commitments | A major assumption, capital requirement, or lead time changes |
| Annual or rolling resource plan | Outcomes, initiatives, people, spend, coverage, sequence, and constraints | Scheduled refresh or an authorized material amendment |
| Period goals | What should change or be proved within the decision window | Progress review, material new evidence, or cycle close |
| Initiatives and tasks | The current route to the goal, including alternatives | Evidence suggests a better route |

Do not call a growth target a strategy. Write: "We believe [diagnosis]. We will serve [customer/arena] using [mechanism], rather than [alternative]. The moves reinforce one another because [logic]. This fails if [evidence]." If leadership cannot supply the choice, deliver an unresolved-choice brief with two or three feasible directions and a bounded evidence plan. Do not manufacture certainty by filling the OKR grid.

Translate strategy into operating consequences. If the choice is faster implementation for smaller customers, specify product scope, onboarding capacity, sales promises, pricing assumptions, and what stops. A growth strategy paired with incentives for large custom deals may contradict itself even when every functional plan looks reasonable.

A long-range financial scenario differs from a durable strategy narrative. Use detail justified by the choice: compute commitments, facility build-out, multi-year contracts, capital needs, or a board requirement may justify a modeled horizon even for a small company. An uncertain distant year should show assumptions and ranges rather than a false detailed roadmap. Never infer that a small company has no need for long-range thinking.

## 3. Choose a planning form

The strongest case for formal OKRs is a shared outcome that needs autonomous teams to coordinate and learn. The strongest case against them is a target or causal path that is still being discovered, or a service team with no control over priorities. Compare forms explicitly:

| Form | Use when | Minimum operating artifact | Failure to test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outcome OKRs | The result matters, the team has usable levers, and a common review helps coordination | Objective, measures, initiatives, dependencies, and review | Attractive metric with no believable path |
| Narrative plus commitments and tasks | The "why" and initial route are missing from terse goals | Short rationale, observable commitments, adaptable tasks | Task completion presented as strategic success |
| Learning plan | The customer problem or causal mechanism remains uncertain | Question, competing explanations, evidence, deadline, and decision | Counting interviews without resolving a choice |
| Fixed-appetite bet | A bounded build can be shaped, with time fixed and scope negotiable | Problem, time/capacity appetite, minimum scope, no-go boundaries, acceptance | Automatic extension when the time box expires |
| One shared outcome plus health guardrails | A small aligned team needs focus more than multiple goal layers | Outcome, operating health, current bets, review | One number hides quality, economics, or service harm |
| Accepted milestones | Delivery or a dependency is the immediate uncertainty | Acceptance evidence, named acceptor, date and next impact check | "Launched" without accepted functionality |
| Longer commitments with frequent inspection | The outcome takes longer to observe than a quarter | Stable commitment, interim evidence, explicit change route | Quietly extending a failed short commitment |

A proposed number of goals is a workload hypothesis. Add a goal only if it represents a distinct strategic choice with resources and review attention. Remove duplicates, mandatory service work dressed as stretch, and objectives created solely to include a function. If a function is off the current critical path, explain its ongoing mandate and longer-term contribution. Check for hidden approvals and service dependencies before deciding it is peripheral.

## 4. Build the annual or rolling resource plan

Use an annual plan when an integrated envelope, hiring sequence, board approval, or long lead-time commitment is needed. A rolling four-quarter view refreshes the near quarter in detail and keeps later quarters provisional under a retained longer direction. It may coexist with an annual budget. It does not erase long-range planning or authorize spend by itself.

Separate four columns throughout: actual result, approved target, current forecast, and authorized resource envelope. A target says what is wanted, a forecast says what is expected under assumptions, and an authorization says what may be spent. Moving one does not silently move the others.

Run four exchanges, with deadlines fitted to the real decision:

1. Leadership supplies strategic choices, assumptions, exclusions, resource envelope, and fixed obligations before teams draft.
2. Teams propose contributions, initiatives, effort by role and period, dependencies, alternatives, and the implications if an ask is denied.
3. The authorized integrator reconciles whole portfolios. Obtain explicit dependency acceptance, expose conflicts, settle scarce-resource choices, and record dissent.
4. Teams finalize the agreed contributions. The actual approver accepts the plan, and affected teams receive the funded work, declined work, and changes to their mandates.

Teams may start reversible work already within their mandate while planning proceeds. Do not make wording approval a bottleneck. Require approval for resource commitments, shared dependencies, obligations, or changes outside delegated authority. An integration meeting is not permission for a process owner to overrule the real resource owner.

A plan includes the strategic mechanism, outcome goals, initiatives, service obligations, capability investments, resources, sequence, assumptions, stop list, and approval status. Keep candidate ideas open all year. Planning chooses and funds work, it need not be the only season for generating ideas.

## 5. Reconcile capacity by period and skill

Start from existing people and committed availability, not an aspirational headcount chart. For each role and period:

~~~text
Net available time = scheduled time - leave - existing commitments
Change capacity = net available time - service coverage - contingency reserve
Unallocated capacity = change capacity - approved initiative demand
~~~

Use mutually exclusive categories so the same hour is subtracted only once. Show the basis for service coverage and reserve. Do not invent a utilization percentage or a protected foundations floor. A reserve is an explicit decision under uncertainty, and its adequacy must be tested under representative load.

Check both total effort and the date bottleneck. Twelve specialist-days in a quarter do not help a dependency that needs twelve days in a week with only eight available. A person allocated to two initiatives is a conflict until their actual time is split. A new hire contributes after recruiting, onboarding, ramp and required supervision, with those costs reflected in existing staff availability.

Use skill-days or another comparable unit within a role. Do not treat an analyst-day as a substitute for a database engineer-day. Money can purchase capacity only when a viable supplier can deliver the required skill, access, quality, and date.

Illustrative constraint test: a specialist has 60 scheduled days, 6 leave days, 12 committed service days, and 6 reserve days. Change capacity is 36 days. Initiatives requesting 24 and 20 specialist-days need 44, so the combined portfolio is short by 8. "Both high priority" cannot close the gap. Compare postponing one bet, removing at least 8 days of accepted scope, moving validated work to another qualified person, or purchasing demonstrated capacity. Check timing again after the total fits.

## 6. Compare feasible portfolios and settle dependencies

First classify obligations: contractual, required approvals, ongoing service, and discretionary work. Ask the owning function which constraints are fixed and which can be renegotiated. Avoid ranking a mandatory service beside an optional launch as if both can be dropped.

Compare whole portfolios using the actual decision criteria: expected benefit, evidence, delay cost, remaining effort, scarce skill, timing, prerequisite value, reversibility, and displaced work. Use scoring only when definitions are comparable. A decimal total does not repair speculative inputs. Present the strongest keep case for rejected work and the condition for reopening it.

For each dependency, agree the deliverable, supplying owner, accepting owner, acceptance test, required date, allocated capacity, latest safe decision date, and fallback. A mention in one team's plan is a request, not a commitment. The receiving team should explicitly accept or decline it.

Resolve conflict in this order: test whether the outcomes themselves conflict, then whether the resource or timing claim conflicts, then whether scope or sequencing can resolve it. Escalate the residual choice with alternatives and consequences. A shared outcome needs one accountable outcome owner, while functional owners retain their actual resource authority. Name the tie-breaker when those rights collide.

Arithmetic allocation is useful for additive quantities with common definitions, such as regional bookings. It is misleading for rates, retention, customer satisfaction, or overlapping cohorts. Do not mechanically turn every company key result into a subordinate objective.

## 7. Variants without universal stage thresholds

| Context | Preserve | Adjust |
|---|---|---|
| Startup seeking a repeatable product | Customer evidence, cash and service constraints, accountable decision | Keep hypotheses and bounded learning explicit. Use a shared plan until separate team plans earn their cost |
| Multiple functions or business units | Common assumptions and accepted dependency commitments | Add cross-unit reconciliation where local success can undermine the shared result |
| Public-company business unit | Corporate targets, disclosure and spending approvals, Finance definitions | Show local control, proposed amendments, and parent decisions. Internal forecasts are not public guidance |
| Consumer or seasonal demand | Cohort quality, customer experience, unit economics | Tie release and observation windows to real usage and seasonal comparisons |
| Enterprise or long sales cycle | Delivery promises, qualified demand, implementation capacity | Match goals to the sales and adoption lag. Do not score a new lead as a realized customer outcome |
| Research or model-dependent product | Accepted evaluations, cost, quality and deployment conditions | Use learning and capability milestones where causal or technical uncertainty dominates, then test user impact |
| Infrastructure or hardware | Capacity commitments, procurement lead time, reliability | Separate irreversible commitments from flexible sequencing and use milestone acceptance before impact |
| Marketplace or multi-sided service | Health on each affected side | Segment liquidity, utilization and harm. Growth on one side is not sufficient evidence of system health |

Resolve cross-team choices before their latest safe decision date. Fit reliable observations within the goal window. Keep planning records when they improve decisions enough to justify their upkeep. There is no validated universal company-size cutoff for these conditions.

## 8. Plan audit and handoff

Before approval, test the plan against a delayed hire, the loss of a scarce specialist, a service demand surge, and a material strategic assumption failing. Use scenarios relevant to the actual company and account for correlated effects. Do not apply arbitrary proportional cuts that leave every initiative half funded.

Show the resulting funded portfolio and stop list, remaining gaps, decision owners, approval status, review dates, and evidence that would change the choice. Then use [goal design](goal-design.md) to define results and [operating reviews](../../operating-cadence/references/reviews-and-health.md) to manage the plan.

## Choose the shortest process that can resolve the conflict

[Philip Joubert](https://philipjoubert.com/how-offerzen-does-annual-planning/) connected marketplace leads, revenue and marketing spend at OfferZen. Before discussing initiatives, let owners change one input and see which spending or capacity assumption moves. If the plan still balances after removing its main demand driver, fix the model first.

[Lenny Rachitsky and Nels Gilbreth](https://review.firstround.com/the-secret-to-a-great-planning-process-lessons-from-airbnb-and-eventbrite/) describe executives giving direction, teams proposing plans, leaders integrating them, and teams revising commitments. Use that second round when two teams have promised the same scarce people or depend on incompatible dates.

[Jiaona Zhang and fellow operators](https://review.firstround.com/annual-planning-sucks-a-cpo-cro-cfo-and-coo-share-advice-on-how-to-make-it-better/) describe trimming the process when speed mattered at Linktree. A single owner with funds and staff already assigned can settle a local trade with the affected peer. Record what both sides gave up.

[Caryn Hubbard](https://buffer.com/resources/creating-startup-budget/) reported that Buffer's first budget took three and a half months. Put an end date on the planning exercise and identify which decisions actually need the full budget.
