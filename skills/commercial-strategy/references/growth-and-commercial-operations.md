# Growth and commercial operating analysis

## Diagnose the constraint

Separate target-customer fit, offer/positioning, distribution, buying conversion, product activation, retention, expansion, and service economics. A weak downstream result can have several causes. Compare them before recommending more traffic, more sales capacity, a discount, or a new channel.

Build a commercial view from the actual buying and use path. For each transition, define the eligible population, event, denominator, time window, owner, and lag. Show counts alongside rates. Break results by customer segment, channel, product, cohort, and meaningful contract terms. Separate one-off enterprise wins from repeatability, a new-customer mix change from same-customer improvement, and billing failure from voluntary departure.

Use a constraint table: observed result, competing explanations, evidence for/against each, economic consequence, missing evidence, and the decision it changes. Do not select a tactic merely because it is a source skill title.

## Positioning and message evidence

Connect the target buyer and triggering situation to their problem, alternative, differentiated capability, resulting value, and proof. A message is useful when the intended buyer understands it, believes it, and takes the next meaningful step. Match the sales asset to the buyer's need for proof, implementation confidence, economics, or vision. Differences in buyer readiness can be a diagnostic hypothesis, but do not explain every stalled sale.

Compare messaging with customer words, win/loss reasons, objections, and actual product behavior. Keep facts, promises, testimonials, and hypothetical benefits distinct. A competitor content gap matters only if the buyer cares about that topic. Offer clear alternative messages and state the specific change each tests. Do not invent customer logos, results, security assurances, or urgency.

## Distribution and acquisition choices

Compare plausible motions: inbound/content/search, outbound, paid media, community, partners, account-focused selling, and product-led adoption. These options combine distribution, sales, and product mechanisms and need not be mutually exclusive.

Assess each option against buyer access, problem awareness, proof requirements, sales cycle, and trialability. Compare capacity, time to evidence, incremental cost, saturation, dependencies, and expected customer quality. Show the strongest reason not to use the recommended channel. A small company's limited staff and runway can make a slower compounding channel unaffordable, even if it could work eventually. A public business unit may instead be constrained by an existing channel or account policy.

Estimate qualified reachable population × transition rates × expected contribution over an explicit horizon. Include labor, creative/production, tools, commissions, partner share, implementation, and support where incremental. Distinguish average acquisition economics from the marginal economics of increasing spend. Check whether a channel reaches new buyers or captures demand that would have converted anyway. Attribution reports describe allocation rules. They do not by themselves prove incremental impact.

A channel test names target segment, offer, exposure, budget/time bound, owner, expected lag, business outcome, and stop/expand criteria. Scale only after checking retention, contribution, service capacity, and whether the next tranche of buyers resembles the tested group. A cheap lead with no path to a retained customer is not efficient acquisition.

## Pipeline, capacity, and handoffs

Define lifecycle and opportunity stages by observable entry/exit evidence, not by a rep's optimism. Specify ownership, routing, rejection reasons, stale-deal checks, and nonstandard-deal controls. Calibrate response times, scoring thresholds, conversion targets, and coverage ratios to actual company evidence.

Track fit and intent separately. Content engagement need not indicate buying authority or readiness. Check scoring against historical won, lost, and poor-fit accounts, and examine false positives and false negatives. Use activity dates, next step, owner, expected timing, customer evidence, and loss reason to identify stalled pipeline. A moved close date needs an explanation. A weighted pipeline is a forecast only when probabilities and timing are calibrated to comparable history.

Reconcile sales targets with productive rep-months, hiring dates, ramp, pipeline creation, deal size, cycle length, win rates, implementation capacity, and renewal/expansion responsibility. Separate capacity from demand. Model attainment distribution rather than only a perfect quota year. A fixed pipeline-coverage multiple cannot resolve the absence of sufficient buyers or capable reps.

For handoffs, name sender, receiver, required information, acceptance event, response expectation, failure path, and measurement. Account routing needs ownership, fallback, available capacity, partner conflicts, and an exception decider. Repeated disputes can justify explicit policy. The threshold is actual conflict or lost work, not a fixed revenue or headcount number. Avoid building a CRM project to solve a few missing fields in a small team.

Sales incentives are commercial and financial inputs: credited event, target behavior, rate basis, attainment bands, ramp, split credit, cap/accelerator interaction, and cost. Test payout scenarios and ensure a rep can recompute pay from the agreed terms. Obtain rates, clawback rules, and legal terms from the responsible owners. Sales, Finance, HR, and Legal own their respective decisions where staffed. The operator can model options and coordinate review when assigned.

## Conversion and activation

Map visitor or account intent to the promised result and next step. Assess clarity, message match, proof, objections, next-action hierarchy, accessibility, and unnecessary friction. These diagnostic lenses generate hypotheses, not evidence of a predicted conversion improvement.

Measure where and for whom the journey fails. A signup is a different event from usable activation. Define first value and repeat value in terms of a customer's successful job, then test whether the proposed activation event predicts later retention across cohorts. Correlation may reflect already-motivated users rather than a causal effect of the event. A test that merely forces the event can fail to improve retention.

For a test, define assignment unit, eligible population, contamination risk, outcome, guardrails, duration based on outcome lag, and evidence needed for a decision. Low traffic or long enterprise sales cycles may favor a bounded paid pilot, customer observation, or phased rollout over an underpowered split test. Distinguish uncertainty from an inconclusive result and retain the status quo when evidence does not support a change.

## Retention, expansion, and growth mechanisms

Decompose loss by voluntary cancellation, failed payment, downgrade/contraction, nonrenewal, and reduced usage. Inspect tenure, segment, acquisition source, price/contract, product experience, and reason. Choose intervention for cause: onboarding help does not repair missing product capability, and a discount does not necessarily repair low value.

Evaluate saved customers by retained contribution after the offer, service burden, later churn, and behavior of customers who would have stayed anyway. A cancel-session save rate can rise while economics worsen. Keep cancellation and billing terms clear. Current consumer or subscription obligations need Legal's current guidance rather than an unverified general rule. Provider retries, technical configuration, and enforcement details remain specialist work.

A growth loop must identify who initiates it, why they act, what value or invitation reaches another person, conversion to meaningful use, repeat frequency, and cycle time. Model drop-off, saturation, overlapping audiences, fraud, and incentives. Referrals and collaboration can be useful without being self-sustaining. Do not call a diagram a network effect or project perpetual exponential growth from a first cohort.

Expansion analysis distinguishes price, seats, usage, add-ons, cross-sell, and mix changes. Include cannibalization, selling/support effort, and whether the customer actually gains value. Revenue growth without contribution or durable use is an incomplete commercial result.

## Output and failure checks

Produce a diagnosis, commercial option comparison, channel test, pipeline/capacity analysis, retention analysis, or launch commercial brief as needed. The reader should see the bottleneck, alternative explanations, evidence, economics, resources, owners, test, and conditions for scaling or stopping.

Rework conclusions based on blended averages, last-touch attribution alone, copied targets, or conversion without denominators. Challenge more spending before retention is understood and uplift without causal evidence. A tactic catalog may generate options. It does not select the investment.
