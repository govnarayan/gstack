# Make a partnership produce usable value

Use this reference when two organizations must contribute something for a commercial result. Begin with the customer outcome and the contributions each party can actually deliver. A signed agreement, an introduction, a shared announcement, or an investment does not establish an operating partnership.

The strongest case against a partnership is the coordination cost. Internal teams can spend scarce time integrating, enabling and supporting a relationship whose other party has little reason or ability to activate it. Compare the expected retained contribution with direct distribution, internal delivery, another partner and keeping the current approach. For the sourcing choice itself, use [capability sourcing](../../strategy/references/capability-sourcing.md).

## Establish the exchange and the reason to act

Describe what each party contributes, what it receives, who controls that contribution and why providing it remains attractive after launch. Keep capital returns, commercial contribution, learning and future access separate. One cannot compensate for a missing requirement in another without an explicit decision.

| Relationship | Contribution and incentive to test | Common operating failure |
|---|---|---|
| Referral or distribution | Qualified access and customer trust exchanged for an agreed reward or a better customer outcome | Audience overlap, weak qualification, rewards for customers who would have arrived anyway, or conflict with the direct sales team |
| Reseller or service partner | Selling, implementation or support capacity exchanged for margin and a viable customer relationship | Insufficient partner margin, untrained staff, unclear account ownership or an offer the partner cannot support |
| Product or technology integration | A working capability, supported interface or deployment path exchanged for revenue, usage or useful access | A demo mistaken for production readiness, incompatible roadmaps, service gaps or dependence on a capability still planned |
| Marketplace or joint offer | Complementary supply, demand or assets exchanged for completed customer outcomes | Both sides wait for the other, a local availability gap, conflicting incentives or one party bearing most recovery work |
| Strategic relationship with capital | Capital and specified access exchanged for a financial claim and separately agreed operating opportunities | A financial holding is counted as commercial adoption, or promised collaboration has no internal sponsor or capacity |

These relationships can coexist. Evaluate each exchange and its obligations separately. A partner's strategic interest does not establish a buyer's need or confer rights to use its assets.

Assess the counterpart as well as the concept. Verify relevant capability, access to the intended customer or asset, authority to commit it, delivery history, credible references, available capacity and motivation to work with this company. Strong reputation in one activity does not establish suitability for another. Distinguish direct evidence from an introduction or an interested executive. Compare another counterpart and the option of buying access without a wider relationship.

### Map the layers before negotiating the combined offer

Trace customer value and how each participant earns or receives value at each layer. Mark what this company already owns, must obtain access to, or would need to build. Compare current alternatives on the same layer, using dated evidence and explicit unknowns. Control over one layer does not give control over the customer relationship, data, delivery or economics at another.

| Layer in a synthetic reporting partnership | Customer/participant value and required right | Current capability, evidence and alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified customer access | Customers find a relevant offer. Distributor receives an agreed reward. Permission to reach eligible accounts and clear account ownership are required. | Partner reports an eligible audience, but independent reach is unknown. Compare direct access and another distributor before assigning expected volume. |
| Working reporting capability | Customer receives an accurate usable report. Provider receives service revenue. Supported access, export rights and change responsibilities are required. | Provider's standard output is live and tested for the pilot use case. An alternate supplier's required export behavior is unknown, not absent. |
| Onboarding and recovery | Customer completes setup and gets problems resolved. Each party avoids unrecovered work. Acceptance, escalation and staffing obligations must be accepted. | Internal onboarding is live and measured. Automated matching is planned. The company could build matching or retain bounded manual work instead of assuming partner access includes it. |
| Economics and customer continuity | Parties recover cost and customers retain service or a usable transition. Charging, reward, settlement and exit responsibilities need agreement. | Pilot economics are modeled and later reconciled to actuals. Renewed access and replacement-provider portability remain untested conditions. |

The completed map names the actor who controls each right, the actor performing the work and the actor capturing value. If the attractive economics depend on a layer no participant can supply, change the offer or stop. For value-chain analysis beyond a relationship, use [strategy](../../strategy/SKILL.md).

## Keep capability status separate from evidence status

For every dependency, record the exact capability, usable version, population, conditions, capacity and owner. Then give it two independent states:

1. Capability state: live, limited/pilot, planned, unavailable, or unknown.
2. Evidence state: directly tested, documented with conditions, reported by a party, contradicted, or unknown, with a source and date.

A planned feature can have a documented roadmap while remaining unavailable. A live capability can still have unknown performance under this customer's load. An empty cell is undefined, not a failed test. Do not infer absence from an incomplete comparison or infer readiness from a sales claim. Assign the unresolved question a test and a consequence for commitment.

Record the rights and responsibilities at the layer where they matter: customer access and account ownership, brand and claims, product/interface and change control, data use and access, delivery/support/recovery, economics and settlement, and termination, portability or transition. The operator prepares the required commercial and operating choices. Authorized commercial, technical and specialist owners establish acceptable terms and approvals.

## Carry reciprocal contributions through activation

Build one relationship record in the existing operating system. Populate it with actual evidence and named owners, rather than merely requesting these fields:

| Record | Required decision content |
|---|---|
| Value and alternative | Customer problem, expected result, strongest feasible alternative, horizon and displaced internal work |
| Reciprocal commitment | Each contribution, internal owner, partner owner, timing, acceptance evidence, dependencies and remedy if it fails |
| Commercial exchange | Eligibility, customer/account ownership, charging and reward events, shares, minimum commitments, exclusions and settlement evidence |
| Activation path | Eligible population → exposure → working first use → repeated or retained value → realized contribution. Counted identity and time window at every step |
| Readiness | Actual capability/evidence states, signed versus proposed commitments, product and service acceptance, enabled staff and permitted claims |
| Operating exceptions | Capacity shortfall, conflicting sales claims, duplicate accounts, missed service, disputed rewards, escalation and decision owner |
| Continue, change or exit | Outcome and economic conditions, review trigger, bounded next commitment, transition obligations and customer continuity |

Set the central commercial commitment before mobilizing dependent teams. Early exploratory work may be bounded and reversible, but it should have its own owner, cost and stop condition. A letter expressing intent is not permission to promise a service or spend beyond delegated authority.

Channel conflict needs an explicit decision. Identify overlapping accounts, competing direct offers, attribution disputes, pricing differences and incentives for both sales teams. Decide eligibility and account ownership before exposure, then reconcile actual exceptions. A channel that shifts existing customers while earning a reward can look successful in partner reporting and reduce company contribution.

Develop the offer with both participants and intended customers before a large commitment. Test customer usefulness, partner willingness to supply the promised contribution and internal ability to deliver together. Feed observed objections, failed activations and service effort back into the participant roles, offer, incentives and rights. A revised joint proposition needs renewed evidence and acceptance where obligations change. Post-signature reporting cannot repair an offer that neither side had reason to activate.

## Calculate retained incremental economics

Use a common horizon and cohort. Separate reachable population, eligible customers, attributed arrivals, incremental customers, first value, retention and contribution. Estimate incrementality using a credible comparison where feasible. Partner attribution alone does not supply it.

Calculate incremental retained customer contribution after relevant service costs, then deduct partner payments, incentives, integration, enablement, support and ongoing administration not already included. Show timing and scarce capacity separately. Reconcile displaced direct sales, cannibalized offers and shared costs without double-counting them. Positive lifetime value based on an assumed long life cannot clear a near-term capacity or cash constraint.

### Populated synthetic record

All quantities below are invented practice inputs. They are not benchmarks or forecasts.

A software business tests a referral relationship over 90 days. Among 100 eligible accounts offered the partner route, 24 activate and 16 retain useful paid use. A comparable randomly assigned group of 100 has 14 activations and 10 retained accounts. The observed incremental retained count is six. The small comparison informs a bounded next commitment. Uncertainty and account differences still matter before larger spend.

Contribution per retained account over this horizon is assumed to be $240 after ordinary variable delivery cost. Partner payments are $300, extra support is six hours at an assumed $40/hour, and one-time setup is $1,200. Estimated pilot contribution is `6 × 240 − 300 − 6 × 40 − 1,200 = −$300`. The 16 attributed retained accounts would overstate incremental value. With these fixed inputs, at least eight incremental retained accounts would clear pilot cost because `1,740 / 240 = 7.25`.

| Decision field | Populated evidence and action |
|---|---|
| Reciprocal contribution | Partner supplied the agreed eligible introductions. Internal team delivered onboarding and measured retained use. Reach beyond this cohort remains unknown. |
| Capability condition | Standard onboarding is live and tested. Automated account matching is planned and only documented. Expansion cannot assume it works. |
| Finding | Retained use improves in the observed comparison, but the first cohort does not recover setup cost. |
| Changed commitment | Complete the bounded pilot. Do not authorize a broad rollout from attributed counts. Test whether another comparable cohort exists and whether manual matching fits support capacity. |
| Scale condition | A repeat cohort with no additional setup would yield `6 × 240 − 300 − 240 = $900` under unchanged assumptions. That is a conditional calculation, not evidence of repeatability. At least three incremental retained accounts clear its $540 recurring cost. Extra setup or service load changes the threshold. |
| Exit and ownership | The commercial owner decides the next cohort after the evidence review. The service owner maintains existing customer support and resolves outstanding rewards if the relationship stops. |

If direct sales would have reached these accounts, a longer sales cycle changes retention, or support peaks exceed available hours, rebuild the comparison. The [cohort method](../../operating-cadence/references/cohort-deep-dives.md) supplies the complete population and timing contract. Use [channel and enablement choices](channel-and-enablement-choices.md) for field readiness and distribution tests.

## Close the operating loop

Review promised versus delivered contributions, activation losses, retained economics, exceptions and changes to the alternative. Choose continued operation, a changed offer or incentive, a narrower relationship, another test, or exit. Preserve what was learned even if financial or commercial value fails. A successful relationship must have an ongoing operating owner after launch work ends.
