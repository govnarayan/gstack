# Pricing and monetization decisions

## Partition the decision

Price level, charging unit, packaging, commercial terms, and billing execution are distinct. Identify which is failing before redesigning all of them. A pricing-page clarity problem may require a clearer explanation, while expensive heavy usage may require a different usage allowance or charging unit.

Use customer value and alternatives to assess willingness to pay. Use cost to serve and company constraints to test viability. Neither alone selects the right price. Buyer acceptance without viable economics is insufficient, and a cost-plus price without buyer evidence can be unsellable.

## Charging unit and packages

Compare per-seat, usage, transaction, flat subscription, outcome, and hybrid models where relevant. Evaluate alignment with customer value, predictability, measurability, explainability, gaming, cost variability, customer control, procurement, expansion, and operational feasibility. A single power user using many AI resources may make seats a weak proxy for cost or value. A usage price can instead create budget uncertainty or discourage useful adoption. An outcome price needs a reliable and agreed outcome measure and treatment of failures.

Packages should let distinct buyers understand and choose an offer. Compare limits, capability, service, support, governance, and usage allowances against segment needs. Test whether entry plans deliver enough value, higher plans have evidenced differentiation, and mandatory safety or baseline reliability is being treated appropriately. Include migration and cannibalization. One offer, multiple tiers, add-ons, and negotiated contracts are alternatives. Three tiers, a premium multiple, or a mandatory free plan are not universal requirements.

For a free plan or trial, state the acquisition/learning mechanism, variable cost exposure, qualified path to paid use, abuse risk, support burden, and limits. Compare it with a paid pilot or demonstration. For marketplaces, model how pricing changes incentives, participation, liquidity, off-platform behavior, and the company's retained share on both sides.

## Willingness-to-pay evidence

Segment evidence by buyer, problem, offer, and buying condition. Begin with current spending, workaround, budget owner, decision process, realized value, and accepted terms. Stated prices and competitor prices generate hypotheses. Paid pilots, accepted proposals, purchases, renewals, and use at an actual price provide stronger behavioral tests, while each still reflects selection and context.

Van Westendorp questions can describe perceived price acceptability for a clearly specified offer: too cheap to trust, inexpensive/good value, expensive but possible, and too expensive. If using the method, make curve construction, direction, intersections, and segmentation explicit and reproducible. Do not label a survey crossing as the revenue- or profit-maximizing price. Poor intersections can reflect heterogeneous respondents, small samples, question quality, or an unclear offer. They do not automatically prove the need to split the segment.

MaxDiff compares relative priorities. It does not directly establish an absolute price. Properly designed conjoint analysis can examine trade-offs among price and offer attributes. Choosing a feature frequently does not automatically establish its economic value. Use specialist research/statistical input for consequential study design and estimation. Simulated buyers are hypothesis-generation tools, never customer evidence.

Choose sample and time horizon from the decision, expected effect, variation, conversion baseline, and renewal or sales cycle. Fixed interview counts, sample sizes, or study durations do not establish adequate evidence by themselves. Report limits, especially when only a few large accounts are affected.

## Economic model for a proposed change

Model current and proposed policy over the same horizon, separately for new customers, existing cohorts, renewals, grandfathered contracts, and different usage segments. Expose eligibility, migration dates, acquisition/conversion, price, discounts, usage, expansion, contraction, churn, cost to serve, support, implementation, commissions, and billing/collection timing. Keep recurring revenue, recognized revenue, and cash separate.

For a simple one-period customer-base test with unchanged usage and unit cost, retained customer count × net price explains the revenue result. The revenue-neutral retention fraction after a price change is old net price ÷ new net price. This is a narrow break-even calculation, not a forecast of how many customers will leave. Contribution break-even instead compares old contribution per customer ÷ new contribution per customer when those contributions are positive and comparable. Multi-period cases need cohort cash/contribution flows because timing, acquisition, retention, and expansion interact.

Model conversion loss together with effective acquisition cost. A higher price can shorten payback per customer while fewer customers make cost per acquired customer rise. Include cannibalization, downshifts to cheaper packages, workload moved to free allowances, partner share, and discounts. Annual prepayment accelerates cash but can lower contract economics, change customer commitment, and create future service obligations. It is not automatically a lifetime-value improvement.

Stress the assumptions that could reverse the choice: price acceptance, incremental churn, usage cost, migration, discount leakage, and timing. Show base/downside/upside only when scenarios reflect coherent drivers. Estimate churn and discount effects from relevant evidence. Trial conversion alone does not determine the pricing choice.

## Commercial policy and implementation readiness

For discounting, define floor economics, legitimate reasons, give/get, duration, approver, exception evidence, and expiry. Apply existing authority rather than inventing a new approval ladder. Contractual, tax, accounting, and employment questions stay with the relevant specialist owners. The operator can quantify effects and prepare the decision.

For a price change, specify affected customers and contracts, notice obligations, timing, grandfathering or migration, communication, sales/CS preparation, billing readiness, monitoring, and rollback or correction path. Notice periods come from current contracts and legal requirements plus the company's choice, not a universal number copied from a source. New-customer-only pricing reduces direct exposure of existing contracts but does not guarantee zero churn or reputation impact.

For usage or outcome pricing, require evidence that the charge can be measured and explained. Check account mapping, duplicate events, aggregation windows, tier boundaries, allowances, credits, failed tasks, disputes, and trace from usage to bill. Product and billing specialists own technical design. Finance owns accounting policy. Ask Finance to specify the applicable revenue policy rather than inferring it from invoices.

## Outputs and failure tests

A pricing recommendation maps segments, offers, charging units, package alternatives, and price evidence. Include comparative economics, sensitivity, break-even, discount rules, migration exposure, decision rights, and test/rollback criteria. A pricing-impact analysis can be narrower when the proposed structure is already fixed.

Rework competitor-only pricing, interviews presented as committed demand, and habitual tier counts. Include heavy-user costs and incremental profit. Test indirect effects and challenge approvals based on one conversion threshold.

## Underwrite both sides of a cheaper plan

[Greg Peters](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/netflix-partners-with-microsoft) introduced an advertising partner for Netflix's lower-priced tier. [Netflix's first-year account](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/one-year-into-netflix-adverts) reported monthly active users. That measures audience reach. It leaves the revenue per paying account and the cost of serving that audience unresolved.

Separate new customers, returning customers and existing customers trading down. For each group, calculate subscription receipts plus realized advertising receipts less incremental delivery and partner costs. Compare with what the group would otherwise have bought. A profitable new signup can coexist with a loss on an existing subscriber moving tiers.

For a marketplace, keep the transaction denominator visible. [DoorDash's operating results](https://ir.doordash.com/news/news-details/2025/DoorDash-Releases-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2024-Financial-Results/) distinguish net revenue and contribution profit against the value of marketplace orders. Ask which costs still sit below that profit measure before using the reported margin to approve an incentive.
