# Customer success playbooks

State why the customer bought, what would count as value, who can confirm it and who controls renewal. Read the signed terms, implementation record and usage history.

## From sale to first value

1. Before close, name the business sponsor, implementation owner, users, renewal decision maker and first use case. Have the receiving owner accept the handoff. [GitLab](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/customer-experience/csm/onboarding/)
2. At kickoff, ask the customer to correct the promised outcome and date. Identify its own dependencies, such as access, staffing or procurement. An internal kickoff is rarely proof of value. [Onboarding](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/customer-experience/csm/onboarding/)
3. Separate setup completed, first useful outcome, repeated use and customer confirmation. Use in-product guidance for repeatable self-service steps and human help when an external dependency blocks progress. [Intercom](https://www.intercom.com/blog/designing-modular-user-onboarding)
4. At each review, record the next customer outcome and owner. An escalation needs a customer impact, severity, next update and recovery evidence. [Escalations](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/customer-experience/csm/escalations/)

## Check whether a health score predicts anything

Start with accounts that actually renewed, contracted or left. Reconstruct signals as they stood before the decision. Exclude later notes revealing the outcome.

Compare flagged and unflagged accounts separately by contract size, age, use case and season. Count false alarms, missed losses and accounts that received an intervention.

When usage contradicts sponsor or budget evidence, preserve both. A single combined score can conceal the reason the account needs action. [GitLab health review](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/customer-experience/csm/health-score-triage/)

A synthetic review finds 12 flagged accounts among 40 renewals due. Of eight losses, five had been flagged. Seven flagged accounts stayed and three losses were missed.

The flag caught five of eight losses. Only five of 12 flags preceded a loss. Inspect the three misses and the seven surviving accounts before increasing intervention volume.

For an early product with few renewals, use named customer risks and follow through on outcomes. Reopen a score when repeated comparable renewal decisions give it a meaningful test.

## Renew, expand or escalate

Work backward from customer budgeting, contractual notice and procurement deadlines. The renewal owner confirms commercial authority. Customer Success supplies verified value and dependencies. [Operator accounts](https://review.firstround.com/21-ways-to-shore-up-your-customer-success-org/)

Separate an account escalation from a service incident. Give the customer one communications owner. Technical responders retain incident authority and provide restoration evidence. [Incident roles](https://www.atlassian.com/incident-management/incident-response/roles-responsibilities)

Offer expansion when the original use case works and a new group has need, a sponsor and budget. Send pricing exceptions through the same approval route as a new sale.

In the synthetic cohort, one missed loss had high usage after the sponsor left. The new buyer never confirmed funding. Add sponsor continuity to the next review.

Update the renewal forecast and cash date. Put the unaccepted sponsor handoff in the weekly review. Close it when the incoming owner confirms the decision path.
