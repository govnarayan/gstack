# Transitions and continuity

Use this procedure when an acquisition, separation, major process change or system transition must keep operating while ownership and delivery change. It complements [delivery and decision methods](delivery-and-decisions.md), which already covers charters, accepted dependencies, feasible schedules and handover. Do not create another tracker when the existing plan can carry these decisions.

Use [commercial operating interfaces](commercial-operating-interfaces.md) when customer/offer choices, account ownership, selling capacity, incentives, brand or service decisions must be reconciled through the transition.

The strongest case for a slower or smaller transition is protecting a working service, customer relationship or scarce capability. Its cost can include duplicate systems, delayed benefits and temporary dependencies. Compare both sides before declaring either speed or preservation the priority.

## Define the actual transition boundaries

Read the approved purpose, current operating model, functional process maps, contracts and service commitments, data and system dependencies, staffing and resource plan. Name the sponsor, functional owners and actual approvers. Legal, Finance, People, Security, Engineering, commercial and other staffed functions retain their decisions. The coordinator constructs the integrated analysis and follows evidence through acceptance.

Separate announcement, legal close, operational change, brand change, systems conversion and final service exit where they differ. Use actual dates and permissions. A legal boundary does not establish that every process changes that day. Counsel determines permitted pre-close contact, information use and agreements. A planning request does not authorize operational coordination or disclosure.

Name the integration or transition lead early enough to receive the decision rationale, diligence findings and benefit assumptions from their original owners. Compare internal or acquired-unit candidates by process knowledge, credibility with affected teams, sponsor access and actual time available. Reconcile a dedicated team with the expected volume and overlap of work. Borrowed leaders need credible coverage for their ongoing duties.

For a restricted information request, record the analytical question, required data, permitted provider and recipient, classification and approval owner, authorized channel and expected usable output. Give it an identifier and track revision, approval, receipt, analysis and accepted return. Current counsel and control owners decide permitted access and any sanitization before operating use. Do not treat historical permission or a repository login as current authorization.

State what must continue for customers, employees, suppliers and other affected users. Have the responsible functions identify mandatory obligations and distinguish them from preferred improvements. Name what the change may not disrupt, whose evidence supports that condition and who may approve a trade-off.

## Choose a disposition by process and horizon

Inventory material processes and systems, including local variations and shared services. Inspect end-to-end handoffs rather than relying only on department checklists. Identify the actual provider, recipient, tools, data, permissions, capacity and acceptance event.

| Transition record | What to decide |
|---|---|
| Current state | Who performs and accepts the work, on which systems, with what demand, quality and service obligations |
| Required transition state | What must work at each real boundary, what remains as today, the provider and recipient, temporary dependencies and resources |
| End state | What is kept, adopted, combined, newly built, separately provided, shared, replaced or discontinued, with rationale and authority |
| Route between states | Milestones, accepted inputs, access/data/system changes, trained coverage, tests, fallback and change notice |
| Conditions to move | Evidence needed, accepting owner, latest useful decision date and consequence if it fails |

For a combination, compare keeping either process, combining selected parts, building new work or buying a temporary service. For a separation, compare separate, shared and replacement arrangements. Choose at subprocess level when broad labels hide different obligations. A single end-state chart cannot answer what happens between now and then.

Keep a must-have and non-goal record. Each item needs a reason it must work at that boundary, functional owner, dependency provider, acceptance evidence, capacity and decision date. Put later improvements into a stated horizon with an owner. A non-goal is not permission to leave a required obligation without coverage.

## Reconcile plans with the people providing the dependencies

Have each lead walk the operating sequence and present the milestones it needs from others. Providers explicitly confirm, qualify or dispute the recipient's date and deliverable. Add missing work and distinguish a resource request from an accepted commitment. Record exact provider-to-recipient interfaces and update the actual integrated schedule after the session.

Test the critical path and scarce resources across workstreams. A systems team may need to separate access before Finance can stop using a reporting service. Moving one milestone can change another team's service exit, cost and notice deadline. Resolve the dependency before reporting both teams green.

Use a shared timeline, live review or direct paired discussion as the work requires. Physical cards, a particular meeting name or a weekly rhythm are optional. The test is whether contradictory commitments become feasible choices with accepted owners, dates and fallback.

## Design temporary shared services with an exit

A temporary service can bridge a capability that will not be ready at the transition boundary. First compare discontinuing the need, transferring the work directly, replicating it, using an external provider and retaining a temporary service. Include customer continuity, knowledge, control, cost and readiness. A contract does not make the delivery capacity real.

Always write the actual provider and recipient before using a directional label. In a separation, parent-to-separated-business service and separated-business-to-parent service are different dependencies. Capture both when present. Do not let a label hide which side retains staff, systems, data, cost or exit work.

Build a service decision record with the owners who will provide and consume it. Counsel supplies legal terms and contractual interpretation. Functions specify service requirements; Finance and Tax validate charges and treatment.

| Field | Evidence to retain |
|---|---|
| Service and direction | Unique identifier, service description, provider, recipient, both owners, scope and permitted users/data |
| Operating requirement | Demand, service measures, access, knowledge, vendor dependencies, coverage and exception route |
| Economics | Recurring charge and basis, setup/migration/exit cost, minimum commitments, partial-exit effects and who bears each cost |
| End state | Successor capability, owner, resources, migration or discontinuation plan and acceptance test |
| Exit conditions | Prerequisite milestones, required notices, approvals, tested successor, records retention and final invoice/payment handling |
| Control and review | Scope/duration/price changes, service issues, escalation, current forecast, accepted extension and planned versus actual exit/cost |

Develop exit plans before commitment where possible. Derive dates from actual dependencies and resources, not hopeful durations. Reconcile contractual notice deadlines with the time needed to build and test the successor. A latest decision date is the required completion date less mobilization, execution, test and approval time under the actual calendar. Add uncertainty explicitly.

Stagger exits when they claim the same migration or receiving capacity. A high recurring fee can justify earlier exit only if the required service remains ready. Partial termination can change the economics of what remains, so recompute the whole affected service rather than subtracting a fraction of its fee. Keep records-retention requirements visible after operational service stops.

Route additions, changed service scope, extensions, disputes and terminations through the actual owners. Update cost, dependencies, notices and exit evidence together. A signed termination is not proof that the recipient can operate without the service.

## Keep three cost views distinct

Coordinate the model with Finance using consistent scope, periods and assumptions. A transition can create costs on both sides even when a budget allocation moves.

| View | Question | Build and check |
|---|---|---|
| Standalone operating cost | What will the separated or receiving business need to run the required service? | Direct work, historical allocated/unallocated support, missing capabilities and actual replacement capacity. Historical allocation is evidence to inspect, not replacement cost |
| One-time transition cost | What must be spent to move between states? | Migration, setup, double running, training, temporary coverage and exit, with cash timing and recurring items kept separate |
| Stranded cost | What spending remains with the parent or service provider after volume or work leaves? | Retained labor, licenses, facilities and vendor commitments, with feasible removal actions, timing and service consequences |

Build each view by service or subfunction and distinguish labor from non-labor. Reconcile costs transferred to another entity with costs remaining and the intended end state. Moving an allocation does not remove a lease, employee or vendor commitment. Time released can create usable capacity without creating cash savings. Identify who will use it and when.

Bridge the early estimate to the scoped project budgets. Preserve the original assumptions, then explain changes in work, amount, timing and required service. Consolidate project costs and sweep for central work that no project owns, such as transition support, training, retained coverage and approved specialist work. Check that shared costs appear once and that quarterly or monthly amounts reconcile to the total. Finance validates accounting categories. The operator retains internal effort and displaced work in the capacity view even when those are excluded from a particular expense report.

Give every material line a sponsor or cost owner, project/service identifier, source estimate, current budget, timing, assumptions and approval status. Report a missing owner or treatment as unresolved. Reconcile subsequent actuals and forecasts through the existing Finance calendar and approval route rather than moving costs between views to preserve the original target.

A temporary service provider can retain costs after that service exits. A parent can also retain costs from separation more broadly. Do not limit the stranded-cost review to one service direction. Accounting classification, entity financial statements, treasury execution and tax remain specialist work. Use [financial resource decisions](../../financial-resource-decisions/SKILL.md) for the comparable model and approvals.

## Protect existing revenue before adding optional growth

Before assigning cross-sell, new campaigns or combined-product targets, identify transition-induced revenue exposure. Use account and partner evidence, affected customer journeys, upcoming renewals, service obligations, ownership changes and actual customer concerns. Distinguish an evidenced risk from a presumed reaction.

Create a continuity record for the affected population: account/segment, revenue boundary and period, exposure mechanism, evidence, relationship owner, service/support path, mitigation, cost and decision date. Reconcile shared account identities and overlapping exposure so the same revenue is not counted twice. Commercial and Finance owners validate definitions and external commitments.

Compare keeping the current relationship and support arrangement, a staged handoff and a wider change. Name who leads a shared account, who accepts a lead, who scopes delivery and who approves pricing or contractual promises. The customer should have a workable path during back-end change. A temporarily retained service model may be worth its cost when premature consolidation threatens existing value.

Reconcile protection work and optional growth against the same sales, technical scoping, implementation and support capacity. Avoid giving each a separate plan that claims the same people. Move growth forward when the continuity conditions and actual capacity support it. Use [commercial strategy](../../commercial-strategy/SKILL.md) for account prioritization, incremental economics and selling design.

When messages depend on one another, build a communication clock with audience, purpose, owner, approval, channel, actual time zone, prerequisite and fallback. Pair priority accounts with a relationship owner and a counterpart where a handoff is needed. Communications, commercial, Legal and disclosure owners determine the actual sequence. Verify that the receiving audience gets an accurate service path and that a changed milestone updates the affected messages.

## Prove readiness and make the decision

Select critical end-to-end processes from actual failure consequence and transition exposure. Decide where functional certification is sufficient and where an active walkthrough or rehearsal is needed with process experts. Compare the cost of testing too late to repair with testing before the operating design is stable enough to exercise.

For each test, record starting conditions, normal and important failure cases, participating providers/recipients, expected result, observed result, gap, owner and retest evidence. Include customers, suppliers and employees where their experience crosses functions. Exercise access, data, notifications, service coverage and fallback, not just installation. The responsible experts own specialist test sufficiency.

Rehearse a consequential cutover with task identifiers, owners, dependencies, times, decision checkpoints and incident routing. The needed rehearsal depth and count depend on what remains uncertain. Track fixes and rerun the affected sequence. Do not treat an attended simulation as a successful one.

Bring one readiness decision to the actual authority: proceed, proceed within explicit conditions, narrow, delay or stop. Keep critical blockers, dissent, evidence reviewed, implemented mitigations, unimplemented conditions and accepted residual risk visible. Identify actions after which reversal becomes difficult before choosing the decision deadline. An average completion percentage cannot cancel one unacceptable critical failure.

During a transition event, maintain the verified facts, open incidents, dependency consequences, decisions and next checkpoints through the existing functional response. A temporary command center coordinates cross-team issues and escalation. It does not replace helpdesks, People channels, technical incident command or functional management.

## Transfer the work and close the temporary structure

Set close conditions before ramping down coordination. Check required milestones, resolved or explicitly owned incidents, restored normal queues, accepted successor services and authorized approval. Transfer ongoing scope, budget, documentation, access, skills, exceptions and remaining work to named owners. A workstream can transfer before every long-term benefit appears if the remaining work and later outcome review are accepted.

Retire duplicate reporting, temporary permissions and unused coordination structures through their owners. Reconcile outstanding service charges and project costs with Finance. Retain required records in the approved system and update the playbook with a named maintainer. Inspect the first real operating cycle after handover and verify corrective changes on their next real use. Documentation alone does not establish continuity or adoption.

## Maintain continuity after the transition

For each critical business process, record its customer or operating objective, minimum usable service, tolerable interruption and data-loss constraints supplied by the responsible owners. Map the people, systems, records, locations and vendors it depends on. Compare a dependency failure with a shared failure affecting several processes, and identify required manual or alternate operation. Technical recovery belongs to the technical owners. The coordinator ensures recovery restores a usable business process and reconciled records.

Specify who may activate the continuity plan, who leads recovery, who accepts restored business service and who approves internal or external communication. Keep contact and approval information available through an authorized route if the normal system fails. Derive recovery priorities from business impact and dependency order, then compare mobilization, restoration, validation and backlog-clearance time with the approved interruption constraint. A restored application alone does not establish that payments, customer work or records are complete.

Plan exercises with the business and technical owners: scenario, affected population, objectives, exclusions, recovery sequence, evidence, elapsed times and acceptance conditions. Distinguish a discussion, a component test and an end-to-end operating recovery. Have experts choose safe technical tests. Capture problems, actual versus required recovery, remediation owner and retest evidence. If a test misses a required condition, obtain the actual acceptance or revised plan and rerun the relevant work. A historical policy's test frequency or retest deadline is not a universal rule.

Keep the plan current when people, products, suppliers, systems, locations, priorities or obligations change. Retain an ongoing business service owner after vendor onboarding and project close. The record should name performance evidence, incident severity authority, recovery responsibility, customer/partner communication and the review or exit trigger.

Link each material risk to the business objective and process, the reason for exposure, existing controls, control owner and evidence that the control operated. Add incidents and near misses to that same record, preserve the supported cause and rival explanations, and track corrective action to verified implementation and effectiveness on subsequent work. Use [scenario response](scenario-response.md#operating-risk-and-control-coordination) for the detailed control/evidence procedure. Specialist owners determine obligations, control sufficiency, incident severity and accepted residual risk.
