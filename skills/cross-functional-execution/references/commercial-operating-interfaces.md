# Commercial operating interfaces

Use this procedure when a combination, separation or cross-functional change requires commercial choices to fit together. Prepare the analysis and executable records with Sales, Marketing, Customer Success, Product, Finance and other responsible owners. The internal operator does not acquire their account, pricing, compensation or contractual authority.

Read [commercial strategy](../../commercial-strategy/SKILL.md) for customer evidence, incremental economics, selling capacity and channel tests. Use [partnership operating methods](../../commercial-strategy/references/partnership-operating-model.md) for reciprocal contributions and partner activation. This reference joins the decisions across functions rather than creating a second commercial system.

## Put the upstream choices before dependent commitments

Establish the customer outcome, existing revenue to protect, offer and product boundaries, segment and geography, routes to customers, brand treatment and required service. Compare the strongest feasible alternative and keeping the current approach. Identify what is decided, provisional or unknown.

Translate those choices into coverage and roles, incentives, processes, systems and field readiness. Record which downstream commitment depends on which upstream choice, its decider, required-by date and fallback. A team can investigate reversibly while a choice is open. It cannot promise delivery capacity or a new offer merely because another team assumed it.

In a separation, make a record for each side: customer ownership, products and intellectual-property rights supplied by specialists, contracts, partners, brand and marketing assets, systems/data disposition, service capacity and interim delivery. Compare standalone, temporarily shared and replacement arrangements. Use [transitions and continuity](transitions-and-continuity.md) for costs, shared services, permission and acceptance. Do not finalize separate territories against an unresolved shared-customer rule.

## Build the account and selling motion

Reconcile customer identities across books before ranking opportunities. Preserve source identifiers, group/entity relationships, duplicate matches, uncertain matches, current relationship owner, permission and relevant contract restrictions. A potential match is an investigation item until accepted by the account owners.

Compare four candidate motions where relevant: advancing existing joint pipeline, introducing a complementary offer to a new account, using a renewal or other customer event, and developing a reference use case jointly with a customer. For each, specify customer need, eligible accounts, buying evidence, retained contribution, time to result, effort and service dependencies. A shared customer is not evidence that it needs both products.

Combine top-down opportunity estimates with account owners' evidence. Rank on customer fit, timing, plausible value, margin, readiness and workload. Use recent purchases, engagement and installed products where they change the buying hypothesis. Ask reps to challenge the list, inspect false positives and preserve reasons for exclusions. A source's fixed large account cutoff or recency score is not a company policy.

Give every accepted opportunity a complete interface:

| Step | Evidence and authority |
|---|---|
| Identify and route | Sourcing owner, account owner, eligible need, permitted contact and destination record |
| Accept or reject | Receiving owner, qualification evidence, response expectation, rejection reason and exception decider |
| Scope and propose | Solution owner, technical/service feasibility, required customer information, proposal and statement-of-work owner |
| Price and contract | Actual pricing and exception approvers, accurate terms and claims, contract owner and required approvals |
| Book and deliver | Finance counting event, service handoff acceptance, implementation capacity, customer relationship and support ownership |

Track the same opportunity through the systems actually used. Assign a canonical identifier and reconciliation owner where two systems remain. Define who may contact the account, resolve overlapping claims and approve an exception before outreach. Verify the path on an actual accepted opportunity and a rejected or disputed one.

## Choose coverage, territories and rollout together

Compare current customer value with potential value using separate evidence. A large existing account, a promising prospect and an expensive account with little expansion potential need different choices. Include service burden and retained contribution, rather than assigning coverage from revenue alone.

Compare a generalist relationship owner, specialist overlay, new-business focus, inside coverage, partner delivery or a combination. Then compare organizing by product, geography/language, industry, customer size or lifecycle. Show which customer requirement each grouping serves, what cross-selling or expertise it gives up, and how shared specialists are prioritized. Product names or organizational fashion do not select the model.

For each territory or segment, calculate required work from account counts and time by activity: relationship coverage, prospecting, scoping, travel, internal work, renewal and handoff. Compare it with productive capacity after existing obligations, absence and ramp. Skills and time windows must fit as well as aggregate hours. Rebalance accounts, narrow the push, change the coverage model, sequence markets or add credible capacity when the plan does not fit.

Prioritize markets using both demand and internal ability to serve: eligible customer need, buying timing, reachable pipeline, local expertise, delivery/support coverage and time to useful evidence. Set an initial bounded scope, exceptions for a justified opportunity outside it, and expansion or reorder conditions. A historical regional phase list cannot establish today's demand.

## Compare incentive mechanics before rates

Start with the behavior and counted event, then assess whether the role can influence it and whether comparable targets can be set. With the responsible owners, compare:

| Candidate | Decision input | Mechanism and failure to test |
|---|---|---|
| Quota-based incentive | A defensible quota and observed attainment distribution | Target incentive multiplied by an approved attainment curve. Weak quotas can reward territory assignment rather than contribution |
| Individual commission rate | Individual quota and target incentive can support a rate | A rate applied to credited business. Check comparability and what happens above target |
| Flat commission | A defined eligible sale and credible rate economics | Rate multiplied by eligible credited amount. Growth in payout must still fit margin and selling contribution |
| Partial nonquota objectives | Important work has observable acceptance but no defensible sales quota | A defined portion of incentive tied to accepted objectives. Vague ratings or all-pay dependence can obscure performance and pay |

Build scenarios using company-supplied terms. Keep fixed pay, target incentive, payout timing, recognized versus consumed versus booked business, split credit, refunds/adjustments, accelerators and caps explicit. Obtain credit and accounting definitions from the owners. A proposed model does not authorize changing a plan or promising compensation.

When rewarding both lead creation and closed business, define the two events separately. Require a sourced lead to be accepted against stated evidence, and a closing reward to use the approved booked/recognized event. Specify duplicate, self-generated, already-owned and disputed claims. Model separate stage caps and any combined cap. Decide explicitly whether one reward offsets another.

Test low, at-target and high attainment, one outsized deal, low-margin business, product imbalance, partner resale, shared credit, cancellation and a timing shift. Use the actual plan's review authority for windfalls and exceptions. Compare payout, retained contribution and behavioral incentive, including situations where a rep can earn more while the company is worse off. Sales and Finance should independently calculate the same pay from the same transaction record before approval.

## Make brand and customer-service choices explicit

For a brand decision, compare keeping separate brands, moving to one brand, a transitional combination and assigning brands to different customer segments or channels. Use actual customer recognition, trust, demand, product fit, cost and confusion evidence. State the strongest keep case and the evidence that would reverse it.

Choose legal, brand and system-conversion timing separately, then plan coexistence. Record customer documents, websites, portals, product names, service contacts, partner materials and contractual dependencies that change. A new brand needs a working service path while legacy platforms remain. Marketing, Legal and Communications approve the actual brand, rights and message. A banking example or fixed delay does not determine the schedule.

For Customer Success, compare onboarding, adoption, useful consumption, renewal and advocacy needs by customer segment. Model workload, skills, cost and customer-side realized value alongside internal efficiency. Select a bounded launch population using customer need and available service capacity, then require both customer benefit and viable operations before expansion. A staffing ratio or internal efficiency improvement alone does not establish the right coverage.

For support, compare prevention versus recovery, tiered escalation versus shared specialist response, fragmented versus unified ownership, standard versus tailored service, assisted versus self-service contact and the influence support needs over product decisions. Choose by actual failures, customer preference, quality, capacity and economics. Automation is one candidate requiring proof.

If an outcome-contingent fee is considered, define the customer's accepted outcome, measurement population/window, baseline, attribution limits, obligations of both parties and economic downside. Compare it with a conventional fee under the same service assumptions. Commercial, Finance and Legal owners decide whether the terms and risk are acceptable. Give cross-product and third-party solutions one accepted contact and escalation path while technical owners retain architecture and recovery decisions.

## Deliver a joined decision and check it

Use one record linking the customer/offer choice, accepted account population, coverage and capacity, lead-to-delivery rights, incentive scenario, brand/service transition, actual approvers, unresolved conditions and next outcome review. Preserve rejected alternatives and the condition that changes the recommendation. Do not call a milestone plan coherent until the dependent owners accept the same assumptions.

The following arithmetic is a synthetic capacity check, not a forecast. Ten existing accounts need four hours each during the period, and twelve optional cross-sell opportunities need five hours each. Required work is `10 × 4 + 12 × 5 = 100 hours` against 80 qualified hours available. Preserving existing coverage leaves `80 − 40 = 40 hours`, enough for eight opportunities under these assumptions. Prioritize those eight, obtain another 20 qualified hours, reduce accepted scope or change the timing. A higher bookings target does not close the gap.

Replace the inputs with observed work, productive capacity and uncertain ranges. Test peak timing, specialist availability and service quality before accepting the revised plan. Inspect an actual routed opportunity, disputed credit and customer handoff after launch. Record retained contribution and customer continuity separately from activity and attributed pipeline.
