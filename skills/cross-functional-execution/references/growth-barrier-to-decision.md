# Remove a growth barrier without a natural owner

Use this procedure when growth is blocked across functions and no existing owner can deliver the whole outcome. Produce an evidence-backed intervention, take temporary ownership within an explicit mandate and verify that customers can use the result. A new tracker is insufficient.

The strongest objection to central intervention is that the symptom may belong to a staffed function whose existing plan already solves it. Test that explanation first. Use [delivery and decisions](delivery-and-decisions.md) for authority, dependencies and handover. This procedure adds selection among competing growth barriers and an executable temporary assignment. All case inputs and outcomes below are synthetic.

## Separate the symptom from the mechanism

Start with a reconciled customer or work population and the business result at risk over a stated horizon. Compare at least the plausible explanations supported by the evidence: insufficient relevant demand, unsupported product need, an operating handoff failure or unavailable delivery capacity. Interview the participants in the same failed work episode and inspect the underlying records. Record contradictions rather than choosing the most senior explanation.

For each proposed barrier, identify the stuck transition, affected customers, evidence the intervention can change it, retained contribution, lag, required work and owner. Compare the strongest alternative and continued operation. Remove overlapping customers/benefits before ranking. Treat pipeline value as exposure, not guaranteed benefit. A large addressable market cannot select an immediate intervention.

Apply mandatory customer, service and specialist constraints before discretionary returns. Compare common-horizon contribution and feasible capacity without converting uncertain benefits into false precision. When the decisive unknown can be resolved cheaply, take a bounded evidence step and specify the decision it changes.

## Take a bounded operating assignment

Ask the actual sponsor to resolve the ownership gap, not to grant unspecified authority over all functions. Record what the operator can build, change or require, resources committed, decisions retained by functional owners, expiry/transfer condition and escalation recipient. If authority is missing, deliver the reversible analysis and test fixtures while seeking the concrete decision. Do not manufacture consent.

Once delegated, perform the missing work: reconstruct the failing handoff, reconcile inputs, prototype the repair, run acceptance cases, coordinate deployment and inspect the first customer use. Request precise specialist inputs when necessary. Asking each function to propose a plan leaves the original gap intact.

Escalate a resource conflict with a choice: named work displaced, bounded scope, moved date or funded capacity, plus the latest useful decision time. The sponsor can resolve priorities only within actual resource rights. Record the resource owner's commitment and update the existing plan. An approved memo without a changed work queue is unimplemented.

## Completed synthetic decision

A unit's next ten-workday launch window contains 12 qualified accounts that have not onboarded. Sales calls this a demand problem. Reviewing all 12 records with Product and Operations finds eight supported customers unable to pass a data handoff and four whose willingness to buy remains unknown. Two older rejected customers have unsupported product requirements and are outside this population. Repairing the handoff cannot recover them.

| Candidate | Evidence and 90-day assumption | Work and contribution after incremental cost |
|---|---|---|
| Repair data handoff | Eight supported customers are stuck at the same reproducible failure. Scenario: six retain useful paid use. | 30 Engineering hours at $80, 12 operator hours at $50 and eight temporary assists at two hours × $50. `6 × $1,200 − $2,400 − $600 − $800 = $3,400` |
| Add demand campaign | The four remaining accounts lack buying evidence. A separate comparable audience could yield three additional retained accounts. | 20 operator hours at $50 plus $2,000 campaign cash. `3 × $1,200 − $1,000 − $2,000 = $600` |
| Change unsupported product scope | The two older rejected customers require an uncommitted capability. No credible 90-day retention assumption exists. | Not a forecastable substitute for the repair. Product investigates separately without adding these customers to the benefit case. |

The contribution per retained account excludes the intervention costs listed in the table. Labor rates are economic capacity costs, not claims that payroll falls. Spending authority and the cash budget are checked separately. Six and three are scenario inputs, not observed conversion rates. Neither candidate's assumptions can establish a company-wide ranking without the stated evidence.

Engineering has 48 suitable hours in this window. An existing service repair consumes 16 hours and cannot move without breaking an accepted commitment. Thirty-two hours remain. The handoff repair fits at 30 hours with two hours spare, conditional on the Engineering lead's estimate and availability. The operator has 28 hours: 12 for coordination/repair and 16 for the eight temporary assists. The campaign cannot be added to that same operator's plan. The sponsor chooses the repair and explicitly defers the campaign, losing its conditional $600 contribution and later demand evidence.

The delegated operator owns the missing interface through the first accepted customer cohort. They may reconcile the permitted inputs, build the mapping fixture, operate an approved manual fallback and maintain the integrated decision record. Engineering owns code and technical acceptance. Product accepts the supported task. Operations accepts the recurring queue. Data/security owners retain permission and control decisions. The operator cannot change customer rights or promise unsupported work.

On workday 1, the operator reproduces the error using permitted test data and builds the failing mapping fixture. On day 2, Engineering commits the 30 hours and Operations confirms fallback coverage in their existing work plans. Engineering and the operator test the repaired mapping against the eight eligible cases and a rejected unsupported case. The unsupported case stays rejected rather than being forced through a permissive rule.

The customer window ends on day 10. The approved manual fallback requires four days and resource confirmation can take two days. Therefore the latest useful escalation is day `10 − 4 − 2 = 4`. If committed repair evidence is absent on day 4, the sponsor must select fallback, narrower scope or changed timing by day 6. Waiting for a day-7 status meeting loses the fallback. Evidence checks follow this clock, not a universal meeting frequency.

## Verify removal and transfer ownership

In the synthetic run, Engineering deploys the accepted fix on day 5. All eight supported customers pass the corrected handoff, seven reach first useful task completion and six retain paid use through day 90. The eighth passes setup but does not use the task. Record setup success, first value and retention separately. Actual repair effort is 30 Engineering and 12 operator hours. Only four manual assists were needed, totaling eight hours. Actual intervention cost is `$2,400 + $600 + $400 = $3,400`, versus the original $3,800 scenario.

The scenario contribution is now `6 × $1,200 − $3,400 = $3,800`, but the causal claim remains limited. The error logs establish that the repair removed the technical block. Some customers might eventually have onboarded through ordinary support. If two retained accounts would have done so within the same horizon, estimated incremental contribution falls to `4 × $1,200 − $3,400 = $1,400`. At only two incremental retained accounts it is −$1,000. The economic decision breaks even at three incremental retained accounts under this actual cost. Request the comparison evidence that distinguishes those cases rather than claiming all six as created growth.

The original proposal's $3,800 cost requires four incremental retained accounts to clear cost. Thirty available Engineering hours are the feasibility threshold under the original work estimate. Below that, revise scope or use the accepted fallback. Raising the growth target does not create the missing hours.

Operations accepts the repaired mapping, access, exception reasons and queue ownership. The operator observes its next normal and rejected case before ending the temporary assignment. The same initiative ID carries the barrier evidence, resource decision, customer outcomes and later [value check](../../financial-resource-decisions/references/value-realization.md). Reopen the work if the error recurs, service deteriorates or supported customers still fail first value. Closing the engineering task alone does not establish barrier removal.
