# Transfer a scaling method without erasing local advantage

Use this procedure after a business has demonstrated a useful operating change and another unit wants to reuse it. Decide what mechanism transfers, what must change and what should remain local. One successful rollout establishes a candidate pattern, not a universal playbook.

The strongest case against reuse is that the original result depended on a buyer population, product, authority or scarce team absent in the next unit. Copying the visible process can preserve its cost while losing its benefit. Compare reuse with local repair, a smaller shared interface and no change. This is original operating guidance. Every completed example below is synthetic.

Use [parent and portfolio value](parent-and-portfolio-value.md) for the contribution of sharing. Apply [operating-model design](../../organization-and-change/references/operating-model-design.md) to capability and authority choices. Use [value realization](../../financial-resource-decisions/references/value-realization.md) for the fixed baseline and observed benefit. Retain one initiative and benefit system across these analyses.

## Extract the causal mechanism and its boundaries

Reconstruct actual work before documenting a method. Preserve the failed approach, population/exclusions, baseline, intervention, evidence of changed behavior, implementation effort, costs, customer outcome and rival explanations. Separate required mechanism from incidental details such as the original tool or meeting name. Record rejected cases so the playbook describes where it fails as well as where it worked.

For every proposed receiving unit, compare the conditions that could change the result:

| Condition | Transfer question |
|---|---|
| Customer problem and buying motion | Does the next population need the same task and make the same kind of purchase? |
| Capability and readiness | Is the offer live, accepted and supportable in the receiving context? |
| Access and commercial overlap | Do account/data rights, relationship ownership and competing offers permit the motion? |
| Resources and economics | Does receiving plus provider capacity fit, and does retained incremental contribution cover adaptation and ongoing cost? |
| Local source of advantage | Which product, talent, customer or decision practice must remain intact, and what evidence would show damage? |

Mark a condition supported, failed or unknown with its evidence. Do not average a failed permission or service condition into a passing score. The receiving owner selects reuse unchanged, adapt a bounded part, run a diagnostic, or decline. A parent team can steward evidence without acquiring the unit's product or resource authority.

## Separate shared interfaces from local decisions

For each asset or workstream, specify the minimum common interface, required enterprise obligation and retained local right. Shared account matching can coexist with local offer experiments. A common customer identifier need not require identical pricing, release timing or product roadmaps. Equally, local speed cannot waive an actual customer or specialist obligation.

Test the autonomy arrangement on a real priority conflict: a local experiment, a shared-account dispute and a provider capacity shortage. Record who decides, time to decision, work delayed and customer effect. Compare the same class of decision before and after transfer. Do not infer preserved autonomy from a charter alone or impose a universal speed target. If a new approval adds delay without protecting a demonstrated dependency, remove or narrow it within the actual owner's authority.

Make the next trial executable: named receiving and provider owners, permitted population, committed hours, accepted interface, local rights, fallback, decision deadline and observations that allow continuation. The operator owns the comparison, adapted method, dependency commitments and trial follow-through within mandate. Functional owners validate specialist evidence. The sponsor resolves the actual contested priority. Do not stop at circulating lessons.

## Completed synthetic transfer decision

A first unit's prior 90-day experiment used qualified relationship-owner introductions and a specialist handoff. Among 100 eligible customer groups offered the motion, 14 retain paid task use, versus eight of 100 randomly assigned to existing selling. Six incremental retained accounts at $800 contribution produce $4,800 before $2,000 setup and $1,200 recurring program cost, or $1,600 net. All outcome windows are mature. The method's candidate mechanism is qualified trusted access plus a working specialist handoff. A broader account blast in an earlier test had produced introductions without useful activation and is retained as counterevidence.

Two further units request the same method. The numbers below are explicit scenarios for their next 90 days, not extrapolated observations.

| Receiving unit | Boundary evidence | Adapted commitment and choice |
|---|---|---|
| B | Complementary supported task, account permission and relationship owners confirmed. Same buying motion, but a different connector and lower contribution. | 24 shared-specialist hours. Assumed five incremental retained accounts × $700 minus $1,000 adaptation and $1,200 recurring cost = $1,300. Authorize bounded adapted trial within delegated rights. |
| C | Same customer logos but a competing offer and unproved task acceptance. The selling team requests a roadmap commitment. | Full rollout would need 40 specialist hours and has no supported benefit estimate. Decline rollout. Authorize a separate 12-hour customer/task diagnostic only if the resource owner accepts the displaced work. No growth forecast attaches to it. |

The shared team has 60 qualified hours after existing work. Simultaneous B and full C need `24 + 40 = 64`, so even a hypothetical positive C case cannot fit. B plus the bounded C diagnostic needs 36, leaving 24 hours available for existing uncertainty or another accepted use. These hours are capacity, not cash savings. Record who reserves them and prevent double booking in the provider's own plan.

B's sponsor accepts the local Product lead's authority over experiments within supported scope. Shared Sales retains customer-contact and credit decisions. The connector team accepts its interface and 24-hour reservation. The operator tests one complete handoff and one disputed-account rejection before exposure, then observes all accepted implementations. C's Product/commercial owners review the diagnostic and can decline the joint offer without manufacturing an integration milestone.

## Update the pattern from the next real use

In B's synthetic trial, 100 groups receive the motion and 100 retain existing selling, assigned by customer group. Thirteen versus nine retain paid task use at day 90: an observed difference of four, with small-sample uncertainty retained. Actual adaptation costs $1,200 and recurring program cost is $1,300. Estimated net contribution is `4 × $700 − $1,200 − $1,300 = $300`, compared with the original $1,300 forecast. The bridge is −$700 from one fewer incremental retained account, −$200 adaptation and −$100 recurring cost. Do not change the baseline to preserve the target.

The tested local experiment class previously had a median two-workday decision time. During B's initial trial it rises to five because local scope changes enter the parent's review queue. The accepted requirement for this class was no later than three workdays, because the next customer test needs that decision. The trial misses its autonomy condition despite positive contribution. Inspect the actual delayed decisions and customer effect, not only the median. Existing enterprise obligations still apply.

The authorized sponsor narrows parent review to changes affecting shared customer claims or delivery commitments. Local supported experiments return to the existing Product route. The operator then observes the next two eligible decisions: both complete within two days with the required owners, and one genuine shared-claim exception still reaches parent review. That verifies operation for these three cases, not permanent autonomy or a broad statistical conclusion. Expansion remains bounded until service, repeat economics and decision behavior hold over an appropriate observation window.

B needs four incremental retained accounts to clear its $2,500 actual cost. One fewer makes net contribution −$400. A repeat cohort with no adaptation cost needs two incremental retained accounts to clear $1,300 recurring cost, conditional on unchanged demand and work. Neither threshold overrides a failed permission or customer requirement. At provider availability below 24 hours, the original B commitment must narrow or move. At 64 hours, capacity alone no longer blocks simultaneous full B/C rollout, but C's failed proposition/readiness evidence still does.

C's diagnostic finds customers prefer the existing offer and no distinct accepted task for the unit's proposed add-on. Record a supported no-rollout decision and the 12 hours consumed. The useful portable lesson is to require task complementarity before sales activation, not that every shared customer is an opportunity.

Promote the pattern with explicit conditions: qualified access, accepted complementary task, live handoff, feasible capacity and bounded shared approvals. Preserve both receiving-unit results and all failed conditions. A later unit must run the same comparison rather than inherit B's approval. Repeated transfer decisions, including declines and repairs, are the scaling evidence. The number of playbooks written is an activity count.
