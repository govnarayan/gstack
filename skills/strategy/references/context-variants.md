# Context variants

Company size is a starting point. The decision's scope, authority, product maturity, commercial motion, and uncertainty determine the actual analysis.

| Context | Emphasis | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Under 20 or finding product-market fit | Customer learning, which hypothesis matters now, short cash/capacity implications, reversible tests, founder decisions. | A multi-layer strategy/goal system before it solves an observed problem. |
| 20–60 | A shared strategic starting point, a few coordinated priorities, explicit foundations, and delegated ownership. | Assuming direct conversation alone resolves repeated cross-functional conflict, or requiring separate documents for strategy, priorities, and ongoing operations. |
| 61–300 | Team-level choices, manager interfaces, staffing/resource dependencies, and reconciliation of conflicting incentives. | Requiring every team to restate the same company goal or losing functional operating obligations. |
| 301–5,000 | Business and product portfolios, multi-layer resource decisions, capability build, and strategy-to-plan consistency. | Adding detail without clarifying decision authority and the level of the strategic choice. |
| 5,001+ or public company | The unit's contribution to wider company direction, portfolio trade-offs, sponsor/decision forums, and interfaces with established functions. | Acting as if the operator controls company strategy or owns Finance, Legal, or Investor Relations. |

For frontier AI labs, examine the uncertainty of research progress, evaluation evidence, compute and talent dependencies, and the authority governing deployment. Do not assume capability milestones eliminate calendar planning for every function.

For AI product businesses, distinguish customer value and adoption from model capability gains. Model/provider changes can alter quality, cost, differentiation, and release assumptions. Evaluate these effects together rather than treating a new model as an automatic strategy change.

For AI infrastructure or physical systems, connect demand, capacity, supply/lead times, utilization, reliability, and cost with commercial commitments. Near-term experiments and long asset commitments can require different horizons within the same company.

For traditional SaaS, distinguish enterprise, self-service, consumer, and platform motions. Do not import enterprise sales assumptions into a product-led business or treat SaaS benchmarks as company-specific targets.
