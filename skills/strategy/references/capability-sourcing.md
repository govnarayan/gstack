# Build, buy, partner, or combine

Use this method after defining the strategic outcome and the resource gap. A mode preference such as "we build everything" or "acquire to move faster" is not a capability analysis. Buying a vendor product and buying its company are different options. Partnering can mean a license, contract, distribution agreement, co-development, alliance, or joint venture, each with different rights and operating obligations.

Capron and Mitchell's resource-pathways method asks whether internal resources support development, an effective external relationship can provide the needed resources, or broad/deep access requires acquisition. The operating test below adds explicit timing, control, scarcity, economics, and execution evidence. It does not prescribe a universal order, weighting system, or automatic acquisition rule.

Sources: [INSEAD's interview with Laurence Capron](https://knowledge.insead.edu/strategy/corporate-growing-pains-build-borrow-or-buy) and [Capron and Mitchell, Resource Pathways Framework, publisher synopsis](https://hbsp.harvard.edu/product/9497BC-PDF-ENG). These support the sourcing distinctions. The following decision procedure and examples are independent application guidance.

## Define the capability and minimum viable rights

Specify what the business must be able to do, for whom, by when, at what service/quality level, and at what volume. Distinguish a technology, operating routine, scarce team, brand, distribution right, data right, license, customer relationship, or integrated bundle of resources. Measure the internal gap against the required capability, not the presence of a similarly named department.

List minimum rights over roadmap priority, changes, IP, data, audit and quality repairs. Include exclusivity, pricing, geographic scope, customer relationships, continuity and exit. Map each right to its business reason and acceptable substitute. Ownership can provide legal control while failing to retain the people or routines that create value. A commercial contract can provide sufficient control without ownership. Specialist owners validate actual legal and regulatory conditions for a live transaction.

## Compare on evidence before assigning preference

| Dimension | Build | Contract or partner | Acquire a business/capability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time to usable value | Recruiting, ramp, development, validation, approval and deployment | Search, agreement, configuration, integration, validation and adoption | Search, diligence, approval, closing, retention, integration and validation |
| Required control | Can the internal roadmap and budget supply it? | Can enforceable rights supply the required decisions and continuity? | Does ownership improve the specific right, and will integration preserve it? |
| Scarcity and transferability | Are relevant resources available to hire/develop in time? | Are resources tradable, licensable or accessible through collaboration? | Is the desired capability embedded in people, relationships or routines that can survive acquisition? |
| Economic exposure | Build cost, delay, maintenance, opportunity cost and scale | Fees, minimum commitments, share of upside, switching and dependency costs | Price, transaction cost, integration, retention, incremental capital and lost flexibility |
| Ability to execute | Evidence of building this type of capability, with available team | Evidence both parties can operate the interface and manage conflicting incentives | Evidence of integration capacity, authority, resource ownership and a credible retention plan |
| Learning and reversibility | Can development be staged and abandoned? | Can a bounded contract test value and preserve an alternative? | What is irreversible, and can the desired outcome be achieved without buying the entire bundle? |

Rarity matters only if the scarce resource is necessary for the advantage and the selected route can secure it. A rare model, dataset, specialist, manufacturing process, certification, or exclusive channel is not equally transferable. Record alternatives to the named resource. Paying for rarity without a causal link to customer value can destroy returns.

Degree of control is not a low/medium/high feeling. Specify who can change the roadmap, stop service, use the data, serve a competitor, or deny access, then test the proposed rights under a conflict. Exclusivity may remove a rival channel and reduce the partner's willingness to invest. Compare the value gained with that opportunity cost.

Speed means time to accepted customer value, not signing, closing or a demo. Identify the latest useful date and the critical path under each route. Add the scarce implementation work and downside timing, not an arbitrary universal integration buffer. A nominally quick acquisition may arrive too late after closing and integration. A quick partner contract can still fail on product acceptance.

## Apply constraints before weighted trade-offs

Reject or redesign options that cannot meet mandatory rights, quality, timing, cash or staffing constraints. Keep the failed criterion and the evidence visible. If no option passes, change the promise, stage the outcome, find an alternative, or stop. Do not let a high financial score cancel a failed required control.

For feasible options, compare common-horizon cash and contribution under explicit lag, adoption, retention and ongoing cost assumptions. Separate strategic benefit from speculative synergy. Include execution probability only if supported by relevant evidence, and keep scenario-specific results rather than averaging them into false precision.

If a weighted decision table helps expose preferences, agree weights and scoring meanings with the actual decision owner. Show sensitivity and any veto criteria. The weights are local choices, not a property of the framework. For a decisive two-option case, direct break-even analysis is usually clearer.

Consider a combination: partner now while building a differentiating layer, buy a narrow license rather than the company, or contract for a learning stage before deciding on acquisition. Give every transition a trigger, cost, retained rights and feasible exit. "Partner then acquire" is not a plan when no purchase right, price mechanism or willing counterparty exists.

## Worked case: scarce decision engine for a new offer

A synthetic company needs a specialized decision engine available by month four. It must retain customer-data rights and final release authority. There are 20 scarce implementation weeks available. The comparison horizon is 24 months. Every quantity is an assumed scenario, in millions of dollars where monetary. Benefits are monthly contribution before route-specific costs, not revenue or cash receipts.

| Route | First usable month | Upfront cost | Monthly benefit after usable | Monthly route cost after usable | Implementation weeks | Rights and capability evidence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Build | 9 | 1.2 | 0.30 | 0.06 | 24 | Full required rights, but recruiting and validation miss deadline and capacity |
| Partner | 3 | 0.4 | 0.30 | 0.14 | 8 | Draft terms preserve data and release authority. Partner can supply the scarce engine |
| Acquire | 6 | 4.0 | 0.34 | 0.08 | 20 | Rights available in principle, but close/integration miss required date and retention unproved |

There are 16, 22 and 19 active months respectively when the first usable month is included through month 24. The undiscounted net contribution proxies are:

1. Build: 16 × (0.30 - 0.06) - 1.2 = 2.64.
2. Partner: 22 × (0.30 - 0.14) - 0.4 = 3.12.
3. Acquire: 19 × (0.34 - 0.08) - 4.0 = 0.94.

Partner is the only feasible route under the stipulated timing and staffing limits. Recommend a bounded partner implementation conditional on validated quality, continuity and enforceable required rights. Do not describe draft terms as signed. Acquisition's extra assumed monthly benefit does not rescue its missed date or unproved retention. The scarce engine is necessary for the offer, but scarcity alone does not justify buying the supplier.

The strongest objection is dependency: the partner could change pricing or deny roadmap access. Set a price/renewal cap or accepted renegotiation trigger, data portability, service recovery and exit-assistance evidence before commitment. Have technical and commercial owners test the interface and downstream customer outcome. Continue only under the actual delegated spending and agreement authority.

### Reversal thresholds and limits

If the deadline can move to month nine, build capacity can rise from 20 to 24 weeks, and every other assumption holds, build becomes feasible. Partner's monthly route cost breaks even with build at 0.161818 million: 22 × (0.30 - cost) - 0.4 = 2.64. Above that cost, build wins on this narrow contribution proxy. Under the original mandatory month-four deadline, that price threshold does not make an infeasible build option executable.

If mandatory control cannot be obtained from the partner, no original route passes. The correct output is a changed promise or stopped launch, not an invented permission to use customer data. If acquisition becomes feasible, compare its upfront price with the partner proxy using the same timing and benefit. The break-even price is 19 × 0.26 - 3.12 = 1.82 million before transaction and integration costs. That is a scenario break-even, not a valuation or offer price. Discounting, risk, later benefits, taxes and financing would require the [financial resource skill](../../financial-resource-decisions/SKILL.md).

## Decision and implementation record

Deliver the gap, required rights and date, options including do less/stop, feasible set, comparative economics, decision owner, strongest objection, thresholds, and conditions still unproved. Record the counterparty or build team, approvals, accepted resources and validation dataset. Name who accepts release, owns the interface and handles exceptions. Specify the continuity or exit test and what triggers review.

At a startup, a broad operator may execute the delegated analysis and bounded implementation. At a public company or staffed business unit, use the relevant Engineering, Product, Procurement, Finance, Legal and corporate-development owners. The strategy deliverable is complete when the sourcing decision and implementation conditions are reviewable. Preparing it does not confer agreement, investment or transaction authority.

## Decide which rights the company actually needs

Write two versions of the investment case. In one, a contract secures the required rights. In the other, ownership supplies something the contract cannot guarantee.

[Microsoft and LinkedIn](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2016/06/13/microsoft-to-acquire-linkedin/) retained LinkedIn's brand and independence and named its reporting line. Specify which operating rights the buyer will exercise after closing. Charge the purchase case for any integration that exercising those rights requires.

[Google Cloud and Spotify](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/gcp/spotify-chooses-google-cloud-platform-to-power-data-infrastructure) describe moving infrastructure to a supplier at considerable scale. Ask who controls the customer experience, who bears migration costs, and what breaks if the supplier leaves. Neither party disclosed the contract economics in that announcement.

Keep a usable standalone plan until approvals arrive. [Adobe and Figma](https://news.adobe.com/news/news-details/2023/adobe-and-figma-mutually-agree-to-terminate-merger-agreement) abandoned their merger after concluding they lacked a clear path to regulatory approvals. For a proposed acquisition, put the last viable date for a partnership beside the expected closing date. Bring the choice back before that alternative expires.

## Price the delay on the same customer assumptions

When a team compares buying a product with building it, estimate when each route can first serve the same customer. Buffer's Respondly decision considered hiring, time to market and missed revenue. Its $100,000 monthly recurring revenue target was a model input. [Buffer's acquisition account](https://buffer.com/resources/buffer-acquires-respondly/).

Validate who would actually buy and who must sell or support the product. Buffer later closed Reply after finding the customer and sales work differed from its core product. [Buffer's retirement account](https://buffer.com/resources/sunsetting-reply/).
