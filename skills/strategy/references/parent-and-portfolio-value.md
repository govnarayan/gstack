# Parent and shared-service value

Use this procedure when deciding whether a corporate parent, shared service, common platform or portfolio arrangement makes its constituent businesses better off. The question is the contribution of the arrangement compared with a feasible alternative. Market growth and relative share do not answer it.

This is original operating guidance. A CoS or S&O operator can prepare the analysis and coordinate a trial within the assigned mandate. Unit leaders, service owners, Finance and the actual decision authority retain their responsibilities. A unit sponsor cannot authorize a group-wide restructuring by approving a local analysis.

## Define the counterfactual

Specify the businesses, activities, customers, period and decision being compared. Separate owning a business from sharing a particular service. Retaining ownership while changing one interface can be a credible alternative to a sale or reorganization.

Compare the current arrangement with feasible alternatives: local provision, an external supplier, selective sharing, a different parent or a narrower central mandate. Establish whether each alternative can actually supply the needed capability, rights, service and date. An imaginary independent business with free systems, capital and leadership is not a valid comparator.

When using a service-cost benchmark, choose peers with comparable work, sector requirements, volume, workforce and footprint. Define the cost and capacity denominators, exclusions and outliers before comparing. Explain a gap by the services actually required, then build the replacement cost from work, skills, systems and commitments. A peer median or historical allocation does not establish what this business can remove or must spend.

Record required obligations and minimum service outcomes before comparing discretionary benefits. Have the responsible owner confirm which constraints can change. An inexpensive arrangement that cannot meet an essential customer or control requirement is not feasible.

## Identify what the parent changes

Start with an activity and its recipient, rather than a claim such as scale or better governance. Investigate the mechanisms that could change this decision:

| Possible contribution | Evidence needed | Cost or damage to test |
|---|---|---|
| Shared specialist, platform or service | Same-quality work delivered with less duplicated effort or better coverage | Queues, integration work, loss of useful local variation and service-owner capacity |
| Pooled purchasing or access | Comparable offers, actual rights and achievable terms at the relevant volume | Minimum commitments, switching costs, reduced supplier choice and unused capacity |
| Customer, channel or capability transfer | A recipient can use the resource, customers value it, and delivery capacity exists | Channel conflict, cannibalization, transfer effort and disruption to the source business |
| Oversight, learning or risk coverage | A specific decision or failure changes through an observable intervention | Extra approvals, reporting effort, delayed decisions and weakened local accountability |
| Capital or portfolio flexibility | A supported change in feasible investment, exposure or ability to redeploy resources | Restrictions, parent-imposed priorities, irreversible commitments and alternatives forgone |

These are investigation prompts, not benefits to add automatically. Identify the strongest case for keeping the present arrangement before proposing a change. A weak unit result can reflect demand, execution or capability rather than a damaging parent. Conversely, a profitable unit can still bear unnecessary coordination costs.

## Build one value ledger

For each material contribution, retain:

1. The activity, recipient, provider, actual owner and comparator.
2. The causal change, baseline, proposed result, period, units and supporting evidence.
3. Incremental receipts, avoidable cash outlays, scarce capacity or service effects, kept in separate columns.
4. Parent/service costs, transition and double-running costs, coordination work, and lost local value or flexibility.
5. Overlap with other claims, evidence still missing, acceptance owner and the condition that changes the choice.

Show both unit and group effects. A lower internal charge can improve a unit's reported result without releasing any group cash. Eliminate transfers when consolidating. Keep existing unavoidable overhead separate from costs that the decision changes. State which costs cease, which move and which remain stranded.

Do not count released employee time as payroll savings unless a supported change in cash outlay follows. Show usable capacity, its timing and the work it could replace. If valuing a displaced activity economically, identify the feasible alternative and avoid also claiming its benefit elsewhere. Lost customer value, slower decisions and reduced autonomy need a causal observation or an explicit unresolved test, rather than an invented monetary score.

Calculate the difference between alternatives over the same horizon and service boundary. Supported incremental benefits less incremental operating, coordination, transition and adverse effects give the economic contribution under those assumptions. Present a separate cash view. Use Finance's timing and discounting conventions when those materially change the decision. Do not average money, hours and service scores into a single total.

## Compare portfolio paths and investment purposes

Compare a concentrated move with a sequence of smaller moves when both could reach the desired end state. Show time to usable capability, unwanted assets or obligations acquired with it, cumulative implementation and management load, transition cost, learning and reversibility. Test the whole sequence against capacity by period. Several individually affordable moves can require the same integration team at the same time.

For each path, record the first commitment, what it enables, conditional follow-ons and the end state. Reassess the remaining candidates after the first move: a previously weak option may become useful because a prerequisite is now present, while another may duplicate the acquired capability. Name the evidence and resource change that would cause re-ranking. A static ranked list cannot represent these dependencies.

Keep investment purpose separate from distance from today's business and from maturity. Work needed to maintain a service, improve productivity, grow an established offer and learn about a future opportunity can require different proof and protection. A new market can be served by a mature capability, and a core product can need uncertain research. Reconcile all demands against the same cash, skills and management capacity.

Explain why the mix should change with the actual transition phase or new evidence. An urgent capability replacement may temporarily displace exploration, but record the learning postponed and the condition for reopening it. Set no universal percentage. Required work needs service and risk evidence. Productivity needs realized cash or usable capacity. Growth needs incremental customer economics. Exploration needs a bounded question and next commitment.

Test the proposed portfolio under shared demand, capacity or market changes. Inspect which businesses fail together, what can be redeployed, and whether another owner or arrangement could use a capability better. Preserve economically useful options only when the rights, resources and time to exercise them exist. This tests the combination's resilience without turning a private portfolio diagram into a mandatory checklist.

## Test the arrangement on actual work

Replay a normal request, a peak or exception, and a disputed priority across the boundary. Record who decides, what capacity is available, what waits, and which customer or service consequence changes. Inspect actual approval and handoff times rather than inferring coordination cost from the organization chart.

For a trial, specify the service promise, retained local rights, provider and accepting owners, access, capacity, escalation, evidence window and fallback. Verify whether shared capability became usable and whether displaced local work or service quality worsened. A signed service agreement or new reporting line is design evidence, not a measured benefit.

Recommend retaining, repairing, narrowing, expanding or ending the arrangement only within actual authority. State the strongest alternative, net contribution and cash effect, constraints, unproved benefits, transition burden and reversal condition. If another owner might create more value, distinguish that hypothesis from a supported valuation or executable transaction.

## Compact completed record

All inputs below are synthetic assumptions for a one-year, undiscounted comparison. They demonstrate the ledger and are not benchmarks.

Two units currently buy equivalent services for $80,000 and $120,000 a year. An available shared offer costs $150,000, with $20,000 of additional annual central cash cost and $15,000 of transition cash cost. Coordination needs 100 scarce staff hours. The owner has 120 usable hours available and values the displaced alternative work at $100 an hour. That opportunity cost does not reduce payroll.

| Record | Completed result |
|---|---|
| Comparator | Continue the two purchases: $200,000 annual cash outlay for the defined service |
| Shared first-year cash | $150,000 + $20,000 + $15,000 = $185,000. Cash improvement is $15,000. |
| Economic effect | $15,000 cash improvement minus $10,000 displaced-work value = $5,000 first-year benefit |
| Recurring effect | $30,000 cash improvement and $20,000 economic benefit before any future change in demand or service |
| Capacity | 100 required hours fit 120 available. Below 100 available hours the proposed arrangement needs redesign. |
| Decision | Trial the shared service conditional on equal service acceptance, available coordination capacity and supplier terms validated by the responsible owners |
| Strongest objection | Common delivery may impair a unit's local customer promise. The monetary comparison cannot waive that requirement. |
| Reversal | First-year economic benefit reaches zero if transition cost rises from $15,000 to $20,000, holding other assumptions fixed. A required service failure can reject the option earlier. |

Internal allocations of the $185,000 do not change the group result. Observed supplier invoices, transition spend, coordination effort and service outcomes must replace the assumptions before calling the benefit realized. Use the [value-realization procedure](../../financial-resource-decisions/references/value-realization.md) to retain that evidence and reconcile revisions.
