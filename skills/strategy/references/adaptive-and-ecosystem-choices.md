# Adaptive direction, ecosystem choices and innovation

Use the procedure that answers the unresolved question. A distant view needs a near-term resource decision. An ecosystem proposal needs willing participants and workable economics. An innovation search needs alternatives beyond adding features. These are different tasks, and none requires applying all three methods.

## Connect a future position to the next commitment

John Hagel and John Seely Brown's zoom out/zoom in approach connects a view of the future business with a few near-term initiatives, committed resources and learning that revises both. Their published horizons are 10–20 years and 6–12 months. Their proposal also gives less attention to intermediate years. Those are features of the source approach, not a reason to omit this company's financing, hiring, contractual or infrastructure commitments. [Primary source: Zoom out/zoom in](https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/insights/topics/business-strategy-growth/alternative-approach-to-building-a-strategic-plan-businesses.html).

Apply it to an actual choice:

1. Describe how customers, alternatives and the surrounding system could change, then state the position and capabilities the business would need. Retain contrary futures and evidence that would invalidate the working view.
2. Identify the next moves that build or test those capabilities. Compare their effect on the future position, present business, learning, cash and scarce capacity. Include work to stop so the chosen moves can be funded.
3. Obtain the actual resource owners' commitments. Do not conceal a portfolio inside one umbrella initiative. For a long project, define a meaningful near-term acceptance or learning result.
4. Compare observed results with the belief and the action. Decide whether to improve execution, change resources or revise direction. A successful milestone does not prove the future view.

Retain one linked decision record: future hypothesis, current evidence, required capability, chosen near move, rejected alternative, accepted resources, observation date and evidence that changes the choice. Use [planning](../../planning-and-goals/references/planning-methods.md) for intermediate obligations and [scenario response](../../cross-functional-execution/references/scenario-response.md) for distinct futures and contingent action.

Failure conditions include an attractive future with no funded action, adequate funding spread across too much work, a near-term result unrelated to the required capability, or repeated action changes that never challenge the underlying belief. A small startup's usable horizon and a public unit's planning commitments can differ. Choose them from the decision, not the source's calendar alone.

## Decide whether to shape an ecosystem

Hagel describes three connected elements: a view of how a market could create more value, a platform that lowers participation effort and improves participant returns, and actions or assets that make the organizer's commitment credible. It requires other organizations to act, beyond agreeing with a narrative. [Primary author: Shaping Markets and Shaping Psychology](https://www.johnhagel.com/shaping-markets-and-shaping-psychology/).

Specify the changed customer outcome and why a bilateral contract, internal build or existing platform would not achieve it as well. Identify the necessary participants, their alternatives, what they contribute and what they receive. Test both adoption cost and continuing incentive. A company cannot declare itself an ecosystem organizer because it has partners.

Define the smallest common interface, standard, practice or shared capability that makes participation easier. Record governance, change rights, data access, funding, customer ownership, dispute and exit requirements with the actual owners. Compare the organizer's retained value with participant economics. Dependence that makes entry attractive can still make continued participation unattractive.

Stage a credible commitment that fits authority and resources. Have participants perform their actual contribution and complete a customer outcome. Review entry effort, time to value, operating burden, retained economics and who still declines. Change the participation terms or sequence, narrow the ambition or stop when a required contribution cannot be secured. The [partnership procedure](../../commercial-strategy/references/partnership-operating-model.md) carries those reciprocal commitments into operation.

The decision output names the proposed change, necessary participants, exchange, shared mechanism, credible commitment, first test and rejection condition. A platform with no participants, a subsidized pilot with no continuing economics or an announcement without usable rights fails this test. A successful partner relationship alone does not establish an ecosystem effect or a durable competitive advantage.

## Generate innovation options beyond features

Doblin's Ten Types of Innovation distinguishes profit model, network, structure, process, product performance, product system, service, channel, brand and customer engagement. It supports examining combinations across the business rather than equating innovation with a new product. [Official method: Deloitte Digital, Ten Types of Innovation](https://www.deloittedigital.com/sea/en/capabilities/creative-experience-design/applied-design-innovation/ten-types.html).

Start with the observed customer or economic problem. Map how today's offer earns, uses other organizations and assets, operates, combines products, supports users, reaches them and establishes trust. Examine competitors using actual evidence. Missing evidence is not an empty market.

Generate materially different alternatives across those areas, including a combination where the changes reinforce each other. For each, explain customer value, changed activities, counterpart incentives, delivery and economic consequences. Test demand and feasibility before selecting the next commitment. Count neither categories nor novelty as value.

Deliver the alternatives, their mechanisms, selected combination, rejected option and decision-changing test. Reject a visually complete map that leaves the original problem unresolved. This is an option-generation lens, not proof of willingness to pay, imitation barriers or return. Use customer research and [the investment-to-release procedure](../../product-and-market/references/investment-to-release-case.md) to test the actual proposed change. Use the official source for its commercial materials. This procedure does not reproduce its diagram, tactics cards or company illustrations.

## Check whether strategy is understood and used

Before adding another planning document, separate three questions: do leaders mean the same thing by the direction, does the evidence support that direction, and do actual resource and operating decisions enact it? Agreement on a survey cannot answer all three.

Collect comparable interpretations from the people who must act, inspect conflicting work and resource decisions, and test the assumption whose failure most changes the choice. Calibrate a self-report against actual examples. Then decide whether the problem is the strategic choice, its communication, resources, authority or implementation. Use the [operating-model procedure](../../organization-and-change/references/operating-model-design.md) when required capabilities and decisions cannot operate together.

Keep the current plan distinct from the capability to produce and revise plans. That capability includes usable evidence, actual decision roles, inquiry, supporting systems and cross-team integration. The output is an owned repair tested on the next real decision. A workshop, a polished narrative or a completed survey is not completion evidence.

## Completed decision record

This compact example uses synthetic assumptions, not observations about a company. A reporting service assumes future buyers will require exports that work across providers. Four engineer-weeks are available after existing service obligations. An export pilot and a dashboard each need three weeks. Two potential delivery partners are willing to test a common format but have not accepted production obligations.

| Question | Completed conditional decision |
|---|---|
| Future view to present action | Fund the three-week export pilot and defer the dashboard. Both require six weeks and do not fit four. The pilot tests the assumed portability requirement rather than merely adding a feature. |
| Shared prerequisite or contingent work | Retain export permissions and a named support owner under either candidate distribution strategy. Partner-specific automation waits until the partnership choice has evidence. |
| Ecosystem or ordinary partnership | Test one common format with both participants. Keep it a bounded interface pilot. No broader ecosystem claim is justified before participants perform and continuing economics work. |
| Innovation alternative | Compare a new dashboard with a portable product system plus partner-delivered service. Choose the latter only while the stated buyer requirement and feasible delivery support it. |
| Next evidence and reversal | Buyer acceptance examples must show a useful cross-provider workflow, both participants must complete their contribution, and the service owner must accept recovery work. If those fail, revisit the requirement and route before spending the remaining week. |

This record connects a hypothesis to a funded test and explicit alternatives. It supplies no market forecast, predicted lift or permission for a production rollout.
