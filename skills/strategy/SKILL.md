# Strategy

Help the accountable leader make a consequential choice. Start from the operating question, existing material, constraints, and decision rights. Distinguish an observed problem from the explanation for it. A goal, roadmap, or forecast can inform strategy without supplying the underlying choices.

## Build a coherent argument

Develop the diagnosis, plausible alternatives, chosen approach, and reinforcing actions. Explain the customer or stakeholder outcome, the economic mechanism, required capabilities, and what will wait or stop. Check whether the actions can coexist within capacity and whether one team's success undermines another's outcome.

Compare alternatives against the same dimensions. Include the strongest case for staying with the existing direction. State the recommendation, trade-offs, evidence, and what would change it. Avoid assigning probabilities or numerical scores without a defensible basis.

Read [choices and evidence](references/choices-and-evidence.md) when developing or challenging a strategic argument. Read [context variants](references/context-variants.md) when the question spans company sizes, business units, product maturity, or AI contexts.

## Select and apply a method

Use the [framework selector](../../frameworks/README.md) to match a method to the disputed question. Define that question before choosing a framework. A completeness check, an industry-profit analysis, a test of durable advantage, and a resource-allocation model answer different questions.

Produce the actual analysis: input evidence, assumptions, alternatives, applied method, resulting choice, and a condition that reverses it. Do not stop at naming a framework or suggesting a workshop. Mark missing inputs and show which ones determine the choice.

Use a small number of complementary methods where necessary. Test whether the recommendation stays coherent across customer value, competitive response, economics, capabilities, and execution constraints. Resolve a disagreement between methods rather than adding scores that use incompatible units. If a framework cannot change or test a decision, leave it out.

Read [growth and portfolio choices](references/growth-and-portfolio-choices.md) for Zook adjacency expansion, competing Playing to Win choices, and the limits of BCG growth-share allocation. It includes completed choices, capacity checks, and reversal thresholds. Read [capability sourcing](references/capability-sourcing.md) when choosing build, buy, partner or a combination. Compare time to usable value, required control, scarce and transferable capabilities, economics, and ability to execute before selecting a route.

Read [parent and portfolio value](references/parent-and-portfolio-value.md) when evaluating ownership, shared services, common platforms or a central mandate. Compare the arrangement with feasible alternatives, reconcile unit and group effects, and count coordination, transition and lost local value alongside supported benefits. Produce the value ledger and an operating trial rather than inferring parent value from a portfolio label.

Read [adaptive and ecosystem choices](references/adaptive-and-ecosystem-choices.md) when a future view needs a funded near-term move or a strategy depends on mobilizing other organizations. Also use it when innovation options are limited to features. It connects public zoom out/zoom in, shaping strategy and Ten Types mechanisms to actual choices, reciprocal commitments, tests and failure conditions. It also distinguishes shared understanding, evidence for the direction and its use in real decisions.

Use [scaling-pattern transfer](references/scaling-pattern-transfer.md) when applying a demonstrated operating change to another acquired business or unit. Test which mechanism and conditions transfer, what must adapt, what stays local and whether the next trial preserves customer value and local decision speed. A previous unit's result does not authorize the next rollout.

## Translate direction

Make the chosen approach usable by naming outcomes, scope, capability gaps, resource implications, dependencies, and boundaries. The level of detail should match the decision. A long-range narrative can carry uncertain later horizons while nearer commitments have clearer evidence and ownership.

Keep strategic assumptions separate from the current operating plan. Use annual or rolling plans to allocate resources within the direction. Use goals to measure selected progress. Do not manufacture a company objective for every function. A function can hold an ongoing mandate or longer-horizon work outside the immediate critical path.

## Review strategy

Ask whether the diagnosis and chosen approach remain valid. A missed target may reflect execution, an incorrect assumption, a bad measure, or a changed environment. A met target may coexist with a weakening business.

Identify the assumption whose failure most changes the decision, the evidence for and against it, the likely detection lag, and the practical response. Choose checkpoints from the pace of material change and cost of reversal. Do not impose a universal quarterly re-derivation or treat every new signal as grounds for a pivot.

## Research that changes the choice

Define the decision and the evidence needed for its disputed claims. Search current primary sources and the user's own material. Choose tools that provide the source access and coverage the question requires. Keep source date, population, company context, and method visible.

Search for counterevidence and analogous failures as well as supporting examples. Separate primary observation, reported experience, interpretation, and recommendation. A company case shows what happened in that context, not a general rule. Explain missing evidence and the decision sensitivity it creates.

## Deliverable standard

Produce the smallest artifact that makes the choice reviewable: decision, diagnosis, alternatives, recommendation, economics/resources, actions and owners, assumptions, and review triggers. Preserve unresolved disagreements. Where asked to improve an existing strategy, recap its argument before recommending changes and show which changes alter the choice versus clarify the expression.
