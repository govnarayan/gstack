# Organization choices and workforce planning

## Structure is one design variable

Map work as it runs today: desired outcome, recurrent outputs, decisions, handoffs, waiting, rework, and scarce capability. Compare the formal reporting structure with who actually controls priorities and resources. Establish whether a strategy change demands new coordination, or whether the current structure could work with clearer authority or better information.

Compare the written priorities with actual resource decisions and experienced leaders' accounts. Important assumptions may be unwritten. Validate differences with the sponsor and affected owners before turning them into a new direction. Translate an approved company choice into the contribution expected from each relevant unit or team, while labeling provisional interpretation. This does not require a cascade of goals or a fixed company-to-product hierarchy.

| Choice | Where it can help | Cost or failure to test |
|---|---|---|
| Functional grouping | Shared expertise, standards, and scarce specialists across related work | Cross-functional outcomes wait between queues or optimize against different objectives. |
| Product, customer, or segment grouping | Dedicated capacity and accountable outcomes where distinct work needs autonomy | Expertise and systems duplicate, shared resources become contested, and local results damage company economics. |
| Shared platform or service | Reuse, consistency, and reliable foundations across groups | Service owners lack capacity or incentives to meet consuming teams' needs. Explicit service commitments and prioritization are needed. |
| Matrix or hybrid | Preserve functional depth while supporting cross-functional outcomes | Two apparent bosses compete for the same capacity. Define final rights and escalation before adding dotted lines. |
| Temporary initiative team | Coordinate a bounded cross-functional outcome without permanent restructuring | A temporary owner becomes a shadow function, or borrowed staff lose their ongoing obligations. Define exit and return conditions. |

There is no automatic best structure for a funding stage. Compare a small change to a broader reorganization, including transition cost and what work pauses. Draw before-and-after responsibility and interface maps, not just boxes. Test an urgent exception as well as ordinary work. A structure that works only when everyone agrees has not resolved authority.

Test causes using actual work episodes before choosing among these forms. Retain the population and selection rule, timeline of active/waiting/rework time, conflicting priority decisions, demand versus usable capacity by scarce skill, and a rival explanation. Then replay the proposed design on the same case: who decides differently, what information or capacity changes, and which delay disappears? [The diagnosis and transition case](diagnosis-and-transition-case.md) shows a completed comparison, interface record, and observed trial rather than an empty organization template.

## Leadership and manager capacity

Count management work rather than applying a fixed span of direct reports: coaching, hiring, coordination, decisions, feedback, and the manager's own delivery responsibilities. Team experience, task novelty, time zones, interdependence, and vacancies change the load. Repeated missed feedback or decisions can indicate overload, but may also indicate poor management or unclear expectations.

Compare adding a layer with reducing scope, removing low-value coordination, delegating decisions, or strengthening team leads. Explain which constraint each option addresses. A promotion opportunity alone is not evidence for a management layer. Keep paths for expert contribution separate from management where the organization can support them.

Diagnose leadership work through the scope served, decisions owned, time horizon, and capability developed in others. Do not assign these from a Director or VP label. Direct intervention may be necessary in a crisis or unfamiliar work, but repeated rescue can prevent the responsible team from building capacity. Make intervention and the handback explicit. Standardize outputs only where someone needs to combine or rely on them, after checking which local variations serve legitimate needs.

When adding a senior leader, specify their outcomes and authority, what current owners retain, and how knowledge transfers. State how the sponsor reinforces the new boundary. Do not assume external prestige proves fit for this stage. Evaluate evidence of building and operating within the relevant constraints.

## Workforce plan

Translate business priorities and ongoing services into required work, capability, capacity, and timing before naming positions. For each proposed role or team, record:

1. The outcome or risk it addresses and evidence that capacity or capability is the constraint.
2. Alternatives: stop or simplify work, redeploy staff, improve tooling, contract for a bounded need, or hire.
3. Expected scope, manager, dependencies, hiring lead time, ramp time, and the capacity available before the hire contributes.
4. Cost assumptions reviewed with Finance, hiring feasibility reviewed with People and the functional owner, and the decision owner.

Show the cost and service impact of waiting as well as hiring. A budgeted requisition is not productive capacity, and a filled role is not a completed ramp. Use scenarios for uncertain demand rather than presenting one precise staffing figure as known.

Protect ongoing obligations explicitly when assigning people to launches or change. State the service level or work that must continue, who can approve a trade-off, and what happens if the same people are claimed twice. A staffing floor is one option to test, alongside service commitments, sequencing, temporary support, and a smaller launch scope.

## Useful outputs

Deliver a diagnosis and options memo, responsibility and interface map, workforce scenario table, or transition design as the problem requires. Every consequential change needs an owner, decision rights, resource implications, affected work, evidence of improvement, and a review condition. Do not add a complete People operating system to a narrow role-clarity request.
