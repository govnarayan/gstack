# Management support and change

## Role and hiring design

Support the hiring manager with a role scorecard: outcomes, scope, required capabilities, interfaces, and what good evidence looks like. Distinguish requirements from preferences. Assign distinct interview questions or work samples to the needed evidence. Give comparable candidates a consistent assessment, with scores and evidence recorded before group discussion to limit anchoring.

A debrief should end in a decision by the authorized owner or a specific remaining evidence request. Do not substitute personal similarity or vague culture fit for work-related behavior. Keep compensation, offer terms, background checks, and employment decisions with the responsible owner and current approved policies. This skill can support operational inputs without becoming the recruiting or HR function.

For a delegated startup recruiting process, make the pipeline visible enough to identify stalled stages, slow decisions, and candidate communication gaps. Use the existing recruiting system where staffed. Do not copy candidate records into a new generalist tracker without a purpose and appropriate access.

## Onboarding and management support

The manager owns role expectations and meaningful first work. Support a plan with tools and access, a named guide, company and customer context, current priorities, how decisions happen, key interfaces, and progressive responsibility. Choose check-in dates and deliverables based on the role's complexity and urgency. A standard 30/60/90 shape can help, but learning-only for a month and full scope at day 90 are not universal rules.

For manager 1:1s, prepare prior commitments in both directions, current blockers, feedback, support needs, and development topics. Keep status collection from consuming the whole conversation. For feedback or reviews, distinguish observed work, its impact, the expected standard, the person's explanation, and the support needed. Use specific examples and development actions rather than personality labels. Note when a review introduces feedback that should have been discussed earlier.

Calibration compares evidence against agreed role and level expectations. A distribution can prompt a question, but does not establish an individual's rating. Do not force outcomes to match a generic rating curve or automatically infer underperformance from a missed business outcome. Employment actions and formal improvement procedures follow People and Legal ownership and current policy.

## Culture as operating behavior

Compare stated values with the decisions, incentives, promotion criteria, and behavior leaders reward or tolerate. Translate an intended value into observable expectations and test it against a difficult real trade-off. Do not presume one founder's style or one function's habits should govern every team.

Use interviews, survey themes, and operating evidence together. Distinguish a few complaints from a broad pattern, and report missing coverage. Small samples can expose individuals despite removed names. Confirm what can be shared and by whom. Collect feedback only with a credible response owner, and report the action or reason for no change. A score without its population, questions, response pattern, and trend is weak evidence.

## Design and implement change

Define the business problem, sponsor, decision owner, affected groups, what is settled, what remains open, and the cost of delay. Compare retaining the current state, a limited trial, and the broader change where plausible. For each group, understand legitimate losses, concerns, capability gaps, and work displaced by the transition.

Distinguish understanding the reason, willingness to change, knowledge of the new way, practical ability, and reinforcement. Use these as diagnostic questions rather than a mandatory sequence. Someone who understands the announcement may still lack time, tools, confidence, or a reason to act differently. Apparent resistance can reveal a defective design.

Build the transition around the work: new responsibilities, unresolved interfaces, training and practice, temporary support, access changes, old arrangements being retired, and exceptions or rollback where feasible. For reorganization, prepare managers and affected people with People and Communications. Sensitive timing and disclosure follow the accountable owners' arrangements. Avoid a generic employee-first sequence that conflicts with actual governance requirements.

A change message explains why now, what was decided and by whom, and what it means for this group. State what remains uncertain, how concerns can affect implementation, and where help is available. Do not claim consultation can reverse a settled decision or pretend everyone benefits equally. Use the author's stated style and adapt detail and tone to the audience and channel.

## Measure whether it worked

Specify the baseline, business outcome, observable behavior, service protection, owner, and review trigger. Check actual use and exceptions, not merely training completion, announcement reach, or apparent compliance. Leaders must use the arrangement they ask others to adopt. Restore or revise it if the evidence shows it worsens the problem.

Maintain an inventory of concurrent changes and the capacity they draw from the same teams. Sequence or defer work when transition load exceeds available capacity. Do not declare every organization needs a fixed recovery period between changes. A crisis and a discretionary tool change impose different constraints.
