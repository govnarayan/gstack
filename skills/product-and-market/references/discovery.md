# Customer problems and discovery

## Choose the learning question

Discovery should resolve a decision that would otherwise consume resources under uncertainty. Separate these questions before choosing a method:

| Question | Suitable evidence | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Does this customer have an important problem? | Recent incidents, observed workarounds, time or money spent, switching attempts | Whether this particular solution works |
| Will they choose this offer? | Paid trial, accepted proposal, purchase, concrete commitment with conditions | Retention or repeatable acquisition |
| Can they use it? | Observed task completion, errors, assistance needed, time to value | Demand or viable economics |
| Can it be built and operated? | Engineering investigation, integration test, representative data, operating constraints | Customer value |
| Can the business sustain it? | Price, acquisition effort, cost to serve, support, risk, retention | Technical reliability or buyer acceptance |

Distinguish value, usability, feasibility, and business viability. Check ethics, distribution, strategy, and team assumptions when relevant. Use these as prompts for missing risks rather than a mandatory scoring exercise. In AI products, distinguish a demonstration from reliable performance on representative tasks. Synthetic examples can expose edge cases. They cannot establish what real customers want or will buy.

## Recruit and interview

Define eligibility in terms of the problem, buying situation, and actual behavior. Recruit across relevant users, budget holders, churned customers, unsuccessful trials, and buyers who chose alternatives. Record which groups were sought, reached, excluded, or unavailable. Friendly current customers are one part of the evidence, not a substitute for the addressable population.

Ask about a specific recent event: its trigger, actions, tools, participants, cost, and failures. Establish what changed afterward. Ask how buying and renewal decisions happen. Separate the problem interview from a pitch. Record permission where recording is involved, exact words as quotes, and summaries as paraphrases. Never fill missing interview fields with plausible invented details.

Choose a recruitment bound based on segment diversity and the consequence of the decision. Report new themes, contradiction, missing groups, and why additional interviews would or would not change the decision. Saturation within a narrow sample does not establish population prevalence.

## Synthesize without erasing disagreement

For each claim, retain participant or source ID, segment, date, exact supporting passage or observed event, and whether it is behavior, stated preference, or interpretation. Count independent people or accounts separately from repeated comments by one person. A theme appearing in many tickets may describe one broken account. Explain the denominator.

Code themes for situation, customer job, frequency, severity, current workaround, economic consequence, desired outcome, switching barrier, and buyer. Include functional, emotional, and social motivations when supported by evidence. Do not fabricate motivations to complete the categories.

Compare claims across usage, interviews, support, win/loss, and transaction data. Reviewers, support users, and active fans enter the sample through different mechanisms. Record those mechanisms before extrapolating. Preserve contrary evidence and outliers that could identify a distinct segment. Sentiment analysis and automated summaries are aids. Check consequential interpretations and quotes against raw material.

A useful synthesis states: who experiences what problem, in which situation, what they do today, cost or stakes, evidence count and denominator, conflicting observations, and what remains unknown. Separate research findings from the implication for a roadmap or positioning decision.

## Compare solution hypotheses and test assumptions

An outcome-to-opportunity-to-solution map can help when teams jump directly to features. Begin with the outcome and real customer obstacles. Compare materially different solutions, including a service/manual option, removing a step, a partner, and doing nothing when credible. Do not require an arbitrary number of branches merely to fill a tree.

For each candidate, name the assumption whose failure would make further investment wasteful. Rank tests by the decision impact of being wrong, current uncertainty, cost of delay, and cost of learning. A feature formula that rewards confidence should not deprioritize testing an uncertain assumption. Any numerical comparison needs consistent units and a clear explanation of what its score means.

Keep a narrow experiment cheap enough to discard, with an explicit decision about stopping, revising, or advancing it. Choose the artifact for the unknown. An engineering investigation tests feasibility, an observed task tests usability, and a paid concierge service tests a different set of assumptions. A narrative can test comprehension or stakeholder reaction. It cannot prove sustained demand. A prototype becomes a production commitment only through a separate decision on reliability, support, ownership, and controls.

An experiment record contains the hypothesis, target participants, method, exposure, measurable result, failure threshold, inconclusive range, guardrails, time/resource bound, and owner. Set decision thresholds before seeing results. Consider false positives, false negatives, and what the sample can detect. Small enterprise samples may support a bounded pilot decision while being insufficient to estimate a conversion lift. Do not turn a fixed number of respondents or days into proof of statistical reliability.

## Output and failure tests

Produce the research brief, interview guide, evidence table, problem synthesis, or experiment plan needed for the stated decision. Recommendations must point back to observed customer conditions and name the cheapest meaningful next test.

Rework analysis that treats compliments as commitment, demos as reliable task success, or requests as validated needs. Surveys need sampling limits, and simulated buyers are not customer evidence. Also rework it if all evidence comes from winners, conflicting segments were averaged, or success was defined after results arrived.

## Decide whose behavior earns the next release

[Rahul Vohra](https://blog.superhuman.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/) started with people who had used Superhuman. He separated users who would deeply miss it and studied which benefits they valued. For an early product, use that segment to choose what to protect. Then test whether the change helps adjacent users who value the same benefit.

[Hiten Shah](https://hitenism.com/slack-product-market-fit-survey/) surveyed Slack users who chose to participate. A reliance percentage only travels with its recruitment method and denominator. Record who never activated, who answered, and whether continued use or renewal corroborates the survey.

[Joel Gascoigne](https://buffer.com/resources/vp-product/) describes partners bringing Buffer signups that converted less often. Keep channel, activation, paid conversion and retained use on the same cohort. Reopen the channel decision if more signups buy less retained use per dollar spent.
