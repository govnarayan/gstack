# Screen opportunities and make diligence change the choice

Use this reference when a product or capability decision requires searching outside the company, comparing available opportunities and testing a proposed commitment. The result is an evidenced shortlist and a changed choice, scope, terms or implementation obligation. A long list, a numerical ranking and a completed question list are intermediate records.

The strongest argument against a broad search is its cost: it can consume the same scarce expertise needed to test a focused alternative. Set the search boundary and next commitment from the disputed decision. A narrowly defined capability, urgent deadline or known feasible supplier may justify a short search. An unclear requirement or a crowded set of materially different routes can justify wider discovery. State which missing evidence would change that bound.

## Agree the profile before scoring candidates

Begin with the customer or operating outcome, required capability, usable-by date, necessary rights/control, resource limit, value hypothesis and accountable sponsor. Compare internal work, external relationships, acquisition where relevant, and doing nothing through [capability sourcing](../../strategy/references/capability-sourcing.md). This reference supplies the search and evidence process for the chosen routes. It does not authorize a transaction.

Separate three dimensions:

| Dimension | What the record must establish |
|---|---|
| Mandatory fit | Requirements that cannot be traded away by a high score elsewhere, and who may approve an exception |
| Comparative value | Differences that affect the stated outcome, including cost to achieve, time, portability, ongoing obligations and displaced work |
| Availability and evidence | Whether the opportunity can actually be pursued and which critical claims can be verified before commitment |

Agree definitions and assessment evidence before examining candidate results. Retain profiles when alternatives solve different problems. Use a rank only when the criteria, direction, trade-offs and evidence quality make the comparison meaningful. A score with missing inputs should retain those gaps. It must not quietly convert unknown into zero or average away a mandatory failure. Source-company size, geography, funding, weights or cutoffs do not become general selection rules.

Keep opportunity attractiveness separate from competitive and commercial readiness. Customer spending or a large served market does not establish domain capability, usable product, delivery capacity, access to buyers or a repeatable route to revenue. Assess readiness using observed work: a concept, representative tested output, accepted deployment and repeated paid use provide different evidence. Define any local readiness bands from those observations and retain rival intensity, required capability and missing evidence beside the market estimate. An attractive opportunity can justify learning while remaining unready for a larger commitment.

When a rubric helps compare a large enough set, define local bands using observable evidence, mark the preferred direction, and explain any weights. Calibrate it on a few varied profiles before using it for the decision: independent assessors should be able to explain why the same evidence receives the same band. Resolve ambiguous criteria and correlated measures before calculating totals. Preserve the candidate profile beside the subtotal and rank. Small differences with weak evidence should not imply a precise ordering.

For example, two synthetic candidates that already clear mandatory fit receive local 1–3 scores for implementation speed, service fit and portability. A's profile is `(3, 2, 2)`, total seven; D's is `(2, 3, 1)`, total six. Equal weights rank A first. If service fit receives twice the weight because ongoing service is the binding constraint, both total nine. That tie changes the next evidence request rather than establishing a winner. Keep the original equal-weight result and explain the changed assumption. Date and version a later criteria change, record who approved its reason, and rescore every affected candidate under the new rubric. Do not overwrite the old ranking or change weights only for the favored option. These invented scores are a demonstration, not a recommended scale.

## Assemble a traceable, available universe

Define the universe by capability, customer/use case, relationship type, geography where relevant and search date. Combine credible databases, product evidence, specialist sources, customer or partner knowledge and relationships as appropriate. Record the query or inclusion rule and the coverage gap for each source. Deduplicate entities and related businesses before counting results.

Keep stages and exclusion reasons: identified → relevant → mandatory fit assessed → available to engage → critical evidence obtainable → ready for deeper evaluation. Those stages need not be a new tool or a fixed funnel. A small search can fit in one table. Preserve how each candidate entered, why it left, who assessed it and which evidence changed the status.

Availability is a fact to test. The relevant capability might be inseparable from unwanted assets, the other party may have no interest in the proposed relationship, required rights may be unavailable, or timing and cost may exceed the constraint. A strategically attractive but unavailable candidate belongs in the record with that limit. If no candidate clears the requirements, revisit the route, scope, deadline or internal alternative instead of promoting the highest remaining score.

## Gate commitments by evidence availability

For each critical claim, distinguish directly examined evidence, a party's representation, inference and missing evidence. Record scope, date, population/version and contradictory findings. Check whether a product demonstration, operating record, customer reference or other relevant direct test is available. A claim can be plausible and still unverified.

Use evidence gates proportional to consequence. A reversible discovery step can proceed with visible unknowns and a cost bound. A material dependency requires enough evidence for its decision owner to accept or constrain the exposure. If the necessary evidence cannot be obtained, choose a smaller test, a conditional commitment, a different route or no commitment. Specialist owners determine acceptable protections and controls. An operator must not treat an untested assertion as resolved because a document exists.

### Plan interviews around the disputed question

Choose a source because of what that person has observed, rather than the prestige of an employer or title. Match each question to a source type and its limits before scheduling. A prospective customer's stated interest, an operator's firsthand workload and a founder's forecast answer different questions.

| Synthetic disputed question | Source and agenda | Record and follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| Does the required output solve the user's problem? | Recent users and unsuccessful adopters: walk through their last task, current workaround, failure and acceptance example | Interview lead records selection and context. Named note owner separates shown behavior from reported preference. Test the contradictory cases. |
| Can implementation fit the capacity limit? | Implementer who performed a comparable deployment: reconstruct access setup, integration, exceptions, recovery and elapsed work | Technical owner reconciles firsthand work with the provider's estimate and asks both parties about the unexplained difference. |
| Will the proposed service exchange work? | Provider commercial owner and customer budget/operating owner: contributions, conditions, ongoing work and the reason each would continue | Commercial owner records proposed versus accepted obligations and returns unresolved conflicts to the appropriate decision owner. |
| Does the surrounding market support the route? | Customers explain buying criteria. Competitors or their product evidence inform positioning. Relevant experts inform the landscape. Suppliers and channel participants explain access, cost and constraints. End users demonstrate unmet task needs | Record each source's firsthand scope and incentives. Commercial claims and competitor inference require independent checks where they change the choice. |

Send an agenda tailored to the question and the person's scope. Assign an interviewer and note owner, even if one person performs both roles. Preserve role, date, firsthand versus secondhand knowledge, examples, uncertainty and possible incentives in the notes. Follow an inconsistency back to the source with a specific competing explanation and evidence request. Do not average conflicting testimony into consensus. Use the result to change a claim, test or commitment in the diligence record.

## Turn the value hypothesis into a diligence record

Carry one chain through the existing decision system: value hypothesis → test → finding and limitation → implication for choice or terms → implementation and value obligation. Distinguish the central coordinator from the specialist who owns a test and the business owner who must realize value. Findings can span product, customers, technology, people, operating requirements, economics and obligations, but the value thesis determines which questions matter most.

Each populated test record should contain:

1. The claim that makes this option valuable, its causal mechanism, strongest competing explanation and condition that would reverse the proposed commitment.
2. The evidence required, test or reconciliation, accountable expert, result and confidence limit. Preserve exceptions and unresolved contradictions.
3. The decision consequence: reject, narrow, price differently, change rights or conditions, stage the commitment, or accept explicitly. State who decides.
4. The operating consequence: owner, acceptance evidence, cost and scarce capacity to achieve value, transition dependencies and what remains after launch.

A generic request for financial, customer and technical documents does not complete this record. Neither does a list of risks without their effect on value or commitment. Quantify exposure where supported and show a range or a missing value where it is not. Route specialist legal, financial, technical and control conclusions to their authorized owners while performing the synthesis and calculations needed for the operating decision.

### Populated synthetic records

The following case and every quantity are invented practice inputs. A software business needs a reporting capability usable in eight weeks. The working limit is four internal engineer-weeks. A customer-facing production deployment, export rights and a named service owner are mandatory. The existing manual process remains the fallback.

| Candidate profile | Observed fit and availability | Screening decision |
|---|---|---|
| A: supported external service | Tested production output fits the narrow use case. Export conditions are documented. Direct evidence of an eight-week implementation is missing | Keep for a bounded feasibility test. Do not rank as ready. |
| B: broad platform | Demonstration covers more features, but the required export right is unavailable under the offered arrangement | Exclude this arrangement on mandatory fit. Breadth does not offset the failed right. |
| C: capability within a larger business | Technical fit is reported by its owner, who will only discuss a broader relationship with additional commitments | Keep as a distinct conditional route, not an available equivalent to A. |

The proposed value thesis for A is earlier usable reporting within the capacity limit. Its attraction is speed. The strongest opposing case is that integration and ongoing service consume more internal capacity than improving the manual process.

| Hypothesis and test | Finding and limit | Changed choice and implementation obligation |
|---|---|---|
| A reduces implementation below four engineer-weeks. Technical owner builds a representative connector and includes access setup, reconciliation and recovery. | Connector work is three weeks, but access and recovery add two: five weeks total. The four-week condition fails. The test does not establish performance for other use cases. | Do not approve the original scope. Evaluate a narrower scope and compare its remaining customer value with the manual alternative. |
| Narrower scope removes enough work without losing the required outcome. Product owner checks the acceptance examples with the affected users; Engineering re-estimates the narrowed work. | Acceptance examples still cover the chosen reporting outcome. The new estimate is 3.5 engineer-weeks, conditional on the provider supplying a verified export format before work starts. This remains an estimate. | Sponsor authorizes only the narrow implementation within the existing limit, subject to that evidence. Provider commitment and export test become an explicit pre-start condition. |
| Service value survives handoff. Operating owner rehearses a failed delivery and reconciliation with the provider. | Escalation works, but no internal owner is assigned to resolve mismatched records. | Assign that owner and reserve its operating time before release. Track mismatch workload after launch against the value case. |

The finding changes the chosen scope, the condition for starting, the internal ownership and the acceptance record. It does not validate the original broad proposal. If the provider cannot supply the evidence, the manual fallback remains available and the sponsor reopens the choice.

For an acquisition or other major commitment, extend the same chain to what actually transfers, retained people/capabilities, liabilities, required approvals, cost to achieve and the intended operating end state. Keep strategic value distinct from financial ownership or a paper increase in value. Use the [strategy skill](../../strategy/SKILL.md) for company portfolio and parent-value choices, and [cross-functional execution](../../cross-functional-execution/SKILL.md) for dependencies and transition ownership.

## Preserve the decision through implementation

Deliver the search boundary, candidate dispositions, selected profile, populated critical tests, unresolved evidence, changed choice/conditions and accountable operating obligations. Link to existing records rather than creating a second master tracker. Record the sponsor's decision and the next authorized commitment.

After implementation, compare realized capability, timing, cost and customer value with the original hypothesis. Separate finding the right opportunity from executing it well. An unsuccessful outcome should change the relevant selection assumption, evidence test or operating obligation. Use [discovery](discovery.md) for the customer problem and [the investment-to-release case](investment-to-release-case.md) when the selected option must become product requirements and release evidence.
