# Executive support

Help leaders make and communicate better decisions, remove avoidable demands on their attention, and turn their intent into owned work. The role is defined by its mandate, access, and authority. A Chief of Staff title does not grant executive decision rights.

## Diagnose the seat before designing support

Establish the company and served-team size separately, stage, public status, operating maturity, current priorities, and functional owners. Read existing mandates, recent decisions, leadership materials, and the user's own work before proposing replacements. Ask only for missing facts that change the assignment.

Distinguish four gaps: executive attention, decision quality, translation between leaders and teams, and an unowned operating responsibility. Each can require a different remedy. An assistant, clearer delegation, better information, or a functional hire may solve the problem more directly than a broader CoS remit.

Produce a short mandate when scope is unclear: outcomes served, work owned, work supported, authority limits, access, escalation route, principal relationship, and a review or handoff condition. Explain the strongest case against adding the role or process before recommending it. Separate advisory access from authority to require work, spend, hire, or commit the company.

## Design the leader relationship

Agree topic by topic whether the operator decides within bounds, recommends for a leader's decision, coordinates an owner, or only prepares information. Learn the principal's judgment through real past decisions: inputs considered, trade-offs, exceptions, and what would have changed the call. Test that understanding on a current case rather than inferring preferences from personality labels.

Make disagreement safe and useful. Clarify whether people differ on facts, assumptions, priorities, or authority. Preserve dissent and recommend a resolution. Private feedback can protect the working relationship, but must not conceal a material error from the forum responsible for it. Once a decision is made, communicate the actual decision and remaining uncertainty without manufacturing consensus.

Avoid becoming an alternative chain of command. Leaders should know when the operator speaks for the principal, exercises explicit delegation, or offers their own analysis. If the principal repeatedly bypasses agreed owners, surface the authority problem rather than adding another tracker.

## Return attention and build transferable capacity

Use a time and work inventory to identify recurring decisions that reach the leader only because delegation, information, or ownership is missing. Compare the hours returned, judgment required, disruption, and continuing maintenance of each proposed intervention. Protect preparation and decision time according to the leader's actual priorities, not a universal calendar percentage.

Execute the [attention and decision case](references/worked-executive-support.md) before claiming a capacity benefit. The case measures leader time and total organizational effort separately. It delegates ownership to functions and follows the recommendation through a memo, facilitated decision and observed handoff. The same reference executes a governance contribution with conflicting denominators and a prior-commitment bridge.

Choose a small amount of work that can be completed and used by others. Observe enough real execution to understand why the current method exists. Urgent stabilization may need action immediately, while a leadership succession may need extended listening. Do not impose a fixed period of inaction.

For temporary functional ownership, name the future owner and transfer condition. A handoff includes working material, unresolved dependencies, access, acceptance by the receiving owner, and evidence they can run it. Keep the operator out of routine work after transfer unless a specific dependency still requires participation. Judge success by better decisions, fewer avoidable escalations, and useful capacity returned, not document or meeting counts.

## Choose the artifact for the job

Read [communication and decision materials](references/communication-and-decisions.md) for memos, leadership updates, all-hands, process documents, or drafts in the author's voice. Read [facilitation and governance support](references/facilitation-and-governance.md) for preparation, meetings, assigned board support, or follow-through. Neither reference authorizes sending, publishing, calendar changes, or new commitments.

Use the company's existing artifact and record owner where it works. Separate a proposed recommendation, an approved decision, and an execution commitment. Give missing evidence a visible status and an owner to resolve it. Check that a reader without the conversation can identify the decision, evidence, trade-off, and action.

## Adapt to context

| Context | What changes |
|---|---|
| Under 20, founder-held | Favor direct preparation, a short work list, and clear delegation. Hands-on functional work can be appropriate when explicitly assigned. Do not build a miniature executive bureaucracy. |
| 20–60, emerging | Make priorities and interfaces explicit where conversation stops reaching everyone. Keep temporary ownership and transfer conditions visible. A headcount band is a diagnostic prompt, not proof a new process is needed. |
| 61–300, repeatable | Develop functional owners and managers, reconcile competing commitments, and hand off recurring work. More executive access does not justify overriding leaders. |
| 301–5,000 or 5,001+, business unit or function | Work through existing leadership forums, Finance, People, Legal, Communications, and other staffed functions. Confirm which decisions belong locally and which require enterprise owners. |
| Public or scaled-governed | Support the assigned internal executive contribution. Follow the accountable governance owner's approval, access, and distribution arrangements. Investor Relations, corporate disclosure, legal records, and accounting remain with their owners. |

## Failure checks

Before delivery, test whether the proposed support duplicates an owner, hides disagreement, implies authority the operator lacks, or creates work only the operator can sustain. Reconcile material claims to current sources, dates, units, definitions, and prior communications. Choose slide count, advance-reading timing, executive time allocation, and meeting frequency for the actual decision and constraints.

## Decision checklist

Use the [shared baseline](../../GSTACK-CORE.md#before-giving-advice) to establish who is asking and which facts could change the answer.

1. Identify who recommends, who can withhold approval and who decides so the delegation has a boundary. [Bain RAPID](https://www.bain.com/insights/rapid-decision-making)
2. Write the disputed assumption and evidence needed so a pre-read advances the decision. [Atlassian project poster](https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/project-poster)
3. Choose advance reading or protected reading time according to preparation and the decision the room must make. [Application](references/facilitation-and-governance.md)
