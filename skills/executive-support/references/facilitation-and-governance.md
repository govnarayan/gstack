# Facilitation and governance support

## Prepare a useful room

Start with the outcome: decision, shared interpretation, design work, or relationship discussion. Status can be a legitimate purpose, but should not consume a decision forum by default. Identify the decision owner, required contributors, affected owners, available evidence, and deadline. Invite people for a contribution they can name, with enough context to participate.

An agenda gives each item an outcome, lead, allotted minutes, relevant prior decision, and preparation. Match advance reading to complexity and the forum's agreed rules. A late paper requires a choice by the responsible owner about a limited decision, postponement, or urgent exception. Do not create a universal delay rule that blocks necessary action.

For a 1:1, pull recent shared work, prior commitments in both directions, relevant decisions, and the person's current responsibilities. Separate observed working preferences from inference. Highlight the few open or awkward items that matter now. Do not turn stale notes into a personality diagnosis or assume a calendar event has been identified when access is missing.

## Facilitate disagreement toward a result

Separate evidence disputes, competing priorities, and authority conflicts. Reconcile facts with their owners. Ask each position what assumption it depends on and what would change it. Independent input before group discussion can reduce early anchoring, but unanimity alone is not proof of poor thinking and conflict need not be manufactured.

Make room for affected people and remote participants. Clarify whether consultation can change the decision or only implementation. Name real constraints without pretending an already-made decision is open. Keep unrelated issues with an owner and a return date. If new information changes the decision, let the decision owner adjust the agenda rather than hiding it to preserve timing.

Close by reading back decisions, unresolved questions, actions, owners, due dates, and what needs approval. A record should distinguish an option discussed from a decision made, and dissent from refusal to execute. Retain the original commitment date when changing a due date and explain the change. Use evidence of acceptance and completion rather than silence as proof of agreement.

Check whether the authorized owner can decide in the room or act on its output. Verify that the alternatives are feasible and the forum can resolve any missing evidence. If two participants disagree on risk tolerance after agreeing on the facts, another data presentation will not settle that choice. Put the trade-off to the owner. If authority is disputed, capture the unresolved boundary and obtain a ruling through the existing route rather than conducting a vote that cannot bind anyone.

## Assigned board and executive governance material

Confirm the operator's assignment, sponsoring executive, forum, agenda owner, and reviewers. Obtain the actual governing documents and current instructions through the accountable governance function when relevant. This skill does not determine legal notice, quorum, reserved matters, official resolution language, minutes requirements, or disclosure obligations.

Build the operating or strategy contribution around current state versus prior commitment, explanation of variance, revised assumptions, choices, recommendation, and the specific advice or approval needed. Carry prior requests forward with status and evidence. Use consistent metric definitions and footnote changes. Include business-model-specific measures only when useful to the question. Separate operating material from financial statements and formal legal records, which their owners prepare or approve.

Agree pack timing with the forum owner. Reconcile tables to approved sources, check dates and definitions, rehearse the hardest questions, and list missing answers. Pre-meeting conversations may surface concerns when the executive or chair assigns them, but do not imply a decision has been privately approved. Contacting directors, circulating papers, and sending investor updates require authorization from the accountable owner.

At a public company, support the internal executive's assigned material through Finance, Legal, the corporate secretary, Communications, and Investor Relations as applicable. Do not assume ownership of those functions or use a startup investor letter as a public disclosure template. Sensitive sequencing follows the company's accountable owners, not a generic internal-first rule.

## Signs the mechanism is failing

An agenda with no outcome, a repeated decision with no new evidence, green status masking a blocker, and action dates silently moving all warrant diagnosis. Check for unclear authority, missing evidence, overloaded owners, or a mismatched forum before adding meetings. A good record and an unowned action still leave the work unowned.

## Choose when people read

[Brad Feld](https://feld.com/archives/2009/08/the-best-board-meetings/) sends directors status material before the meeting so the board can spend its time on consequential issues. This works when directors prepare and the CEO tells them what help the business needs.

[Jeff Bezos](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/2017-letter-to-shareholders) describes Amazon executives reading a narrative together at the start. Use shared reading time when participants need the same evidence to reason together and advance preparation has been unreliable. Budget for reading and revision when choosing this format.

[Andrew Bosworth](https://boz.com/articles/reviews) distinguishes informing, discussing and deciding. If the agenda has no disputed issue or decision, circulate the update. After the meeting record what changed, who owns it, and which concerns remain open.
