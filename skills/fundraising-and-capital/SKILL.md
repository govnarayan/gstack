# Fundraising and capital

Start with the decision due and the person making it. Check which founder, officer or director has authority to start the process. An advisor, candidate or analyst may only have public evidence.

Read the conversation, company record, cash forecast, existing financing agreements and board decisions available to the requester. Research dated company announcements, product evidence and relevant partners’ investment histories.

State the company baseline in one paragraph. Identify the caller’s role, the decision owner, available cash, the milestone and what the evidence cannot establish. Ask only for missing inputs that could change the call.

Use this skill from startup financing through the final private rounds before a public offering. Where Corporate Finance or Investor Relations owns the process, deliver assumptions and analysis to that team. Counsel confirms obligations and closing authority.

## Raise now, wait or spend less

Date the next piece of proof an investor could inspect. Name the event, its cash cost, the person doing the work and the consequence of delay.

Build a monthly bank balance through proof, diligence and receipt of funds. Include collections timing, debt service and the minimum cash needed to operate. Test a delayed milestone and a failed raise.

Raise enough to cover the largest projected shortfall against that minimum, including fees. If existing cash covers the downside, compare faster execution against founder time and ownership sold.

Julia Enthoven faced that trade as one of two engineers at a self-funded video software company. Sending one founder out to pitch would halve engineering capacity. [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/blog/fundraising-conversation-with-myself/)

Unearth could fund customer discovery with angels while it moved from drone mapping to construction workflow software. The seed investor already knew the founder. Cold prospects offer weaker evidence of funding. [Madrona](https://www.madrona.com/when-to-raise/)

The alternative matters when testing requires heavy upfront spend. In a cooling 2016 market, Randy Komisar favored raising before a burn increase. Eric Feng worked backward from the next fundable state. [Interview](https://www.kleinerperkins.com/perspectives/all-your-questions-about-startup-fundraising-asked-and-answered/)

### A milestone that slips

A synthetic team has $900,000 cash and spends $100,000 net each month. Its minimum operating balance is $200,000. Customer renewal evidence arrives after month four.

With three months from evidence to cash, the balance reaches its minimum at closing. A two-month renewal delay leaves a $200,000 shortfall before fees.

Start investor preparation now. Waiting for the renewal becomes affordable if monthly net spending falls to $77,777 or less over those nine months.

Check that the spending cut preserves the work needed for renewal. Otherwise the cheaper plan also moves the evidence date.

Kapwing later accepted a lead investor after organic adoption and inbound interest changed the trade. Revisit the financing decision when comparable evidence arrives. [Founder follow-up](https://www.kapwing.com/blog/why-we-decided-to-raise-money-for-our-startup/)

## Compare cash, ownership and control

| Choice | What to calculate | When the choice changes |
|---|---|---|
| Post-money SAFE | This agreement exchanges cash for future equity. At a binding $10m post-money cap, $1m buys roughly 10% before new round money. | Model every outstanding agreement together. A lower conversion price can increase the share count. [YC guide](https://www.ycombinator.com/safe) |
| Priced preferred round | At $8m before financing, $2m of new cash buys 20% after financing, before other changes. | Rebuild ownership with conversions and the option pool. Compare sale proceeds at three exit values, voting rights and board seats. [Model documents](https://nvca.org/model-legal-documents/) |
| Venture debt | Schedule draws, fees, interest, repayment and any warrants, which give the lender rights to buy shares. | Reject a repayment plan dependent on an unsigned round. Model contractual restrictions on using cash with the lender. [Lending guidance](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2025/bulletin-2025-45.html) |
| Extension | Calculate ownership using the actual security and price. Identify whether the existing round permits another closing. | Use when a specific unfinished milestone still changes the next financing. Adcendo added a new investor and board member during its extension. [Issuer announcement](https://adcendo.com/wp-content/uploads/Adcendo-Series-A-Extension-PR.pdf) |
| Secondary sale | The buyer pays an existing holder. Selling existing shares alone adds zero company cash and issues zero shares. | Split a mixed offer into company funding and seller liquidity before calculating runway. Ask counsel about transfer restrictions. [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/capital-raising-building-blocks/private-secondary-markets) |

For a priced round, give each closing condition an owner and a due date. Have counsel identify which document grants each negotiated right. The [NVCA stock purchase agreement](https://nvca.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/NVCA-Model-SPA-Fall-2026.docx) distinguishes closing conditions from rights documented elsewhere.

A synthetic offer combines $2m primary at an $8m pre-money valuation and $1m secondary at the same price. The buyer owns 30% afterward. Only $2m reaches the business.

Compare that offer with $3m entirely primary at the same valuation. The buyer owns 27.27%, and the company has $1m more cash before fees.

A $1m extension at $4 per share creates 250,000 shares. With 2m existing shares and no conversions or pool change, the new shares represent 11.11%.

A $1m loan with warrants to purchase 25,000 shares at $4 creates a different outcome. Exercise against 2m existing shares leaves the lender with 1.23% before other issuances.

Keep the loan repayment and the exercise payment separate. Ask whether the warrant specifies a share count or coverage against the loan amount. [HSBC guide](https://www.hsbcinnovationbanking.com/-/media/hinv/pdf/venture-capital-term-sheet-guide-2025/hsbc-ivb-vc-term-sheet-guide-2025.pdf)

## Build the case and run the process

1. Write the disputed claim beside the evidence needed to believe it. At pre-seed, a working demonstration can establish feasibility while leaving demand unresolved. [Graham](https://paulgraham.com/convince.html)
2. At seed, identify who returned or paid without unusual help. At expansion, show acquisition cost, repeat purchases, gross margin and cash by customer group. [Kleiner Perkins](https://www.kleinerperkins.com/perspectives/all-your-questions-about-startup-fundraising-asked-and-answered/)
3. Match the proof to the business. A subscription renewal tests continued demand. A clinical development milestone tests a different risk. State what remains after the funded work. [Adcendo](https://adcendo.com/wp-content/uploads/Adcendo-Series-A-Extension-PR.pdf)
4. Record partner, mandate, check size, ability to lead, references, decision meeting and next action. Separate investors willing to act independently from those waiting for a lead. [Fundraising process](https://paulgraham.com/fr.html)
5. Time conversations around actual partner decisions. Test the story with credible prospects before spending the strongest introductions. Keep investor interest, written terms, signed documents and cleared cash distinct. [Graham](https://paulgraham.com/fr.html)
   Reserve an allocation when the investor confirms the amount and terms in writing. If both parties agree to a range, record its lower end. Keep a promise contingent on finding a lead outside committed capital. [YC handshake protocol](https://www.ycombinator.com/handshake).
6. Open the [data room](templates/data-room-checklist.md) in stages. Assign an owner to each exception. Compare offers after financing costs, preference payouts, control, closing conditions and investor references.
7. Ask counsel to confirm approvals, signed documents, filings and closing conditions. Reconcile cleared funds to the signed share schedule. Update the cash forecast only for money the company received.

## After closing

Give investors a short update showing milestone against plan, cash, material misses and one specific introduction or request. Assign an owner and due date to each promised follow-up.

Reserve board time for decisions. For each, name the alternative, downside, proposed resolution and evidence that would reverse the call. Record the vote or delegated action and revisit it next meeting.

## Connections

Finance owns the cash forecast. Closing changes the receipt date and net primary proceeds. A missed milestone changes the downside balance and the date directors must consider spending cuts.

Planning owns the funded hiring schedule. Release contingent positions only after the recorded financing condition is met. Log any unmapped handoff as a proposed connection with its owner and changed record.
