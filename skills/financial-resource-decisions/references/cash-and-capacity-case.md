# Execute a resource choice through capacity, economics, and cash

Use this reference when a hire, contractor, project, or capacity commitment must fit both the work and the cash path. The procedure and case are original synthesis. All amounts, evidence, reserves, scenarios, and results are synthetic. They are not external benchmarks or company policy.

## Assemble the minimum linked model

Before calculating, give every input a source period, unit, owner and evidence status. Use the relevant actuals, demand/work records, employment/vendor terms, collections schedule, and approved service obligations. Missing data remains missing. The case's made-up values must never fill a live model.

Connect these schedules rather than building independent attractive totals:

1. Work: opening backlog + new demand − completed − canceled = closing backlog. Completion cannot exceed both eligible work and feasible capacity. Do not bank unused labor from an earlier month.
2. Capacity: paid time − leave, fixed duties and ongoing service work = available delivery time, adjusted for the actual skill and ramp. Model the peak and skill constraint when a monthly total can conceal failure.
3. Economics: completed/accepted units × contractual price, less incremental direct cost, setup, and resource cost. Separate recognized revenue, invoices, receipts, allocated cost and cash.
4. Cash: opening available cash + receipts − payments + committed financing = closing available cash. Build receivables, payables, inventory or prepayment schedules where timing is material.
5. Decision: compare common service conditions and horizon, cash low point, obligations beyond the horizon, displaced work, downside, and the input that reverses the choice.

Use [incremental cash flow/NPV](../../../frameworks/references/economics-investment.md#npv), [sensitivity](../../../frameworks/references/economics-investment.md#sensitivity), or [staged options](../../../frameworks/references/economics-investment.md#real-options) for those specific questions. The [strategy skill](../../strategy/SKILL.md) handles a wider build/buy/partner strategic choice. This reference supplies affordability and work feasibility, not a competing strategy framework.

## Continuous case: hire, contract, or delay demand

A US Series A technology company has 42 employees and $900k of available opening cash. A delegated S&O operator prepares the analysis. The founder owns the choice. Finance validates cash and economic definitions, the delivery owner validates work and skill assumptions, and People/procurement validate applicable terms. The six-month decision is how to serve a temporary installation demand increase while preserving existing service. All dollar figures below are in thousands.

### Establish feasible work first

Three specialists each have 16 usable days/month after leave and fixed duties: 48. Ongoing service uses 18, leaving 30 installation days. Each installation requires three specialist days, so existing capacity is ten installations/month. The delivery owner confirms peak scheduling and skill coverage for these inputs. The 18-day service forecast is checked against obligations rather than treated as a universal staffing floor.

| Month | M1 | M2 | M3 | M4 | M5 | M6 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| New eligible demand | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 | 12 | 12 |
| Existing capacity | 10 | 10 | 10 | 10 | 10 | 10 |
| Completed with current staff | 6 | 8 | 10 | 10 | 10 | 10 |
| Closing backlog | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 |

Total demand is 60 and current completion is 54. The unfilled six can move to M7 under the case's customer terms. No contractual service obligation is breached. The forecast is not a guaranteed sale. The sponsor decides whether delay is acceptable and confirms the separate customer consequence. Earlier spare capacity cannot perform installations before customers and sites are ready.

Compare three alternatives:

| Option | Capacity and timing | Incremental resource cost |
|---|---|---|
| Delay six installations | No added capacity. Six remain for M7 | No added resource cost in M1–M6. Benefit and direct costs shift later, with possible customer loss explicitly uncertain |
| Hire one specialist | Starts M2, adds 1/2/4/4/4 installations of capacity in M2–M6 after ramp | Recruiting $15 in M1 plus $15/month fully loaded cash compensation in M2–M6: $90. Employment continues after M6 unless the actual terms say otherwise. |
| Bounded contractor | Two additional installations/month in M4–M6 | Setup $12 in M3 plus $4 per incremental installation: $36. Vendor terms cap scope at six with no minimum beyond M6. |

Both expansion options can serve all 60 installations. The hire's unused capacity is a real option for other work only if that work is identified and feasible. It is not automatic revenue. Contracting may lose internal knowledge and continuity. The delivery owner must validate equivalent quality and service, and existing staff must have capacity for setup and oversight. In this case setup is purchased in the quoted fee and oversight is included in the usable-time estimate. If either is false, rerun capacity and economics.

### Model the incremental economics and collections

Each of the six accelerated installations earns $12 on acceptance, incurs $4 of direct product/service cash cost on completion, and is paid the following month. Two additional installations finish in each of M4–M6. Thus incremental revenue is $72, direct cost $24, and receipts within the horizon are $48. The last $24 sits in closing receivables and is expected in M7. None of that receivable is cash at M6.

| Six-month effect versus delaying | Hire | Contractor |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue accelerated into horizon | 72 | 72 |
| Direct product/service cost | (24) | (24) |
| Incremental resource cost | (90) | (36) |
| Incremental economic result | **(42)** | **12** |
| Cash receipts within horizon | 48 | 48 |
| Cash payments within horizon | (114) | (60) |
| Net cash effect | **(66)** | **(12)** |
| Closing incremental receivables | 24 | 24 |

The $24 difference between economic result and cash effect reconciles to receivables. The case assumes acceptance revenue equals invoicing. Expense and pay the listed direct and resource costs as modeled. The case assumes no tax difference, depreciation or other working capital. Finance must replace those assumptions where actual policy differs. It is not a full three-statement model.

The $72 is accelerated rather than necessarily lifetime incremental revenue. A horizon comparison can favor acceleration even when a long-horizon comparison does not. Show M7 receipts, delivery timing, customer loss evidence and continuing employment costs before claiming a durable return. At M7 the hire has another $15/month commitment under this assumption. The contractor has none for this bounded scope. Extending the horizon without extending costs would falsely improve the hire.

### Reconcile to the cash envelope

The starting forecast includes the 54 existing-staff installations and other operations. Its receipts below already exclude the six accelerated installations. Operating payments include existing payroll and direct cost of those 54. Do not add them again. An existing $120 commitment is due M2. No new financing is assumed. The founder and Finance have set a $300 operating reserve for this synthetic decision.

| Cash schedule | M1 | M2 | M3 | M4 | M5 | M6 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Base receipts | 180 | 180 | 210 | 240 | 240 | 270 |
| Base operating payments | (240) | (240) | (260) | (260) | (260) | (260) |
| Existing commitment | 0 | (120) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Base closing cash | 840 | 660 | 610 | 590 | 570 | 580 |
| Hire closing cash | 825 | 630 | 565 | 522 | 503 | 514 |
| Contractor closing cash | 840 | 660 | 598 | 562 | 550 | 568 |

For the hire, M4 incremental movement is −15 payroll −8 direct cost = −23. For M5/M6 it is +24 receipts −15 payroll −8 direct cost = +1 each month. For the contractor, M3 movement is −12 setup. M4 is −8 contractor −8 direct = −16. M5/M6 are +24 −8 −8 = +8 each. Summing all movements reproduces the table. The hire low point is $503 at M5. The contractor low point is $550 at M5. Both exceed the assumed reserve in this base case, but affordability alone does not justify either.

### Solve the decision boundary and downside

Let `n` be installations accelerated into the same horizon, retaining the same price/direct cost and comparable delivery conditions. Hire economics are `8n − 90`. Contractor economics are `8n − 4n − 12 = 4n − 12`. The hire overtakes the contractor when `8n − 90 > 4n − 12`, or `n > 19.5`: at least 20 whole installations. The hire needs at least 12 installations to produce a nonnegative six-month economic result, but the contractor can still be better. A positive result is not the same as best use of cash.

These are conditional breakpoints. The case has only six supported incremental installations, and the quoted contractor capacity is six. Do not extrapolate its price or availability to 20 without a new quote. Do not extrapolate the hire's ramp without skill and demand evidence. The breakpoint tells the team what evidence to collect, not what volume it should invent.

A coherent downside delays $180 of base M2 receipts until M4, while all commitments remain. Subtract $180 from each base-case closing balance for M2 and M3, then restore it at M4. Contractor cash falls to $480 at M2 and $418 at M3, still above the $300 reserve. Hire cash falls to $450 and $385. Neither can absorb any arbitrary delay: for a delay lasting through M3, contractor headroom at its trough is $598 − $300 = $298. Hire headroom is $565 − $300 = $265. A delayed amount above those values breaches the reserve at M3 under these inputs. Add correlated demand loss, cancelation terms and service shocks where supported. A collection delay alone is not a full downside distribution.

### Completed decision record

Recommend the bounded contractor if the founder values serving the six installations now and the owner validates equivalent service. Its six-month economic result is +$12 and cash cost is $12, versus −$42 and $66 for hiring. The strongest objection is that delay may preserve $36 of resource spend with no lifetime revenue loss. If evidence confirms customers will wait and no strategic/service consequence requires acceleration, delay may be preferable. The recommendation therefore depends on the value of earlier service and customer-loss evidence, not an invented churn claim.

Authorize only the scoped arrangement through the existing owner. Before signing, confirm the quote, quality, oversight, customer terms, and base collections. Revisit a hire when supported continuing work, skill requirements and a longer cash horizon justify it. Trigger a cash refresh when collections slip or the reserve headroom shrinks below the time needed to act. The operator prepares and reconciles this record. It is not itself spending or hiring approval.

## Change the boundary with company scope

In a small startup, a delegated operator may construct this model from cash, work records and commitments, with specialist validation. In a staffed public-company unit, use Finance's forecast and definitions, not an alternative generalist ledger. Replace company runway with the unit's authorized envelope when that is the actual constraint. Distinguish corporate cash, a unit allocation, an internal charge, and incremental company spend.

For example, a unit charged $90 for the hire may show a local saving from contracting, while company spend does not fall if the employee remains elsewhere. Show released capacity and its next use separately. For capital-intensive hardware, add inventory/deposits, installation, warranty and salvage timing. For AI infrastructure, replace nominal seats or devices with feasible usable workload at required quality and include fixed capacity commitments. Technical feasibility remains the technical owner's input.

## Behavioral and model checks

Recompute completion from demand and capacity before trusting revenue. Trace every incremental payment into cash exactly once. Reconcile receivables to invoices less receipts and economic-to-cash differences to timing/noncash items. Exercise zero demand, late start, no collection, higher service load, and scenario changes. Report undefined results for zero denominators. A favorable NPV, a balanced spreadsheet, or a cheap vendor cannot override an infeasible workload, an unapproved promise, or an unsupported input.

## Test how the bank balance improves

[Peter Reinhardt](https://rein.pk/startup-finance-for-founders-part-ii-strategic-finance) describes annual prepayment changing Segment's cash path with the same contract economics. Run the same customer contracts twice, once with monthly collections and once with annual collections. Put the later delivery costs and renewal exposure into both versions.

Segment declined debt when new revenue had little predictable history. Test repayment against a slower collections case before counting borrowing capacity as runway.

[Buffer's investor buyout](https://buffer.com/resources/buying-out-investors/) used available cash to provide shareholder liquidity. Classify the cash by recipient. In a secondary sale between shareholders the company receives no operating funds. A company-funded buyback uses cash and competes with the operating reserve.

## Can the durable business pay for the fixed team?

[Emilie Choi on The Peel](https://www.thespl.it/p/the-coo-playbook-with-emilie-choi) credits a director with asking whether subscription and service revenue could cover operating expenses across trading cycles. Treat that as an objective. Revenue mix alone cannot establish coverage.

In a synthetic platform, $12 million of repeat revenue leaves $8 million after delivery costs. A $10 million fixed operating base still has a $2 million gap before transaction profits. Stress the repeat revenue too. Examine which components fall alongside transaction volume or interest rates. Then show the cash trough after payment timing and committed spend.
