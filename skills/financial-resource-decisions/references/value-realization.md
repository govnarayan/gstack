# From a claimed benefit to realized value

Use this procedure when a resource decision depends on savings, added contribution, faster delivery or released capacity. It applies to internal programs, shared services and capability changes. It does not supply transaction valuation, accounting policy or compensation authority.

This is original operating guidance. Produce a benefit register, a reconciliation from the original case to the current forecast, and an evidence-backed view of what has occurred. A list of completed initiatives is not a realized-value calculation.

## Lock the comparison before counting value

State the decision, affected entities and population, horizon, currency, units, cash or contribution basis, and actual owner. Preserve the original approved case and its source cutoff. Build the counterfactual from what would happen without this action, including already-approved improvements and unavoidable costs. Record uncertainty rather than quietly moving the baseline to improve the result.

Keep the approved target, original estimate, current forecast and observed result distinct. Reconcile definitions before comparing them. An annualized run rate is neither cumulative benefit received nor cash collected. A forecast after implementation remains a forecast until the relevant operating and economic evidence exists.

## Classify the change, then calculate the difference

| Claim | Counting rule | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Created benefit | Count the supported difference from the no-action outcome in each period | A specific changed driver, affected population, implementation and resulting outcome |
| Accelerated benefit | Count only the timing difference until the counterfactual catches up, plus any supported later differences | Original timing, revised timing, actual usable date and extra cost of acceleration |
| Already-planned improvement | Keep it in the baseline. Do not claim the whole improvement again. | Dated plan, existing commitment, owner and expected timing |
| Released capacity | Report usable hours or throughput separately from cash savings | Work removed, quality/service preserved, timing and credible redeployment or avoided demand |
| Avoided or reduced cost | Distinguish a prevented future outlay from a reduction in an existing outlay | Contract, staffing or spending decision establishing what would otherwise be paid and when |

Classify at the level where the comparison is meaningful. One initiative can contain created, accelerated and already-planned components. A label does not establish causation. Compare rival explanations such as price, mix, demand, unrelated cost actions or changed measurement. For a consequential causal claim, use evidence appropriate to the decision rather than treating a balanced bridge as proof.

Model the action and counterfactual by period. Include ramp, adoption, attrition, collection/payment timing and service constraints where they matter. Deduct implementation, migration, retention, ongoing operating and other incremental costs that the action requires. Preserve negative effects on other products, teams or services. Have Finance and functional owners validate their respective inputs.

## Keep a line-level register

Each material line needs the following information. Use existing planning and Finance records where possible rather than creating a competing master model.

| Field | What the record must allow a reviewer to do |
|---|---|
| Identity and boundary | Find the initiative/benefit ID, population, period, economic basis and related claims |
| Mechanism and comparator | Reproduce the driver change and what would happen without this action |
| Amount and timing | Recompute gross benefit, one-time cost, ongoing cost and net effect by period |
| Versions | Compare original case, current forecast and observed result without overwriting history |
| Evidence and state | Distinguish a hypothesis, accepted implementation, supported forecast and observed outcome. Inspect source, cutoff, limitations and missing evidence. |
| Ownership | Identify the business benefit owner, implementation owner, validating Finance/functional owner, actual decision authority and next review |
| Dependencies and response | Find the capacity, prerequisite, overlap, risk, stop/revise condition and continuing operating home |

An implementation owner can finish work while the benefit owner still lacks adoption or collection evidence. Record both states. If two programs claim the same reduction, customer movement or freed hour, assign one counted amount or an explicit shared-attribution rule. Retain the relationship between claims so removing one does not make the other disappear unnoticed.

Eliminate internal transfers in a group view. Keep cash, contribution, accounting expense and capacity effects separate. Do not add a payroll reduction and the full value of redeployed hours for the same people and period. Cost avoidance is not a cash reduction against current actuals, but it can be incremental against a supported future-spend counterfactual.

## Reconcile changes without rescuing the case

Start from the original net estimate on a fixed horizon and basis. Record signed revisions for overlaps or errors removed, lost or infeasible benefit, timing changes, supported additions, cost changes and adverse operating effects. For each revision, retain the affected line, old assumption, new evidence, owner and date.

`current forecast net value = original net estimate + sum of signed revisions`

The same lost benefit cannot be subtracted again under timing or overlap. Recompute from the current line-level model and reconcile it to the bridge. Additions need independent evidence. An approval shortfall does not justify inventing replacement benefits. If the supported case no longer works, present the changed decision.

Show why a benefit was lost: failed adoption, unavailable capacity, a changed operating model, unsupported demand, retention failure, delay or mistaken attribution require different responses. Keep actual cost to achieve visible alongside run-rate benefit. A higher eventual run rate can still have worse near-term cash or value because it arrives late or costs more.

## Verify what occurred and decide again

For the observed period, compare actual activity and economics with the fixed counterfactual using matched definitions. Trace a consequential claim to underlying records and independently recompute it. Show which costs are paid, committed or forecast. Report incomplete observation, attribution limits and unresolved disagreements.

The output should distinguish implemented work, observed gross effect, observed incremental net effect and remaining forecast. Name who accepts the evidence and what result changes funding or scope. For handover, require an operating owner, stable service evidence and a later benefit check when outcomes lag delivery. Do not close the value record because the project team disbands.

## Compact completed register and bridge

All figures are synthetic assumptions in thousands of dollars over six months, with no discounting. The comparison uses avoidable outlays, not revenue or employee-time valuations. Assume the specified contracts establish the baseline and observed reductions, with no other price, volume or service change.

An existing plan would reduce a vendor outlay by 10 a month beginning in month four. A new program starts that same reduction in month two. Its original case mistakenly claimed 10 in every month from two through six. A second saving was estimated at 3 a month over those five months, but 1 a month overlaps the first claim.

| Line | Original case | Supported current forecast | Observed through month three |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Accelerated reduction | 50 | 20: only months two and three precede the baseline | 20 |
| Separate created reduction | 15 | 10: five months at 2 after removing overlap | 4: two months at 2 |
| One-time program cost | −12 | −14 | −14 |
| Ongoing program cost | −10 | −10: five months at 2 | −4: two months at 2 |
| Net | 43 | 6 | 6 |

The revision bridge is `43 − 30 timing error − 5 overlap − 2 extra one-time cost = 6`. It reconciles to `20 + 10 − 14 − 10 = 6`. The original plan's month-four onward reduction remains in the baseline. It is not a continuing incremental benefit of acceleration.

The benefit owner retains the baseline contract and paid-outlay comparison. The implementation owner confirms the month-two start. Finance validates cost records and the duplicate claim. This is a completed example of evidence assignments, not evidence from an actual company.

Do not expand on the strength of the original 43 claim. The remaining three months forecast 6 of unique created savings and 6 of ongoing costs, so they add no further net benefit. The decision owner must compare continuing with stopping, including any exit costs or service obligations. Past realized value does not justify new spending. Any extra avoidable cost makes this remaining financial case negative unless another supported benefit changes it. Across the entire six-month case, an additional cost of 6 would bring net value to zero, holding everything else fixed.

If only annualized savings or project completion were available, the observed-value column would remain unverified.
