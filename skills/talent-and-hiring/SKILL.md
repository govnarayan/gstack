# Talent and hiring

Start with the work someone must do. A hiring bar is the minimum evidence that someone can do that work under the conditions this team offers.

Read the role, current goals, examples of strong work, past hiring decisions and available performance evidence. Check public company materials for stage, business model and how teams describe their work.

Establish whether the requester is a hiring manager, candidate, advisor or sponsor. State what they can see and decide. Ask only what would change the recommendation.

## Choose the work before the person

Write the first outcome, the authority to deliver it and the help available. If the previous owner missed it, test whether competing goals or dependencies caused the miss.

Raylene Yung describes Stripe moving from generalist engineers to specialist recruiting as problems diverged. Stripe changed interview questions, recruiting and onboarding together. [Stripe](https://stripe.com/guides/atlas/scaling-eng)

Choose a generalist while the next assignment changes faster than the role can specialize. Switch when a recurring body of specialist work and a manager can sustain that role.

## Set the bar and run the interview

Start with a job analysis, pilot the exercise and train interviewers using sample answers. [OPM interview checklist](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/assessment-and-selection/structured-interviews/guide.pdf)

Close the decision with reference checks, a written selection reason and manager sign-off. [Sam Houston State checklist](https://www.shsu.edu/offices-departments/human-resources/documents/hiring-evaluation-checklist.pdf)

1. Write what good work looks like before opening applications. Give every must-have an observable result. Remove pedigree requirements that substitute for evidence. [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/hiring-process/)
2. Give each interviewer one question to resolve. Set scoring anchors and share the same instructions and available preparation time with candidates. [GitLab](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/hiring/conducting-a-gitlab-interview/)
3. For product, give a fictional roadmap conflict. Score how the candidate identifies affected users, tests a priority and explains what gets delayed. Provide equivalent information to everyone.
4. For engineering, review a small job-relevant change together. Wellfound scheduled its onsite before requesting a take-home so the candidate had evidence of reciprocal commitment. [Wellfound](https://wellfound.com/blog/our-hiring-process-was-broken-heres-how-we-fixed-it)
5. For sales, simulate a buyer conversation. Score discovery and the agreed next step. Test Labs used a cold call and individual pitch when hiring for business development. [Test Labs](https://testlabsuk.com/blog/how-we-hire-fast-without-breaking-delivery-cash-or-culture/)
6. Collect independent written evidence before discussion. Have the decision owner distinguish a failed requirement from an untested one. Reopen only the specific missing observation. [Interview guide](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/hiring/conducting-a-gitlab-interview/)
7. Check outcomes after ramp. Compare hires’ work with interview predictions. Include withdrawals by stage, declined offers and who never reached evaluation. [Buffer’s retired audition](https://buffer.com/resources/45-day-period/)

## Set pay and levels

Describe levels by work owned, scope of decisions and impact on others. Classify the job before looking at the candidate’s previous title or pay.

Compare the proposed offer with incumbents at the same level. Model cost across the group if the exception becomes policy. Show base, incentives and equity separately.

Buffer described an offer combining the wrong level and location band. The mistake survived because its formula had become hard to use. Have a second person reproduce the offer. [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/salary-system/)

Netflix links pay to an individual’s external market. That requires managers who can explain why the role and market changed. A common pay schedule favors consistency across comparable roles. [Netflix](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture)

For sales, test whether quota, territory and inherited accounts drive pay differences. For engineers and product managers, compare scope and decision ownership before assuming more reports justify higher pay.

## Coach, move, retain or exit

Review concrete episodes with the employee. Separate missed output from missing tools, blocked authority, ambiguous expectations or a changed job. Agree on the evidence that would show improvement.

Use a dated coaching plan when capability can improve within the business deadline. Name the manager’s support, the employee’s next opportunity to demonstrate progress and the review date. [GitLab talent assessment](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/people-group/talent-assessment/).

When the job changed, test a real internal opening against its requirements. When the gap persists despite support, review evidence and alternatives with People and counsel before an exit decision.

Netflix asks managers whether they would hire the person again. Its memo also calls for regular conversations and judgment across the whole record, including mistakes. [Culture memo](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture)

Use that question only after naming future work and reviewing evidence together. A single failed bet provides weak evidence when the company asked the employee to take a risk.

Ask someone at risk of leaving which work they want, what is blocking it and what would make staying worthwhile. Test whether changed scope, management, pay or workload addresses that cause.

For a product manager without decision authority, another title leaves the blockage intact. For an engineer seeking deeper technical work, adding reports changes the job in the wrong direction.

For a strong seller facing an unworkable territory, compare qualified demand and coverage before changing compensation. Assign the actual remedy an owner and follow up with the employee.

## Worked hiring decision

A synthetic 42-person subscription company interviews a product lead. Two interviewers like her presentations. Neither observed how she resolves a disagreement with engineering.

The team uses a fictional product conflict and gives all finalists the same customer and capacity evidence. It scores the trade-off and communication separately.

One candidate selects the urgent account request without checking the dependent release. Another proposes a smaller commitment and identifies the engineering owner whose agreement it requires.

The second meets the stated requirement. The decision record cites the observed dependency and sequencing. Revisit the signal after the hire’s first contested roadmap decision.

## Connections

A funded role changes the workforce plan through the approved level, cost, manager and start date. Finance owns the forecast update. The hiring manager owns evidence of ramp.

A role redesign can change decision authority. Record the new decision owner and effective date before judging the person against the revised job.

Where a retention issue reveals an unmapped dependency, propose a connections row naming the receiving owner and record.

- [Financial decisions](../financial-resource-decisions/SKILL.md). Send the headcount forecast with the start date and employer cost to the finance lead.
- [Business administration](../g-and-a-operations/SKILL.md). Send the employee roster with the location, date and entity to the people operations lead.
- [Cross-team delivery](../cross-functional-execution/SKILL.md). Send the decision register with its owner and effective date to the program sponsor.

## Test whether the work standard was ever taught

[Emilie Choi on The Peel](https://www.thespl.it/p/the-coo-playbook-with-emilie-choi) describes a senior hire from traditional finance who had never used Slack at work. Silence in a channel the team uses for decisions can have more than one explanation.

Show the hire one example of a useful written intervention. Assign a relevant decision, explain who needs the input and inspect what the person contributed. Score the judgment and its effect. Counting posts rewards volume and can penalize someone who contributes at the right moment.

## When several teams open the same role

If teams assess the same work, run a shared interview and let qualified candidates meet the hiring manager before choosing a team. Keep specialist roles on a separate track. Give one recruiter ownership of replies so candidates do not disappear between teams. [Jade Rubick on bundled hiring at New Relic](https://www.rubick.com/bundled-hiring/).
